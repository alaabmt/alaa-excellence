# Tamayuz 10X CPHQ Item-Writing Standard
Version 1.0 | 18 October 2026 | Status: Editorial standard; Mizan academic certification remains item-specific.

## Scope and provenance
This standard is calibrated to the 188 source-traceable historical mock-examination items imported into the regular Tamayuz 10X CPHQ bank, comprising 63 from Mock 2016, 62 from 2020 Form A, and 63 from 2020 Form B. These are historical **mock** materials, not verified official NAHQ examination questions. Their source wording is reference material; new items must be independently written and not paraphrases. The full original staging set contains 194 records; six were not imported into the standard bank. Historical answer keys are not automatically correct or independently certified.

## Observed source characteristics (database-derived, n=188)
- Stem length: mean 20.9 words; median 17; 25th percentile 12; 75th percentile 27; 137/188 (72.9%) at most 25 words; 13/188 (6.9%) over 40 words. Tokenization is whitespace-based; extracted chart/table text and line breaks may distort individual counts.
- All 188 have four stored answer choices.
- Stem vocabulary (overlapping categories): “which” 80; “following” 86; “should” 43; “most” 36; “best” 30; “first” 14; “next” 6; “not” 7; “except” 3. These are case-insensitive whole-word counts, not cognitive classifications.
- 48 stems contain at least one of the textual cues table/chart/graph/data/rate/percentage/%, a broad keyword proxy **not** a confirmed number of quantitative reasoning questions.
- Historical domain labels: leadership 48; performance/process improvement 49; data analytics/information management 43; patient safety 48. These historical labels do not map perfectly onto the current seven-domain CPHQ blueprint and must not be used as current blueprint quotas.
- Source answer positions: A=64, B=44, C=46, D=34. The source distribution is not balanced; do not imitate it or mechanically force an answer key.
- Two historical source explanations are absent in the larger archive; 15 imported items required explicit missing-rationale notices in the standard bank. Historical source explanations and keys require independent checking.
- These metrics are descriptive; neither psychometric difficulty nor discriminative validity can be inferred without response data. There has not yet been an independent manual classification of all 188 by cognitive level, distractor quality, or item defects.

## Mandatory authoring rules
R01. **Blueprint first.** Specify the current CPHQ domain, task/competency, and intended cognitive action before writing. Confirm current blueprint independently; do not infer it from historical categories.
R02. **Single decision.** One focused problem and one unambiguous lead-in. Avoid combining multiple independent tasks in one stem.
R03. **Concision with sufficiency.** Target 12–27 stem words for straightforward conceptual/role questions, reflecting the observed interquartile range; this is an editorial target, not a hard cap. Use more words when the clinical scenario, dataset, denominators, chronology, or constraints are essential. Never remove information required to distinguish options.
R04. **Realistic professional context.** Present healthcare quality professionals, multidisciplinary teams, leaders, safety systems, measurement, and improvement activities in realistic organizational settings. Avoid unnecessary narrative or artificial drama.
R05. **Four parallel alternatives.** Each option should answer the lead-in, be grammatical, mutually distinguishable, and comparable in specificity, length, and action level.
R06. **Plausible distractors.** Wrong options should reflect realistic misconceptions: wrong measure type; premature intervention; correct tool for a different phase; incorrect accountable role; incorrect interpretation; insufficient evaluation; or failure to address system factors. Never use unethical or absurd straw-man choices to make the answer obvious.
R07. **One defensible best answer.** Test the key against the strongest distractor; if another option could be correct under the stated facts, add the decisive context or rewrite. Do not rely on an unstated local policy or controversial guideline.
R08. **Vary the decision type.** Include recognition/application, role/accountability, selection of improvement method, interpretation of performance data, best action, sequencing/priority, and evaluation. Do not default every item to “What should be done FIRST?” The observed 188 use “first” in only 14 stems; this count is not a quota.
R09. **Use priority wording precisely.** “FIRST” requires a real sequence; “MOST appropriate” requires meaningful tradeoffs; “BEST” requires a clear criterion. Avoid superlatives when several answers are equally reasonable.
R10. **Avoid answer clues.** No grammatical mismatches, conspicuously longer correct answer, implausible distractors, absolute qualifiers without justification, or repeated distinctive stem words only in the key. Avoid negative leads (NOT/EXCEPT) unless unavoidable and clearly emphasized.
R11. **Data integrity.** For numeric/data questions, provide all necessary denominators, units, periods, and comparison context; verify computations independently. Charts and figures must be fully legible, accessible, and available in the delivery interface.
R12. **Source integrity.** Historical stems and keys are retained as traceable references, never presented as official NAHQ questions. New stems must be independently constructed and checked for close textual overlap with source materials and existing bank items.
R13. **Option-specific rationales.** Supply four independently meaningful explanations (A–D), including why the key is best and why each distractor is less appropriate. Do not repeat a generic paragraph under all four options. Never invent source-backed rationale where no evidence exists.
R14. **Contemporary accuracy.** Verify clinical, regulatory, accreditation, statistical, and quality-management claims against current reliable sources. Flag jurisdictional and dated requirements. A historical answer key is not sufficient evidence.
R15. **No certification by import.** Separate technical completeness, editorial readiness, subject-matter verification, psychometric review, and Mizan certification. Publication status is not proof of academic approval.

## Item archetypes
A. Short conceptual distinction (e.g., structure/process/outcome measures).
B. Organizational approach or leadership choice.
C. Team role and accountability.
D. Quality-improvement method or phase selection.
E. Data/measurement interpretation with complete data.
F. Patient-safety systems decision.
G. Sequencing/prioritization when the facts establish order.
H. Evaluation of intervention effectiveness.
Select archetypes according to current blueprint needs, not an invented percentage allocation. Track and periodically rebalance the mix using manually verified labels.

## Construction and adversarial review
1. Record competency, intended inference, reference evidence, and one-sentence correct-answer justification.
2. Draft the key first; write three credible competing choices at the same conceptual level.
3. Write the stem with only decision-relevant facts; ensure no answer is uniquely signaled.
4. Attempt to defend each distractor as correct; if two answers survive, revise or reject.
5. Confirm option independence, clinical and regulatory accuracy, and current blueprint mapping.
6. Produce option-specific explanations and a short learning takeaway.
7. Check textual similarity with all historical and original Tamayuz bank items.
8. Run structural validation and a separate subject-matter review; mark Mizan certified only after an actual documented review.
9. Publish through the existing standard question interface; verify next/submit/status, daily limits, and guest/registered behavior.
10. Preserve version, reviewer, decision, evidence citations, and correction history.

## Proposed Mizan editorial scoring rubric (policy proposal, not a source-derived statistic)
- Stem clarity and sufficiency: 15 points.
- Current CPHQ blueprint alignment: 20.
- Four plausible competitive alternatives: 20.
- Unambiguous evidence-supported best answer: 20.
- Appropriate cognitive task: 15.
- Accurate option-specific rationales: 10.
A score >=90 qualifies for *further academic review*, not automatic certification. Scores 75–89 require revision; below 75 requires substantial rewrite. Automatic fail regardless of score: incorrect key, two defensible keys, unsafe/outdated guidance, missing critical data or images, unsupported rationale, near-verbatim duplication, or unresolved blueprint mismatch.

## Required item record
item_id; version; original_or_imported; historical_reference_ids (optional); current_domain; current_task; archetype; cognitive_action; stem; four_choices; correct_index; four_rationales; takeaway; references_and_verification_dates; author; reviewer; originality_check; editorial_score; reviewer_decision; mizan_status; publication_status; change_log.

## Calibration and limitations
This v1.0 is a descriptive standard informed by **all 188** integrated source stems and their available metadata. The numerical patterns above were computed across the full set. It does **not** claim a validated item-response difficulty distribution, full human coding of cognitive complexity, or confirmation of source-key correctness. Next phase: manually code every item by archetype, cognitive demand, distractor defect, and evidence risk, with independent adjudication; use student response statistics when available. Revise the rulebook only after documented evidence, and keep versions in GitHub.

## Implementation gate
New question generation must follow R01–R15; the auditor should withhold publication for any automatic fail, log the exact defect, and preserve the historical source as reference. Do not equate historical-source similarity with academic correctness or with NAHQ endorsement.
