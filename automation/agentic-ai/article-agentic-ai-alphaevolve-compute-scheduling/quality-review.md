# Quality Review — AlphaEvolve Compute Scheduling

## 20–30 second beginner test

A beginner should be able to say:

1. Some data-center capacity becomes unusable when free CPU and memory are split across different machines.
2. AlphaEvolve did not simply suggest code; it generated executable scheduling rules, tested them in a simulator, and retained better candidates.
3. Google reports that the deployed rule recovered an average 0.7% of fleet compute that otherwise would have been stranded.
4. The absolute fleet size, recovered capacity, observation period, uncertainty, cost and energy effects were not published.
5. The key 10X decision is to build a trustworthy evaluator, staged testing and rollback before giving an agent deployment authority.

Result: **PASS for comprehension.**

## Beginner critique

- The opening shows a concrete machine-level mismatch before naming the technology.
- The first technical definition explains what makes the system agentic and evolutionary.
- Simulation, unseen test data and live fleet deployment are separated.
- The 0.7% figure shows its denominator and every unpublished component.
- The matrix table shows total 54, then 38/54, 14/54 and 2/54; no outcome category is hidden.
- The local 23% kernel improvement is separated from the 1% overall training-time effect.
- The CodeEvolve comparison is isolated in a side box with its 9-problem sample.
- The enterprise example is explicitly hypothetical and does not predict a 0.7% result.
- Evidence limits distinguish public mathematical verification artifacts from the closed agent and internal operational claim.
- The 10X section produces four institutional decisions rather than a generic call to adopt AI.
- English terms in the Arabic draft appear inside parentheses.

## Bilingual semantic parity

- Both editions open with the same resource-mismatch problem without mirroring sentence structure.
- Both define AlphaEvolve as an evolutionary coding agent whose candidates are executed and scored.
- Both separate historical simulation, unseen evaluation and fleet deployment.
- Both attach 0.7% to fleet-wide compute and state that the absolute numerator, fleet size, time window, variation, cost and energy effect are absent.
- Both show the complete matrix breakdown: 38/54 matched, 14/54 surpassed and 2/54 fell behind; the reconstructed shares are 70.4%, 25.9% and 3.7%.
- Both explain 48 versus 49 scalar multiplications as a specific mathematical operation count, not an application-speed claim.
- Both isolate CodeEvolve as a separate 9-problem sample: 5/9 versus 4/9.
- Both distinguish the 23% local kernel speedup from the 1% whole-training effect and state that the input-shape count is undisclosed.
- Both label the 500-unit and 3.5-unit enterprise calculation as hypothetical.
- Sources, evidence limits, glossary concepts and 10X decisions match semantically.
- The English edition reads as native editorial English rather than translated Arabic.

Result: **PASS for bilingual parity.**
