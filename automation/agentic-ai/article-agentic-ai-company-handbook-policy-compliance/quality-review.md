## Company Insider Critique

Simulated perspective: enterprise compliance and internal-controls leader. This is an analytical review, not a statement from any named company.

### Main challenges

1. **Authority hierarchy was oversimplified.** A live organization may have valid amendments, emergency authorities and policy owners. “The handbook always wins” would be inaccurate.
2. **Repeated trials could be mistaken for independent cases.** The reconstructed 94/260 result comes from four runs of 65 tasks.
3. **Strict failure severity is heterogeneous.** One missed formatting field and one unauthorized termination both fail strict grading, although their consequences differ.
4. **Self-report should not be the audit artifact.** Tool and system state must control assurance decisions.
5. **External guards are an author recommendation, not a tested intervention in this benchmark.**
6. **The workflow must show who reads, interprets, authorizes, executes and audits.** The decisions section should distribute these functions instead of implying the model owns them all.

### Revisions made

- Added a paragraph explaining legitimate policy-author amendments and the need to resolve authority, recency and scope.
- Clarified that 94 outcomes are repeated trial results, not 94 independent workplace cases.
- Preserved the evidence-limit statement that strict grading does not rank severity.
- Preserved the distinction between observed benchmark failures and the authors’ guardrail recommendation.
- Strengthened operational decisions around executable approvals, audit traces and reversible authority.

### Unverifiable gaps retained as limits

- No live-enterprise outcome or employee/customer impact.
- No published prevalence denominator for each qualitative failure pattern.
- No intervention experiment comparing in-context policy with deterministic guards.
- No independent reproduction performed for this article.
