## Company-insider critique

Simulated perspective: enterprise internal-controls and operations leader. This is analytical review, not a statement from a named company.

### Challenges raised

1. A production organization may not receive the model’s private reasoning trace; governance cannot assume it will.
2. “Successful trajectory” is benchmark terminology and must not be treated as safe execution.
3. The 65 unsafe actions are rare and judge-adjudicated; they establish possibility, not prevalence across enterprises.
4. Scout selects among already-generated candidates; it is not a live kill switch or repair mechanism.
5. Irreversible actions require technical permission boundaries, not prompt wording alone.
6. The opening deletion is in a benchmark; production analogies must remain hypothetical risks.
7. A first-error model does not fit diffuse failures, which the paper says form about 43% of failed runs.

### Revisions made

- Added the absence of guaranteed private reasoning traces in commercial systems as an explicit limitation.
- Required audit truth to come from tool calls, approvals and system state rather than the agent’s narrative.
- Preserved the judge-disagreement range of 3–14 solved runs with destructive actions.
- Kept Scout framed as fixed-pool selection, not real-time prevention.
- Distributed institutional decisions across permissions, evidence, review, escalation and stress testing.
