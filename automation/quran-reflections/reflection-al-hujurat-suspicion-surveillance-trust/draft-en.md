# When Does the Desire to Know What People Conceal Become an Injustice?

> **Method note:** This is a contemporary reflection informed by established tafsir. It is neither a new interpretation of the verse nor a legal ruling on modern monitoring systems.

A vague alert reaches a team manager. Something about an employee’s behavior looks unusual, but nothing has been established. The first thought is tentative: “Perhaps there is more behind this.” Within minutes, possibility becomes a story. The story becomes a search through messages, browsing records and movements. What the search finds then becomes material for a conversation held while the employee is absent.

The process may be described as caution or institutional protection. Yet a prior question remains: when is seeking the truth a responsible duty, and when does it become an injury to the dignity of someone against whom no allegation has been proved?

Surah Al-Hujurat places this question within a wider protection of human relationships. Verse 10 calls for reconciliation between believers. Verse 11 prohibits ridicule, defamation and offensive names. Verse 12 then addresses suspicion, spying and backbiting, before verse 13 recalls humanity’s shared origin and makes righteousness—not access, tribe or rank—the measure of honor.

Sahih International begins the rendering of 49:12: “O you who have believed, avoid much [negative] assumption. Indeed, some assumption is sin.”[1] The verse continues by forbidding spying and backbiting, confronting the reader with the image of eating a dead brother’s flesh, and closing with the call to fear Allah and remember that He accepts repentance and is merciful.

In this setting, the verse does more than regulate speech. It protects a person’s dignity precisely when that person is not present to defend it.

## Before a suspicion becomes a file

Al-Tabari explains that the verse does not prohibit every thought. The danger is unjustified negative suspicion toward a believer. Al-Qurtubi develops the evidentiary boundary: blameworthy suspicion is an accusation without a valid sign or apparent cause, not every judgment reached through recognized evidence.[2][4]

That distinction matters. A person may not control the first possibility that enters the mind, but can refuse to promote it to the status of knowledge. Injustice begins when “perhaps” quietly becomes “he must have,” and an action is then built on certainty that never existed.

The verse does not require people to ignore an apparent risk or suspend responsible inquiry. It prevents us from filling an evidentiary gap with the worst story about someone and then treating that story as proof. Three questions restore discipline:

- What do we actually know?
- What have we inferred?
- What remains unsupported?

These questions do not weaken accountability. They keep accountability from becoming accusation.

## When inquiry crosses into intrusion

The order of the verse is striking: first suspicion, then spying, then backbiting. As a reflection on that order, we can see a possible progression of harm—an undisciplined inward judgment, a search into what was concealed, and finally speech about the absent person.

Al-Tabari describes tajassus as pursuing people’s private matters and searching their secrets in order to expose faults; he contrasts this with acting on what is manifest.[2] Al-Sa‘di similarly explains it as searching for concealed faults and notes that negative suspicion can lead to prohibited words and actions.[5] Ibn Kathir’s discussion includes the warning that pursuing people’s faults may corrupt them, a particularly serious caution for anyone who holds authority or surveillance capability.[3]

Not every verification process is the same as tajassus. A defined incident may require an authorized investigation to protect accounts, data or people. But there is a moral difference between a necessary, proportionate inquiry into a specific risk and comprehensive collection simply because the organization has the power to collect.

Technical capability is not, by itself, permission. What can be gathered is not automatically what should be gathered.

## The absent person consumed by words

The verse then prohibits ghibah, or backbiting. The tafsir sources preserve a clear distinction: backbiting is saying something true about an absent person that the person would dislike; if the claim is false, it becomes buhtan, or slander.[2][3][4]

The absent person cannot restore the missing context, explain why something happened or ask why certain details are being circulated. Even accurate information can move from a place of legitimate need into a place of harm. Truthfulness alone does not make every disclosure just, and access to information does not authorize unrestricted distribution.

The Qur’anic image refuses to make the harm sound harmless: eating the flesh of a dead brother. The person’s absence is part of the moral force of the image.

Yet the verse does not end with disgust or condemnation. It closes with repentance and mercy. Return remains possible: stop searching, correct the premature judgment, restore the dignity of the person harmed, and redesign a process that made exposure easier than restraint.

## A hypothetical case: monitoring that never turns off

Imagine that an institution experiences a limited leak of an internal file. Instead of identifying the vulnerability and narrowing the incident, management proposes permanent, detailed monitoring of every employee: messages, websites, login times and perhaps activities unrelated to the event.

The stated purpose is protection. Before deploying a tool, however, the institution needs answers that the tool cannot provide:

- Is there a specific indication that justifies the measure?
- Is collection limited to information necessary for the inquiry?
- Who may access the records, how long are they retained and what prevents reuse for another purpose?
- Can a less intrusive method provide the same protection?
- How are employees informed about the scope of the measure and their rights?
- When does monitoring end, and who reviews whether it remains necessary?

This case is hypothetical, not an interpretation of the verse. It does not label every security audit as spying and it offers no legal or religious ruling on a specific system. Its purpose is narrower: to ask whether the institution is seeking a defined truth with the least necessary intrusion, or turning suspicion into an open license to collect people’s lives.

## Three 10X questions

The verse is not a modern management framework. It can, however, open responsible questions that connect with several Tamayuz 10X pillars.

### Governance starts with evidence

Here, governance means the rules that determine who decides, on what evidence and who reviews the decision. Any measure affecting a person’s privacy or reputation requires an apparent reason, defined authority and a review path. Good governance protects the institution from external threats and from injustice produced by its own tools.

### Institutional intelligence is not total collection

Institutional intelligence is the ability to turn reliable information into a better decision, not simply to store more. Data may multiply while understanding deteriorates. A capable institution defines the question first and then gathers what is needed to answer it. Retaining every digital trace “just in case” creates new risks of leakage and misuse and may build a culture in which every action is feared out of context.

### Culture is tested in someone’s absence

Respect is easy to proclaim in public. Its harder test comes when the person concerned is not in the room. Is information verified before it circulates? Is the person given a right to respond? Does discussion stop with those who genuinely need to know? In these moments, dignity becomes an operating practice rather than a value statement.

## Questions before searching what is concealed

1. What apparent evidence justifies moving from concern to investigation?
2. What is the least amount of information needed to establish the truth?
3. Do affected people know the limits of the process, and who reviews the use of their data?
4. Even if the information is accurate, does this person genuinely need to receive it?

**The 10X question:** If your organization’s ability to see what people do doubles tomorrow, will it double surveillance—or design wiser limits that protect security and dignity together?

## A final word

The verse begins in a place no one else can see: suspicion within the heart. It then moves to an act that searches what was concealed, and finally to words that harm someone who is absent. At each stage, there is an opportunity to stop.

Do not turn possibility into fact. Do not turn capability into permission. Do not turn absence into an invitation to speak.

And if the boundary has already been crossed, the road is not closed. The verse ends by naming Allah as the Accepter of repentance, the Merciful. Taqwa includes guarding people from our injustice—and having the courage to return when we discover that we have gone too far.
