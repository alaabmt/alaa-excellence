# Research notes — Al-Hujurat 49:12

**Research date:** 26 October 2026  
**Working title:** متى تتحول الرغبة في معرفة ما يخفيه الناس إلى ظلم؟  
**English title:** When Does the Desire to Know What People Conceal Become an Injustice?

## Selection and scope

Selected angle: Al-Hujurat 49:12—suspicion, spying and the dignity of what remains unseen. Al-Mutaffifin 83:1–6 was rejected because it overlaps the published Shuʿayb reflection on fair measure; Al-Ma’idah 5:2 remains a future candidate.

The article may use a hypothetical employee-monitoring case, but it must not equate every legitimate audit, investigation or security control with tajassus. The application is the author’s reflection, not the historical tafsir.

## Verified primary text

**Surah:** Al-Hujurat (الحجرات), **49:12**

> يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا اجْتَنِبُوا كَثِيرًا مِّنَ الظَّنِّ إِنَّ بَعْضَ الظَّنِّ إِثْمٌ ۖ وَلَا تَجَسَّسُوا وَلَا يَغْتَب بَّعْضُكُم بَعْضًا ۚ أَيُحِبُّ أَحَدُكُمْ أَن يَأْكُلَ لَحْمَ أَخِيهِ مَيْتًا فَكَرِهْتُمُوهُ ۚ وَاتَّقُوا اللَّهَ ۚ إِنَّ اللَّهَ تَوَّابٌ رَّحِيمٌ

The Sahih International rendering was checked at legacy Quran.com. Its opening reads: “O you who have believed, avoid much [negative] assumption. Indeed, some assumption is sin. And do not spy or backbite each other.” Use that verified source during drafting.

## Immediate context: 49:10–13

- **49:10:** brotherhood and reconciliation establish the social purpose behind the restraints that follow.
- **49:11:** prohibits ridicule, defamation and offensive labels; protection of dignity precedes verse 12.
- **49:12:** moves from an adverse ungrounded judgment to searching into what is concealed and then speaking about the absent person.
- **49:13:** makes taqwa—not tribe, access or rank—the measure of honor.

Calling this a “moral sequence” is an editorial synthesis, not a verbatim claim attributed to every mufassir.

## Tafsir findings

### Al-Tabari

- “Much suspicion” is not every thought. The prohibited case is evil suspicion toward believers without right, while thinking well is encouraged.
- Tajassus is following private matters and searching secrets to expose faults; judgment should rest on what is manifest, not unknown inner matters.
- Ghibah is saying in a person’s absence what they would dislike; if false, it is buhtan.

### Ibn Kathir

- The verse prohibits accusation and distrust without a proper basis and connects this with the prophetic warning against suspicion and spying.
- Reports about rulers pursuing suspicion reinforce the governance relevance of restraint.
- He preserves the distinction between ghibah—a disliked truth—and buhtan—a false claim.

### Al-Qurtubi

- The warning concerns accusation without a valid sign or visible cause; it does not condemn every evidence-based judgment.
- Tajassus is searching for what is concealed and following private faults after God has covered them; he contrasts this with responding to wrongdoing that appears openly.
- His evidentiary distinctions must not be converted into a modern compliance code without separate legal and institutional analysis.

### Al-Saʿdi

- Unfounded suspicion can generate prohibited speech and conduct.
- Tajassus is searching for and following hidden faults; restraint is advised toward matters that surface only through intrusive searching.
- The image of eating a dead brother’s flesh makes the ugliness of ghibah perceptible, while the close preserves repentance and mercy.

## Agreement, differences and terms

All four sources protect a person from baseless suspicion, pursuit of concealed faults and harmful speech in absence. Al-Tabari and Al-Saʿdi are compact; Ibn Kathir supplies a broad hadith frame; Al-Qurtubi most fully distinguishes unsupported suspicion from evidence-based judgment.

- **سوء الظن:** adverse inward judgment without adequate evidence—the epistemic starting point.
- **التجسس:** active pursuit of concealed faults or secrets—suspicion turned into intrusion.
- **الغيبة:** communicating a disliked truth about an absent person; a false claim becomes buhtan.
- **Manifest vs concealed:** reacting to apparent wrongdoing is not the same as searching merely to expose private faults.
- **Reflection vs ruling:** the article may discuss necessity, proportionality and dignity, but is not a fatwa or complete privacy doctrine.

## Contemporary application boundary

Use one clearly hypothetical case: an institution considers always-on employee monitoring after a misconduct incident. Ask whether it is necessary, proportionate, authorized, time-bound and directed at a defined risk. Distinguish accountable controls from curiosity, indiscriminate surveillance and collecting private faults.

## Draft thesis

The verse protects absent dignity by interrupting a moral chain: do not treat unsupported suspicion as knowledge, turn it into a hunt for hidden faults, or convert a person’s absence into permission to consume their reputation. Responsible inquiry begins with evidence, defined necessity and restraint.
