# Topic selection — 26 October 2026

## Duplicate baseline

The published-topic registry, Qur’anic collection indexes, general article indexes, publication-date registry, sitemap and repository search contain no Qur’anic reflection dated 2026-10-26.

## Candidate angles

1. **Al-Hujurat 49:12 — suspicion, spying and the dignity of what remains unseen**
   - Central question: When does the desire to know what people conceal become an injustice?
   - Contemporary application: a hypothetical institution considering intrusive employee monitoring, with necessity, proportionality and human dignity kept distinct from tafsir.
   - Novelty: no published reflection currently treats suspicion, spying, backbiting or surveillance.

2. **Al-Mutaffifin 83:1–6 — demanding a full measure while giving less**
   - Central question: Where do we ask for precision from others while accepting distortion from ourselves?
   - Contemporary application: asymmetric service metrics and selective reporting.
   - Novelty risk: materially overlaps the published Shuʿayb reflection on fair measure and deprivation of others’ due; not selected.

3. **Al-Ma’idah 5:2 — the boundary of cooperation**
   - Central question: Is every request for help worthy of our capability?
   - Contemporary application: a team asked to use legitimate system access for an improper purpose.
   - Novelty: distinct from the existing justice-at-disagreement reflection, but broader and more likely to drift into generic ethics.

## Selection

**Selected:** Al-Hujurat 49:12 — suspicion, spying and the dignity of what remains unseen.

**Working Arabic title:** متى تتحول الرغبة في معرفة ما يخفيه الناس إلى ظلم؟

**Working English title:** When Does the Desire to Know What People Conceal Become an Injustice?

**Thesis:** The verse places suspicion, spying and backbiting in one moral sequence that protects a person’s unseen dignity; a modern monitoring example may illuminate the question of restraint, but it is an application proposed by the author, not the tafsir of the verse.

**Scope boundary:** Research must establish the verse text, surrounding context, accepted tafsir and any differences over the prohibition’s scope. The article must not equate every legitimate audit, investigation or security control with tajassus, and must not infer hidden intentions.

## Research plan

- Verify Qur’an 49:12 and its immediate context, especially 49:10–13.
- Review al-Tabari, Ibn Kathir, al-Qurtubi and al-Saʿdi.
- Preserve the distinction among سوء الظن, التجسس and الغيبة.
- Identify what the sources do and do not say about exceptions, public wrongs and authorized inquiry.
- Use one clearly hypothetical contemporary example only after the tafsir boundary is established.
