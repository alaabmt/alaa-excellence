## Qur'anic accuracy

### Primary ayah

The Arabic text quoted for 39:18 matches the verified text in the research checkpoint. The surah name and ayah number are correct.

### Context

The draft correctly anchors the primary ayah in 39:17, where avoiding false worship and turning to Allah precede the description of those who listen and follow the best. It also preserves the contrast introduced in 39:19.

One contextual sentence was tightened so that it now refers more precisely to “one upon whom the word of punishment has become due,” rather than using a looser summary.

### English translation dependency

No English Qur'an translation appears in the Arabic draft. The English stage must use the already verified Sahih International wording and attribute it explicitly.

## Tafsir and attribution

### Al-Tabari

The draft's statement was revised to mirror the recorded finding more closely: Allah guided them to right conduct and sound judgment. It does not present a reported occasion of revelation as the sole scope of the ayah.

### Ibn Kathir

The attribution that they understand speech and act upon it is supported by the research record. The connection to sound intellect and upright disposition is preserved without inflation.

### Al-Qurtubi

The draft correctly says that al-Qurtubi records multiple explanations and explicitly warns against treating them as a single consensus. The examples were revised to use more concrete reported possibilities: following the Qur'an over other speech, following its decisive guidance, and preferring pardon in its relevant setting.

### Al-Sa'di

The draft safely attributes broad discernment while retaining the indispensable limit that Allah's speech and the Messenger's speech are best in the absolute sense.

## Tafsir–reflection boundary

The boundary is explicit and repeated at the points where confusion would be most likely:

- the article says the organizational application is a contemporary reflection, not tafsir;
- it rejects reducing the ayah to an unrestricted opinion-comparison technique;
- it refuses to define “best” by preference, popularity, efficiency or status;
- the decision-team scenario is clearly labeled hypothetical;
- the methodological note restates that the applications are not a new interpretation of the ayah.

A four-item application passage was revised from declarative “requirements” into self-review questions. This reduces the risk that readers mistake a contemporary management framework for an exegetical conclusion.

## Arabic editorial review

The draft reads as original contemporary Arabic rather than a literal translation. Its paragraphs are generally short, its headings carry distinct ideas, and its punctuation is consistent.

Strengths:

- the opening presents the tension quickly;
- the article returns repeatedly to the transition from listening to following;
- the religious frame precedes the organizational application;
- the hypothetical example is concrete without claiming a real institution or event;
- the conclusion returns to the ayah rather than ending on managerial language.

Non-blocking risks checked:

- **Repetition:** recurring references to hearing, discernment and following remain purposeful rather than redundant.
- **Overstatement:** no claim of consensus or exclusive interpretation remains.
- **Anthropocentric framing:** divine guidance remains central.
- **Meta language:** no unnecessary “this article will discuss” phrasing appears.
- **Institutional overreach:** the workplace section is expressly framed as reflection, not a Qur'anic operating model.
