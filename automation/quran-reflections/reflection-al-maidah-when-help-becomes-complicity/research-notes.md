### Selected angle

**Al-Ma’idah 5:2 — the boundary of cooperation.**

This angle is distinct from the published reflection on Al-Ma’idah 5:8. The earlier article examines justice when dislike pulls against an opponent; this cycle asks when assistance itself crosses from cooperation in righteousness and piety into cooperation in sin and aggression.

### Central question

### Tafsir synthesis

#### Al-Tabari

Al-Tabari explains cooperation in `al-birr` as believers helping one another to do what Allah commanded, and `al-taqwa` as avoiding what Allah commanded them to avoid. He explains `al-ithm` in relation to leaving what was commanded and `al-‘udwan` as exceeding the limits Allah set. His reading keeps cooperation subordinate to the revealed boundary rather than to the usefulness of a shared objective.

Source: https://quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/tabary/sura5-aya2.html

#### Ibn Kathir

Ibn Kathir summarizes the command as mutual assistance in good deeds and in leaving wrongdoing, while prohibiting support for falsehood, sins and forbidden acts. This supports the article’s central distinction: the fact that an action helps another person does not by itself make the cooperation good.

Source: https://quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/katheer/sura5-aya2.html

#### Al-Qurtubi

Al-Qurtubi treats the cooperation clause as a general command and describes several forms of mutual help. In the same discussion he states that the transgressor is not to be supported and should be turned back from the transgression. He distinguishes `al-ithm` from `al-‘udwan`, describing the latter in relation to injustice toward people.

Source: https://quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/qortobi/sura5-aya2.html

#### Al-Sa‘di

Al-Sa‘di describes `al-birr` broadly as what Allah loves and is pleased with in outward and inward deeds, and `al-taqwa` as leaving what Allah and His Messenger dislike. He includes assistance by words that encourage good and by actions that enable it. He describes aggression as violating people in their lives, property and honour, and says a person must refrain from wrongdoing and help others leave it.

Source: https://quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/saadi/sura5-aya2.html

### Convergence and material differences

- The four tafsir sources converge on a broad moral distinction: cooperation is commanded when directed to what Allah commands and approves, and prohibited when it supports sin, falsehood, prohibited acts or transgression against others.
- Al-Tabari gives compact definitional links to command, prohibition and the limits set by Allah.
- Ibn Kathir states the contrast in terms of good deeds versus falsehood, sins and forbidden acts.
- Al-Qurtubi emphasizes the generality of the command and gives examples of mutual aid, including withholding support from a transgressor.
- Al-Sa‘di expands the practical forms of cooperation to words and actions and makes harm to people’s life, property and honour explicit.
- The commentators discuss differences elsewhere in the verse concerning sacred months, pilgrimage rites and abrogation. Those questions are not required to establish the selected cooperation clause and will not be flattened into a claim of consensus about every part of the verse.

### Interpretive boundary

The proposed title uses the contemporary phrase “participation in wrongdoing” to surface the human question. It is not a quotation from the verse or from a commentator. The article must:

1. present the verse and tafsir before the contemporary application;
2. identify the application as reflection, not binding tafsir or a modern rule attributed to the Qur’an;
3. avoid claiming that every indirect contribution has the same moral or legal status;
4. avoid issuing a legal ruling about complex chains of responsibility;
5. direct genuinely difficult fiqh questions to qualified scholars rather than resolving them through an institutional example.

### Contemporary application — working scenario

A hypothetical team is asked to accelerate access to a beneficial public service. One proposed shortcut would omit a safeguard that protects a vulnerable group from exclusion or misuse of data. The scenario will not ask only whether the service goal is good; it will ask whether the requested contribution helps the good itself or helps a wrongful means. The practical reflection may distinguish among:

- clarifying the legitimate goal;
- naming the harmful step rather than hiding it inside urgency;
- refusing the wrongful contribution;
- proposing a lawful, proportionate alternative;
- documenting the unresolved risk and who is accountable for the decision.

These are contemporary reflections, not the verse’s tafsir and not a universal compliance checklist.

### Evidence limits

- Tafsir sources establish the moral boundary of cooperation; they do not describe the modern organizational scenario.
- The verse does not supply a ready-made governance procedure or numerical risk threshold.
- The selected example is hypothetical and must be labelled as such.
- The article must not equate disagreement, uncertainty or ordinary error with `ithm` or `‘udwan`.
- Questions involving indirect causation, necessity, coercion, mixed benefit and harm, or formal legal responsibility may require specialist jurisprudential analysis beyond this reflection.
