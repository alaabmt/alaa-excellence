## Selected angle

- Arabic working title: **متى تفقد العطية معناها؟**
- English working title: **When Does a Gift Lose Its Meaning?**
- Qur'anic focus: Al-Baqarah 2:262–264.
- Working thesis: the verses place what happens after giving inside the moral quality of the act. A gift is not evaluated only by what leaves the giver's hand; reminders and injury can destroy its reward, while kind speech and forgiveness can be better than material charity followed by harm.
- Contemporary application: an explicitly modern analogy about reviewing an aid, grant, or public-service journey for dignity, privacy, and freedom from humiliation or coercive gratitude.
- Methodological boundary: the verses do not prescribe a modern service-design framework. Tafsir, reflection, and application will be labeled and kept distinct.

## Immediate Qur'anic context

- **2:261** opens the passage with the multiplied reward of spending in Allah's cause.
- **2:262** describes those who do not follow spending with reminders or injury.
- **2:263** states that kind speech and forgiveness are better than charity followed by injury.
- **2:264** prohibits invalidating charity through reminders and harm, and gives the image of dust on a smooth rock washed bare by heavy rain; the same verse also distinguishes sincere giving from spending to be seen by people.
- **2:265** contrasts this with spending that seeks Allah's pleasure and firmness from within.
- Contextual conclusion: the passage moves from the multiplication of sincere charity to the conditions that preserve or destroy its reward, then to a contrasting image of rooted, fruitful giving.

## Tafsir findings

### Al-Tabari

- On 2:262, al-Tabari explains that the giver does not follow the spending with `mann` or injury; he describes the reminder as showing the recipient, by word or action, that a favor was done for them.
- On 2:264, he treats the verse as a prohibition against voiding charity by reminders and injury and explains the smooth-rock image as an appearance of soil that cannot sustain growth once the downpour exposes the stone.
- Direct sources:
  - https://quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/tabary/sura2-aya262.html
  - https://quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/tabary/sura2-aya264.html

### Ibn Kathir

- On 2:263–264, Ibn Kathir's abridged explanation states that reminding recipients about charity is forbidden and that reminders may be conveyed in words or actions.
- On 2:264, he says the charity is invalidated by what follows it of reminder and injury, and compares the moral loss to the bare rock after rain.
- Direct source:
  - https://quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/katheer/sura2-aya264.html
  - supporting page text: https://quran.com/2/263

### Al-Qurtubi

- On 2:264, al-Qurtubi explains that the verse expresses non-acceptance and deprivation of reward as invalidation. He records interpretive detail on whether the invalidation concerns the affected charity specifically and notes the rock image as exposing the true condition of the act.
- He explicitly limits the affected act rather than treating one harmful instance as cancelling every other good deed.
- Direct source:
  - https://quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/qortobi/sura2-aya264.html

### Al-Sa'di

- On 2:264, al-Sa'di reads the prohibition as mercy and care: the giver is urged to complete and preserve good work from what corrupts it. He contrasts a deed intended for Allah with an act performed for people and explains the smooth rock as incapable of producing growth.
- Direct source:
  - https://quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/saadi/sura2-aya264.html

## Agreed meaning and cautions

- Strong common ground across the sources: reminders and injury after charity are prohibited and can destroy the reward of the affected act; sincerity for Allah is central; visible giving alone is not proof of sound moral substance.
- The article should not broaden this into a claim that every later mistake cancels all a person's good deeds. Al-Qurtubi's discussion is a useful boundary.
- The modern language of “dignity,” “privacy,” and “service journey” is a reflective application. It is not to be attributed verbatim to the mufassirun.
- Avoid judging the intentions of identifiable people or organizations. The article should examine designs and behaviors, not claim knowledge of hearts.
- Do not reduce the passage to donor etiquette. The primary meaning concerns worship, sincerity, reward, and conduct before Allah.
