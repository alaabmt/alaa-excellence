# Company Insider Critique — Costa Rica PES

> **Status:** Simulated adversarial review, not a statement or endorsement by FONAFIFO, the Government of Costa Rica, the World Bank, the OECD or any programme participant.

## Insider challenge

If this article reached a programme director, finance officer, evaluator, participant or excluded landholder, the strongest objections would be:

### 1. “You are turning cumulative programme coverage into unique land area.”

Valid objection. The 1.3 million-hectare figure is a cumulative programme coverage measure. Contract renewals and modalities can make it different from a map of unique, permanently protected hectares.

**Required revision:** Add “cumulative programme coverage” and explicitly say it is not a unique-area or additional-impact count wherever the figure is first used.

### 2. “You make the fund look like a single-purpose contractor, but it operates within a broader legal and institutional system.”

Valid. FONAFIFO is the financial/contract integrator, not the sole actor responsible for forest recovery, enforcement, protected areas or REDD+ outcomes.

**Required revision:** Retain the wider policy-package language and add that monitoring/verification functions are distributed across the broader system.

### 3. “The 0.21-hectare estimate could be read as a universal annual saving.”

Valid. The IDB estimate is sample- and design-specific, significant only in the first year in that analysis.

**Required revision:** State that 0.21 hectares is an average estimated first-year treatment effect per enrolled farm/parcel in the study sample, and never annualise or scale it nationally.

### 4. “Your equity section mixes participant categories, contracts and people.”

Valid. OECD and World Bank denominators differ. Legal entities, male/female beneficiaries and Indigenous associations are administrative categories, not population groups that can be compared without qualification.

**Required revision:** Label the OECD percentages as the participant/contract distribution cited by that source; avoid implying that a legal entity and an individual are equivalent units of social benefit.

### 5. “The carbon result is not a PES result.”

Valid and already substantially addressed. The USD 16.4 million/3.28 million-tonne result belongs to the wider national emissions-reduction programme.

**Required revision:** In the executive summary, mention the verified carbon payment only as a later financing layer if used; keep the broader-programme attribution warning adjacent.

### 6. “A payment contract does not prove biodiversity, water or scenic outcomes.”

Valid and explicitly recognised.

**Required revision:** Strengthen the transferable lesson: institutions should not use contracted hectares as a permanent proxy for four different ecosystem services.

### 7. “The programme has changed over nearly three decades; one static description is misleading.”

Valid. Eligibility, priorities, modalities and payment differentiation evolved.

**Required revision:** Date the 2024 service-stacking description and present it as a current design iteration whose outcomes are not yet proven.

### 8. “Fuel-tax financing is both a strength and a transition risk.”

Valid and well handled. The article should preserve this tension and avoid presenting earmarking as universally transferable.

## Factual challenge results

- Law 7575 and four services: supported.
- FONAFIFO roles and 3.5% fuel-tax share: supported.
- 1.3 million hectares and USD 600m scale: supported with period/cumulative caveats.
- IDB first-year 0.21-hectare estimate: supported but must remain study-specific.
- 11%–17% farm-level finding: supported and regional only.
- 2018–2019 verified carbon result: supported and belongs to broader REDD+ policy.
- Participation gaps: supported but denominators require careful language.
- “PES restored Costa Rica's forests”: rejected.

## Editorial and reputational challenge

The draft is independent and avoids promotional language. The strongest reputational risk is not criticism of the programme; it is overstating causality and then forcing programme officials or researchers to correct the article. The causal limits should remain in the executive summary, results section and conclusion—not be buried in references.

## Verdict

**PASS WITH FOUR TEXTUAL REVISIONS BEFORE ENGLISH DRAFT**

1. Qualify 1.3 million hectares as cumulative programme coverage.
2. Specify the unit/context of the IDB 0.21-hectare estimate.
3. Clarify equity denominators and non-equivalence of administrative categories.
4. State that monitoring and broader forest outcomes involve a wider institutional system than FONAFIFO alone.

No objection requires rejection. The critique confirms the case's value precisely because it distinguishes institutional achievement from a simplistic forest-recovery claim.
