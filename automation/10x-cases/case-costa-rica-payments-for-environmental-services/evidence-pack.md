# Evidence Pack — Costa Rica Payments for Environmental Services

- **Case slug:** `case-costa-rica-payments-for-environmental-services`
- **Evidence review date:** 2026-10-29 (Asia/Dubai)
- **Gate decision:** PASS WITH EXPLICIT CAUSAL AND EQUITY LIMITS
- **Editorial thesis permitted:** Costa Rica changed the institutional economics of forest protection by recognising ecosystem services, funding a public contracting mechanism and iterating its design.
- **Thesis not permitted:** PES alone restored Costa Rica's forests or eliminated deforestation.

## Evidence classification

| Evidence state | Meaning in this case |
|---|---|
| Declared goal | What the law, programme or later policy says it intends to achieve. |
| Design capacity | The legal, financial, contracting and monitoring mechanism available to act. |
| Implemented activity | Contracts, hectares enrolled, payments or verified programme operations. |
| Observed result | A measured change, without assuming the programme alone caused it. |
| Causal estimate | A counterfactual or quasi-experimental estimate of incremental programme effect. |

## Required separations for publication

### 1. Declared goal
Pay landholders for four legally recognised environmental services and strengthen conservation, restoration and sustainable forest management.

### 2. Design capacity
Forest Law 7575; FONAFIFO as fund manager; contracts; technical procedures; monitoring; a fuel-tax earmark plus water, donor, credit and results-based finance.

### 3. Implemented activity
About 1.3 million hectares covered since 1997 and more than USD 600 million paid over the OECD's cited 24-year period. These are programme scale measures, not ecological impact measures.

### 4. Observed result
National forest recovery, regional declines in mature-forest conversion and independently verified 2018–2019 emissions reductions. These outcomes reflect a policy package that also includes clearing restrictions, protected areas, agricultural/economic change and tourism.

### 5. Incremental causal result
Credible studies find positive but heterogeneous additionality: a strong first-year effect in the 2022 IDB study, no significant later-year effect there, and region/cohort-specific gains elsewhere.

## Confidence summary

- **Legal and institutional redesign:** High
- **Funding mechanism:** High
- **Programme scale and expenditure:** High, with period caveats
- **Verified 2018–2019 emission reductions/payment:** High
- **PES contribution to forest protection:** Medium-high, heterogeneous
- **Claim that PES alone caused national forest recovery:** Rejected
- **Poverty/well-being benefit:** Insufficient; do not claim
- **Equitable access:** Mixed; documented participation gaps must be disclosed
- **2024 stacked-payment effectiveness:** Not yet established

## Evidence gaps that must remain visible

1. No clean national attribution of forest-cover recovery to PES alone.
2. Contracted hectares do not equal additional forest saved or restored.
3. Carbon-payment verification covers a broader emissions-reduction programme.
4. Ecological services beyond forest cover/carbon are not consistently measured at contract level.
5. Participation and land-tenure barriers remain, especially for women, some Indigenous landholders and smallholders.
6. Long-term causal effects differ by cohort, location and baseline threat.

## Evidence-gate verdict

The case clears the evidence threshold because the institutional transformation, operating mechanism, programme implementation and multiple outcome layers are documented by primary and independent sources. Publication remains conditional on retaining the causal limits above and avoiding the false headline that one programme “restored the forest.”
