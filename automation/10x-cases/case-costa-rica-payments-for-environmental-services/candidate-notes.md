# Candidate Scan — Costa Rica Payments for Environmental Services

- **Working title (EN):** How Costa Rica Paid to Keep Forests Standing
- **Selection status:** Selected for evidence-pack review; publication is not yet authorized.

## Problem-first rationale

By the mid-1990s, Costa Rica had lost roughly half of the forest cover it held in 1950. The institutional problem was not a lack of environmental ambition alone: public incentives had historically made forest clearing economically rational. The candidate is analytically valuable because the country changed the operating model—recognising four environmental services in law, paying landholders through contracts, assigning a dedicated fund manager, and linking the mechanism to earmarked and market-based finance.

## Preliminary evidence threshold

The candidate advances because the initial scan found:

1. a clear strategic challenge and policy reversal;
2. an identifiable legal and operating mechanism rather than a campaign;
3. nationwide contracting and a long implementation record;
4. verified outcome measures and an independent impact evaluation;
5. explicit limits to attribution, allowing separation of national forest recovery from the incremental effect of PES contracts;
6. a verified World Bank video suitable for an end-of-article embed if the final gate passes.

## Duplicate and novelty check

No existing 10X case with this slug, institution, or mechanism was found in the publication registry/current case index reviewed for this run. The novelty is the institutional financing mechanism—paying for verified ecosystem services—not generic reforestation or a climate award.

## Kill criteria for the next stage

Reject the case if the evidence pack cannot:
- distinguish national forest-cover recovery from the causal contribution of PES;
- separate targets, contracted area, observed outcomes and verified emission reductions;
- substantiate the funding and governance mechanism with primary sources;
- identify material distributional or targeting limitations;
- verify every published number and the video source.
