# Evidence Pack — Sweden Vision Zero

- Case: Sweden's Vision Zero road-safety transformation
- Slug: `case-sweden-vision-zero-safe-system-road-safety`
- Evidence review date: 2026-10-23 (Asia/Dubai)
- Decision: **PASS to analysis**, with explicit causal limits
- Overall confidence: **high** for policy architecture, implementation and reported outcomes; **medium** for national-level attribution; **high** for evaluated 2+1-road and speed-limit interventions.

## Evidence architecture

### 1. Declared future state

**Claim.** Sweden's Parliament adopted Vision Zero in 1997 as the basis for road-safety work: no one should be killed or suffer lifelong/serious injury in road traffic.

- Primary: Swedish Transport Administration, “This is Vision Zero,” updated 21 January 2020.  
  https://bransch.trafikverket.se/en/startpage/operations/Operations-road/vision-zero-academy/This-is-Vision-Zero/
- Primary: Swedish Transport Administration, “Management by Objectives,” updated 30 January 2020.  
  https://bransch.trafikverket.se/en/startpage/operations/Operations-road/vision-zero-academy/management-by-objectives/
- Independent: OECD/International Transport Forum, *The Safe System Approach in Action*, 2022.  
  https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2022/06/the-safe-system-approach-in-action_80a2cfc3/ad5d82f0-en.pdf
- Confidence: **high**
- Limit: A parliamentary vision is an intent and governance commitment, not itself an achieved safety result.

### 2. Strategic redefinition

**Claim.** Vision Zero reframed the central problem from the occurrence of crashes to whether predictable crashes lead to death or lifelong injury; roads, vehicles and users must operate as one system designed around human fallibility and biomechanical tolerance.

- Primary: Swedish Transport Administration, “This is Vision Zero,” lines 93–95.
- Primary: Swedish Transport Administration, “Key interventions based on Vision Zero,” updated 10 December 2019.  
  https://bransch.trafikverket.se/en/startpage/operations/Operations-road/vision-zero-academy/Key-interventions-based-on-Vision-Zero/
- Independent: OECD/ITF 2022, pp. 13–14, describes Safe System as working backward from eliminating fatalities and serious injuries rather than relying mainly on correcting human error.
- Academic: Lubbe et al., “Injury risk curves to guide safe speed limits on Swedish roads,” *Accident Analysis & Prevention*, 2024; PubMed PMID 38669899.  
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38669899/
- Confidence: **high**
- Limit: This establishes the design logic; it does not quantify the contribution of each component.

### 3. Governance and learning model

**Claim.** Sweden translated the long-term vision into interim casualty targets, leading safety-performance indicators, annual data analysis, stakeholder conferences and a common priority-setting cycle.

- Primary: Swedish Transport Administration, “Management by Objectives,” lines 90–120.
- Independent: OECD/ITF 2022, pp. 13–14, recommends interim targets, safety-performance indicators, data collection, cross-sector governance and backcasting.
- Confidence: **high**
- Important counterevidence: the first interim target—no more than 270 deaths by 2007—was not achieved. Independent assessments identified unclear management, weak stakeholder involvement and inadequate monitoring, prompting a redesigned management-by-objectives model in 2008–2009.
- Interpretive boundary: The missed target is evidence of an adaptive governance loop, not evidence that the original plan worked as designed.

### 4. Data and diagnostic capability

**Claim.** The operating model uses fatal-crash in-depth studies and STRADA (the Swedish Traffic Accident Data Acquisition system), plus leading indicators tied to verified safety outcomes.

- Primary: Swedish Transport Administration, “Vision Zero and ways to work / Data Collection.”  
  https://bransch.trafikverket.se/en/startpage/operations/Operations-road/vision-zero-academy/Vision-Zero-and-ways-to-work/data-collection/
- Primary: Swedish Transport Administration, “Management by Objectives,” lines 102–116.
- Confidence: **high**
- Limit: Reporting coverage and injury definitions can change; fatality counts are more comparable than severe-injury series.

### 5. Infrastructure implementation

**Claim.** Sweden implemented physical and operational measures including median-separated 2+1 roads, traffic cameras, roundabouts, alcolocks and speed management, rather than relying on awareness alone.

- Primary: Swedish Transport Administration, “Key interventions based on Vision Zero,” lines 96–105.
- Primary: Swedish Transport Administration, “Median Barriers,” notes the first median-barrier pilot opened in summer 1998 on a previously high-fatality section.  
  https://bransch.trafikverket.se/en/startpage/operations/Operations-road/vision-zero-academy/Key-interventions-based-on-Vision-Zero/median-barriers/
- Primary outcome inventory: Swedish Transport Administration, *Analysis of Road Safety Trends 2022*, published 2023, pp. 22–25.  
  https://bransch.trafikverket.se/globalassets/dokument/vision-zero/analysis-of-road-safety-trends-2022.pdf
- Confidence: **high**

### 6. Scale of protected-road deployment

**Claim.** By the end of 2022, Sweden had 5,620 km of national roads with median barriers; 4,970 km were on national roads with speed limits above 80 km/h. On roads above 80 km/h, 84.8% of traffic volume travelled on median-protected roads.

- Primary: Swedish Transport Administration, *Analysis of Road Safety Trends 2022*, lines 783–824.
- Confidence: **high**
- Limit: The 5,620-km inventory includes different median-separated road types, not only 2+1 roads. It must not be presented as 5,620 km of 2+1 roads.

### 7. Evaluated effect of narrow 2+1 conversions

**Claim.** A before–after study with a control group, traffic-volume adjustment and an Empirical Bayes check found that narrow 2+1 roads with median barriers reduced fatalities and serious injuries by 50% overall and 63% on road links; personal-injury crashes fell 21% overall.

- Academic/independent technical source: Vadeby, Swedish National Road and Transport Research Institute (VTI), “Traffic safety effects of narrow 2+1 roads with median barrier in Sweden,” 2016.  
  https://trid.trb.org/view/1454135  
  Full text: http://vti.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:920845/FULLTEXT01.pdf
- Secondary independent corroboration: PIARC World Road Association road-safety manual reports a 76% fatality reduction for the broader Swedish 2+1 programme.  
  https://roadsafety.piarc.org/en/planning-design-operation/intervention-selection/project-network-approaches
- Confidence: **high** for the evaluated road sections; **medium** for generalisation to all roads.
- Limits: The after-period was short; most converted sections also moved from 90 to 100 km/h; the result is section-specific and cannot explain the entire national trend.

### 8. Evaluated speed-management effect

**Claim.** Sweden's 2008 national rural-speed-limit review linked posted speed to road design. On 2+1 roads where the limit fell from 110 to 100 km/h, the study estimated about 16 fewer serious injuries per year.

- Academic: Vadeby and Forsman, “Traffic safety effects of new speed limits in Sweden,” *Accident Analysis & Prevention*, 2017; PubMed PMID 28219638.  
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28219638/
- Primary policy explanation: Swedish Transport Administration, “Speed limits on roads.”  
  https://www.trafikverket.se/resa-och-trafik/trafiksakerhet/sakerhet-pa-vag/hastighetsgranser-pa-vag/
- Confidence: **high** for the evaluated intervention.
- Limit: This is not an estimate of the total Vision Zero effect.

### 9. Decade-scale outcome

**Claim.** Finalised OECD/ITF data record 270 road deaths in 2014 and 213 in 2024, a decline of 57 deaths or 21.1%. Swedish authorities reported traffic volume rose by roughly 4% over the decade.

- Independent: OECD Data Explorer, annual road fatalities, dataset updated 9 June 2026.  
  https://data-explorer.oecd.org/vis?df%5Bag%5D=OECD.ITF&df%5Bds%5D=dsDisseminateFinalDMZ&df%5Bid%5D=DSD_TRENDS%40DF_TRENDSSAFETY&df%5Bvs%5D=1.0
- Primary: Swedish Transport Administration, “Fewer road deaths, but more action is needed,” published 14 January 2025.  
  https://www.trafikverket.se/om-oss/nyheter/nationella-nyheter/2025/januari/farre-doda-i-vagtrafiken-men-fler-insatser-behovs-for-att-na-nollvisionen/
- Confidence: **high** for final counts; **medium-high** for the exposure comparison.
- Reconciliation note: the January 2025 Swedish page used a preliminary 2024 count of 210; later Swedish and OECD/ITF reporting use 213. The case will use **213** and identify it as the finalised figure.

### 10. Long-run outcome

**Claim.** Road deaths fell materially after the 1997 adoption. Contemporary official reporting describes a reduction of roughly 60% over about 25 years despite higher traffic volume.

- Primary: Swedish Transport Administration, 14 January 2025 update.
- Independent contextual corroboration: OECD/ITF Sweden Road Safety Country Profile 2025, published 30 January 2026, records 213 deaths in 2024 and a 21% decline from 2014.  
  https://www.itf-oecd.org/sites/default/files/sweden-road-safety.pdf
- Confidence: **high** for the direction and order of magnitude; **medium** for attribution.
- Causal limit: Sweden's fatality trend was already improving before 1997 and is affected by vehicle safety, enforcement, exposure, emergency care, economic cycles and other policies. Use “consistent with and supported by the implemented system” rather than “caused solely by Vision Zero.”

### 11. Current gap to ambition

**Claim.** Sweden has not achieved zero deaths. Its 2030 interim target is no more than 133 road deaths, and its protected-road indicators show implementation gaps.

- Primary: Swedish Transport Administration, January 2025 update; 2030 target of 133.
- Primary: *Analysis of Road Safety Trends 2022*, pp. 22–25: the 90–120 km/h protected-traffic indicator was not on track; 8,870 km of 90/100 km/h road remained without median barriers at end-2022.
- Confidence: **high**
- Interpretation: Vision Zero is a disciplined direction of travel, not a completed success story.

### 12. Transferability warning

**Claim.** Copying the label without system redesign, political commitment, funding, cross-agency delivery and measurable interventions does not reproduce Swedish outcomes.

- Independent: OECD/ITF 2022, pp. 13–14, on governance, shared responsibility, investment and leading indicators.
- Independent journalism/data analysis: *Washington Post*, 2025, found many US city Vision Zero programmes did not reduce pedestrian death rates where implementation, funding and political support were weak.  
  https://www.washingtonpost.com/business/interactive/2025/pedestrian-deaths-vision-zero-roads/
- Academic: Bragge et al., “Characteristics of successful government-led interventions to support healthier populations,” *BMJ Global Health*, 2023; PubMed PMID 37225262.  
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37225262/
- Confidence: **medium-high**
- Limit: Cross-country implementation environments differ; this is a transfer condition, not a direct causal test of Sweden.

## Intent–capacity–implementation–result separation

| Layer | Verified statement | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Declared intent | No deaths or lifelong/serious injuries | Adopted; not achieved |
| Design capacity | Human-tolerance design rules, shared responsibility, staged targets, leading indicators, STRADA and in-depth studies | Established |
| Actual implementation | 2+1 median barriers, cameras, speed management, roundabouts, alcolocks, annual review and stakeholder action plans | Documented at scale |
| Evaluated intervention result | 50% reduction in killed/seriously injured on evaluated narrow 2+1 sections; about 16 fewer serious injuries annually on evaluated speed-limit reduction | Supported, intervention-specific |
| National achieved result | 213 road deaths in 2024; 21.1% below 2014 OECD count | Verified outcome |
| Future target | No more than 133 deaths in 2030; ultimate zero | Not yet achieved |

## Implementation chain

1. **Desired future:** zero fatal or lifelong injuries.
2. **Operating-model rule:** system designers share responsibility and design for human error/tolerance.
3. **Capabilities:** data integration, in-depth investigation, safe-road engineering, vehicle standards, speed enforcement and cross-sector coordination.
4. **Pilot/testing:** first median-barrier pilot in 1998; later evaluations of narrow 2+1 configurations and speed changes.
5. **Indicators/baseline:** deaths, serious injuries, sober traffic, speed compliance and traffic share on high-safety roads.
6. **Learning/adjustment:** missed 2007 target and independent reviews triggered management-by-objectives redesign.
7. **Scale:** thousands of kilometres of median protection, national camera and speed-management deployment.
8. **Result:** lower fatality counts and independently evaluated intervention effects, while the zero-harm vision remains unmet.

## Final evidence decision

**PASS.** The evidence supports a substantive institutional transformation, implementation at scale, measured outcomes and transferable operating lessons. Publication must preserve these restrictions:

- never claim Vision Zero alone caused Sweden's national decline;
- identify 213 as the finalised 2024 fatality count;
- keep intervention effects tied to the evaluated road sections;
- state that zero has not been achieved;
- distinguish protected-road inventory from 2+1-road kilometres;
- retain the failed 2007 target and remaining infrastructure gaps as part of the analysis.
