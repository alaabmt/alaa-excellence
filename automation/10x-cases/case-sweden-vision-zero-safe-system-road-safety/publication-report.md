- English title: How Sweden Shifted Road Safety from Correcting Driver Error to Designing for Human Error

## Why selected

The case documents a genuine operating-model transformation: road safety shifted from relying mainly on correcting user behaviour to designing roads, vehicles, speeds, data and accountability around predictable human error. The Evidence Pack established implementation at scale, evaluated intervention results, a national outcome trend, remaining gaps and transferable management lessons.

## Evidence confidence

- Policy architecture: high
- Implementation: high
- 2+1-road and speed-intervention effects: high
- National fatality counts: high
- Attribution of the national trend to Vision Zero alone: medium and explicitly rejected

Key verified outcomes:
- Parliament adopted Vision Zero in 1997.
- OECD/ITF records 270 road deaths in 2014 and 213 in 2024, a 21.1% decline.
- A controlled evaluation of selected narrow 2+1 conversions found 50% fewer fatalities and serious injuries overall and 63% fewer on road links.
- At end-2022, Sweden had 5,620 km of national roads with median barriers; implementation gaps remained.
- Zero has not been achieved; the 2030 interim ceiling is 133 deaths.
