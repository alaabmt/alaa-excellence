# Company Insider Critique — Sweden Vision Zero

**Nature of review:** simulated institutional insider challenge, not testimony or approval from the Swedish Transport Administration or any Swedish entity.

## Overall result

**PASS WITH REQUIRED REVISIONS.** The draft captures the system transformation and avoids claiming that Vision Zero alone caused the national decline. Five revisions are required before the English draft and final gate.

## Challenge 1 — The title creates a false binary

“من لوم السائق” suggests Sweden previously did nothing but blame drivers. Sweden already had a long history of systematic road-safety work before 1997. The verified change was a stronger reallocation of responsibility and a Safe System design logic.

**Required revision:** change the title to “كيف نقلت السويد سلامة الطرق من تصحيح خطأ السائق إلى تصميم نظام يتسامح مع الخطأ؟” and use the equivalent English formulation.

## Challenge 2 — Finalised 2024 count needs cleaner source handling

The draft correctly chooses 213 from final OECD/ITF reporting, but reference [2] contains a preliminary 210 count. Readers should not have to infer why the citation disagrees.

**Required revision:** state once, in a concise note, that the January 2025 Swedish release was preliminary and later official/international reporting settled at 213. Add the 2026 Swedish Transport Administration source that explicitly compares 2025 with 213 in 2024.

## Challenge 3 — Shared responsibility needs operational precision

The draft says system designers and operators share responsibility, but does not state the governing consequence: when users fail to follow rules, designers must take further steps to prevent death or serious injury. Without this, “shared responsibility” can sound like diffuse accountability.

**Required revision:** add one sentence explaining that user responsibility does not discharge system designers from corrective action.

## Challenge 4 — The 4% traffic-volume figure is institution-reported

The exposure comparison strengthens the trend, but it is not independently reproduced in the Evidence Pack. It should remain attributed to the Swedish Transport Administration.

**Required revision:** retain explicit attribution and avoid using it as proof of causal impact.

## Challenge 5 — 2+1 evaluation scope must stay visible

The draft appropriately confines 50% and 63% to evaluated narrow-road sections. The wording “ثم اتسع التطبيق” risks making the study sound representative of the entire scaled network.

**Required revision:** state that the evaluation covered selected narrow-road conversions and that network-wide benefit is directionally consistent but not measured by that study.

## Additional strengths to retain

- The failed 2007 target is included and used to explain governance redesign.
- The draft distinguishes intent, implementation and result.
- The current gap to the 2030 target is visible.
- The eight official pillars are all evidence-linked.
- STRADA is explained at first use.
- The official Trafikverket video is suitable and positioned correctly.
- The mandatory Arabic disclaimer is exact.
- Numerical claims use inline reference markers.

## Required decision

Revise the Arabic draft, then use the revised version—not the initial draft—as the factual and analytical source for the English edition.
