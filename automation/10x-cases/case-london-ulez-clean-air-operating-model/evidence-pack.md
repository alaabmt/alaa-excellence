# Evidence Pack — London ULEZ

## Decision
**PASS for full analysis, subject to preserving the official/independent evidence tension.**

Case: London's Ultra Low Emission Zone (ULEZ) as an operating-model redesign for vehicle emissions, not merely a road charge.

## Scope and definitions

- **ULEZ — Ultra Low Emission Zone — منطقة الانبعاثات شديدة الانخفاض:** an area in which cars, motorcycles, vans, specialist vehicles and minibuses that do not meet defined emission standards must pay a daily charge to drive.
- **ANPR — Automatic Number Plate Recognition — التعرّف الآلي إلى لوحات المركبات:** cameras record vehicle plates; TfL cross-references anonymized detections with Driver and Vehicle Licensing Agency (DVLA) records on type, age, Euro standard and emissions.
- **NO2 — nitrogen dioxide — ثاني أكسيد النيتروجين:** a harmful gas strongly associated with road-traffic combustion and used as a central air-quality measure in this case.
- **NOx — nitrogen oxides — أكاسيد النيتروجين:** a broader family including nitric oxide and nitrogen dioxide.
- **FEV1 — forced expiratory volume in one second — حجم الزفير القسري في ثانية واحدة:** the amount of air a person can forcefully exhale in one second, used to assess lung function.

## Four-layer separation

### 1. Stated target
Improve London's air quality and public health by accelerating the shift away from the most polluting road vehicles. This is a public-policy objective, not itself an achieved result.

### 2. Designed operating capacity
- fixed vehicle-emission standards rather than a general traffic ban;
- 24/7 geographic rule across a defined zone, excluding Christmas Day;
- £12.50 daily charge for subject, non-compliant vehicles;
- ANPR detection linked to DVLA vehicle records;
- exemptions, registration rules, payment and penalty processes;
- published compliance monitoring;
- scrappage/donation/retrofit grants and public-transport offers;
- staged geographic expansion and periodic evaluation.

### 3. Verified execution
- Central London ULEZ launched 8 April 2019.
- Expanded to inner London in October 2021.
- Expanded across all London boroughs on 29 August 2023, excluding the M25.
- TfL continues to operate the charge, camera/record-matching, exemptions, payment and enforcement system.
- The London-wide scrappage scheme ran from 30 January 2023 to 8 September 2024 and changed funding and eligibility during operation.

### 4. Observed/modelled outcomes
- TfL reported overall vehicle compliance of 96.7% in September 2024, up from 91.6% in June 2023 before London-wide expansion. This is institution-reported operational evidence.
- For outer-London cars specifically, TfL reports 97.4% compliance in September 2024 (1.820 million compliant of 1.868 million detected on an average day). This is a different denominator from the overall compliance figure.
- TfL/GLA's one-year model estimates roadside NO2 in 2024 was 27% lower across London than a scenario without ULEZ and its expansions. This is a counterfactual model estimate, not a directly observed 27% before/after fall and not independent.
- An independent 2025 causal analysis estimated that the 2019 central ULEZ reduced NO2 at central-London traffic sites by 19.6% during the first three months and urban-background NO2 by 8.2%; it found no statistically detectable incremental NO2 effect from the 2023 expansion.
- A 2026 prospective London–Luton cohort found that children's NO2 exposure fell more than twice as much and twice as fast in London; London children's lung-growth trajectories improved until lung function was comparable with Luton. The design supports a meaningful association, but it is not randomized, overlapped the pandemic and experienced substantial attrition.
- A 2026 interrupted-time-series study found adult emergency admissions trends declined after the T-charge/central ULEZ sequence; adjusted reductions remained for cardiovascular and all-cause admissions, but not respiratory admissions. The short ULEZ period prevented separation of ULEZ from the earlier T-charge.

## Implementation chain

### Desired future
A city where exposure to harmful road-traffic emissions falls because the most polluting vehicles no longer circulate routinely, while residents retain mobility choices.

### Operating-model design
A rule is attached to measurable vehicle characteristics rather than to the driver's stated intention. Each detected plate is checked against vehicle records; compliant vehicles continue without charge, while non-compliant vehicles face a daily price or must change, retrofit, avoid the zone or qualify for an exemption.

### Capability and infrastructure
- legal scheme orders and mayoral/TfL governance;
- emissions-standard definitions;
- ANPR camera network;
- DVLA data linkage and back-office matching;
- payment, exemption, appeal and enforcement processes;
- monitoring network and counterfactual modelling;
- scrappage grants and alternative transport support;
- public communication and vehicle checker.

### Testing / staged rollout
The public record shows phased implementation—central 2019, inner 2021, London-wide 2023—supported by consultations, impact assessments and monitoring reports. It should be described as staged rollout/expansion, not as a single undocumented pilot. No evidence supports calling the entire 2019 launch a pilot.

### Measurement
- compliance rate based on camera detections and vehicle records;
- detected non-compliant vehicles;
- emissions and pollution concentrations;
- counterfactual estimates;
- cohort lung function and pollution exposure;
- hospital-admission trends.

### Learning and adaptation
- expansion after earlier phases;
- changes to grant values and eligibility;
- additional funding;
- vehicle-donation option;
- continued monitoring;
- evidence that behavior shifted before the London-wide start, creating a smaller post-start margin.

### Scale
From central London to inner London, then all boroughs. Scale depended on law, cameras, record matching, payment/enforcement operations and support—not on signage alone.

## Causal interpretation

### Supported
- The 2019 central ULEZ materially affected vehicle compliance and traffic-related nitrogen pollutants.
- The system changed incentives and fleet composition.
- Children's pollution exposure and lung-growth trajectories improved in London relative to Luton during the study period.
- Adult admissions trends shifted after the combined T-charge/ULEZ sequence.

### Not supported
- That the 2023 expansion independently caused the full official 27% London-wide NO2 estimate.
- That ULEZ alone caused all health improvements.
- That the 2023 expansion had zero effect because one independent study found no detectable incremental effect.
- That the policy eliminated congestion, all air pollution or distributional burden.
- That scrappage grants fully neutralized affordability effects.

## Confidence summary
- Policy chronology: **high**
- Operating mechanism: **high**
- Compliance data: **high**, with denominator caveats
- Official 27% counterfactual estimate: **medium-high**
- Independent 2019 air-quality effect: **high**
- Incremental 2023 effect: **high confidence in “not statistically detectable” finding; low confidence in any claim of zero effect**
- Children's health association: **medium-high**
- Adult admissions association: **medium-high**
- Equity and full economic impact: **medium/insufficient for broad conclusions**
- Congestion effect: **insufficient for causal conclusion**

## Acceptance rationale
The evidence is sufficient because it documents the problem, operating redesign, staged implementation, adaptation, scale, operational outputs, independent effects and material limitations. The case remains publishable only if the article keeps the official model, independent causal estimates and health associations separate.
