Shortlisted the Dutch Room for the River programme because it shows a documented shift from repeatedly constraining rivers primarily with higher/stronger dikes toward a portfolio that gives rivers more discharge and storage space while treating spatial quality as a co-objective.

## Why this candidate clears the initial threshold

1. **Strategic challenge:** near-floods/high water in 1993 and 1995 exposed the limits of a predominantly defence-based approach; climate change raised the prospect of larger winter discharges.
2. **Value redefinition:** the programme paired flood safety with spatial quality instead of optimizing hydraulic protection alone.
3. **Operating-model change:** a national programme combined centralized goals, budget and assurance with decentralized design and delivery by regional and local authorities.
4. **Documented execution:** Rijkswaterstaat reports measures at 34 locations, a €2.3 billion cost, and official completion in early 2019.
5. **Measurable project result:** the European Climate-ADAPT case reports that Nijmegen achieved a 35 cm design-water-level reduction versus a 27 cm target; its new side channel carries roughly one-third of flow during high water.
6. **Independent institutional evidence:** peer-reviewed studies document the programme's adaptive multi-level governance, programme-management performance and contribution to a broader transition toward integrated flood-risk management.
7. **Transferable lesson:** transformation depended on a governed portfolio, local option-space, hydraulic models, design-quality assurance and participation—not on copying a single engineering intervention.

## Initial cautions

- The 16,000 m³/s figure is a **design discharge capacity/standard**, not proof that the full system has been empirically tested at that discharge.
- “Around 4 million people” is an official scope/beneficiary statement, not a causal impact estimate.
- Programme completion is not equivalent to proof that floods were prevented.
- Meuse evidence from the 2021 flood concerns the wider Dutch room-for-rivers approach and related Meuse works; it will not be presented as direct empirical proof of the Lower Rhine programme.
- STOWA records an important knowledge gap: system-wide change in flood risk (probability × consequence) had not been properly quantified in the cited evaluation.
- Land acquisition, relocation and opportunity costs are material trade-offs; the official programme page explicitly notes that people and businesses may have to move.
- The 2026 ecosystem-service valuation study models strategies and benefits; it is not a retrospective causal evaluation of programme outcomes.
