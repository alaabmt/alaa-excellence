# Candidate Notes — India UPI as Interoperable Payment Infrastructure

**Cycle date:** 2026-10-22 (Asia/Dubai)  
**Proposed slug:** `case-india-upi-interoperable-payments-infrastructure`  
**Decision:** Advance to Evidence Pack

## Scan

### UAE — digital government and zero bureaucracy

Current official sources document extensive redesign, shared enablers and targets. The strongest recent examples still combine verified implementation with forward targets or self-reported programme results that need more independent outcome evidence. This remains a future candidate, not today’s selection.

### Region — Saudi Ada’a performance-management network

A recent peer-reviewed overview reports expansion from 33 hospitals and 10 indicators in 2017–2018 to a much wider health-system network by 2025. It is promising, but the article is partly authored from within the programme and needs a stronger independent outcomes layer before publication.

### Global — Republic of Korea food-waste pricing

The policy mix, pay-as-you-throw model and RFID implementation are documented by OECD, the World Bank, ADB and recent research. It is a viable future case. It was not selected today because the most transferable institutional mechanism and current national outcome series require more reconciliation across waste streams and local-government implementations.

### Selected — India’s Unified Payments Interface (UPI)

UPI is not simply a popular payment app. It is a common instant-payment layer created by the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI), promoted by the banking system under Reserve Bank of India guidance, that allows banks and third-party applications to exchange payment messages across providers.

## Why it qualifies

- **Strategic need:** India’s retail payments were fragmented and cash-heavy; closed networks restricted reach and user choice.
- **Value/model shift:** competition moved from owning a closed payment network to building services over a shared interoperable rail.
- **Implementation:** NPCI launched UPI in April 2016 over existing Immediate Payment Service infrastructure, with banks, third-party apps, identifiers and QR acceptance added to the operating model.
- **Verified scale:** NPCI’s current statistics list 756 live banks and 24.068 billion transactions in September 2026. RBI reports 228.28 billion UPI transactions in calendar 2025, worth ₹300 lakh crore.
- **Independent analytical evidence:** IMF research using granular UPI transaction data finds evidence consistent with interoperability supporting adoption; BIS analyses the public-infrastructure and competition design.
- **Transferable lesson:** an institution can standardise the shared transaction layer while leaving customer experience and service innovation contestable.
- **Limits worth analyzing:** scale is not identical to financial inclusion; concentration among front-end apps, fraud, outages, zero-price sustainability and dependence on bank accounts remain material.

## Duplicate check

No UPI case or equivalent India instant-payments article appears in the Arabic or English article indexes, the publication-date registry, or repository search.

## Evidence thresholds for continuation

The Evidence Pack must:
1. verify launch chronology and operating roles from NPCI/RBI;
2. separate design intent, network capacity, actual transactions and any future targets;
3. use a dated monthly statistic and a comparable annual statistic without mixing units;
4. distinguish UPI adoption from broader gains caused by Aadhaar, bank-account programmes, smartphones or demonetisation;
5. document market concentration, operational resilience and fraud/sustainability limits;
6. avoid claiming that UPI alone caused financial inclusion or economic growth.
