## Structural parity

Both versions use the same decision sequence:

1. executive snapshot;
2. strategic problem and root constraints;
3. future state;
4. operating capability and implementation;
5. verified outcomes;
6. evidence limits and risks;
7. eight official 10X pillars;
8. transferable actions;
9. future-facing question;
10. editorial disclaimer;
11. references.

The English text is an idiomatic editorial equivalent rather than a sentence-by-sentence translation. No conclusion, caveat or material number appears in only one language.

## Terminology review

- UPI is expanded and explained at first use in each language.
- NPCI and RBI roles are described without implying endorsement.
- Interoperability is explained in plain language.
- QR is expanded in Arabic and familiar usage is retained in English.
- “Open” is constrained to interoperable participation under rules.
- “Public good” is not used as an ownership or pricing claim.

## Caveat parity

Both languages explicitly reject:
- single-cause attribution;
- inclusion and welfare inference from transaction scale;
- the idea that back-end interoperability prevents front-end concentration;
- an assertion of costless operation;
- unverified culture, AI, fraud or reliability claims;
- a claim that performance improved uniformly by tenfold.

## Decision

**PASS.** The pair is materially equivalent in evidence, numbers, causal interpretation, risks, official-pillar judgments, practical lessons and source set. No further bilingual correction is required before the final quality gate.
