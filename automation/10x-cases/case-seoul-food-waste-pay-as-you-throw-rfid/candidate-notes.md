# Candidate scan — 24 October 2026

## Decision

**Selected for evidence validation:** Seoul / Republic of Korea — the shift from flat waste charges and landfill disposal to source separation, weight-based charging and resource recovery for food waste.

Provisional slug: `case-seoul-food-waste-pay-as-you-throw-rfid`.

The case is not being accepted because it is famous or because RFID is novel. It proceeds because the available record shows a multi-decade operating-model change: a nationwide volume-based fee in 1995; mandatory separation and a landfill ban for food waste; later use of household-level weight charging through radio-frequency identification (RFID); local processing infrastructure; and measurable waste-reduction and recycling outcomes. The evidence pack must still test definitions, baselines, attribution and the difference between collection for recycling and final beneficial use.

## Why the selected case may meet the 10X threshold

- **Strategic problem:** landfill capacity, odour and leachate made mixed disposal increasingly untenable.
- **Value redesign:** payment moved from a flat charge toward “pay for what you discard,” while separated recyclables were collected without the same charge.
- **Operating-model redesign:** households separate food waste; bags, chips or RFID bins record disposal; municipalities collect; dedicated facilities dewater and recover feed, compost or biogas.
- **Implementation path:** policy changes unfolded over decades rather than through a single technology launch.
- **Documented results to verify:** Seoul food waste reportedly fell 23.9% from 3,181 tonnes/day in 2013 to 2,419 tonnes/day in 2023; a quasi-experimental study found substantially larger reductions in treated apartment complexes; national official figures reported 96.8% of 4.81 million tonnes collected/recycled in 2023.
- **Transferable insight:** pricing, visibility, rules, citizen behaviour and processing capacity have to work as one system; copying the bin alone would miss the transformation.
- **Material limitations already visible:** official recycling figures may describe material entering designated recovery routes rather than independently verified final use; moisture reduction can change measured weight; local funding, corrosion and coverage gaps constrain expansion; recycling does not eliminate overproduction of food waste.
