# Company Insider Critique — simulated review

**Status:** Passed with required revisions  
**Perspective simulated:** senior municipal waste-system leader responsible for policy, billing, collection and treatment  
**Nature of review:** evidence-based simulation; not testimony from Seoul Metropolitan Government, Korea Environment Corporation or any implementing team

## Main challenges to the draft

1. **The article could still be read as an RFID success story.**  
   Internal teams would stress that the bin depends on national separation rules, municipal billing, collection routes, facility permits and recovery markets. The draft generally makes this clear, but every outcome statement must keep the system—not the device—as the causal unit.

2. **The city and national scopes must remain separate.**  
   The 23.9% decline is a Seoul measure. The 96.8% recycling rate and 4.81 million tonnes are national. The draft separates them; this distinction is non-negotiable in cards, metadata and both languages.

3. **Coverage denominators can mislead.**  
   81.6% refers to apartment residents; 37.9% covers all residences. These cannot be compared as if they measure the same population. The draft explicitly explains the denominator difference.

4. **The phrase “recycled” is administratively stronger than the known final-use evidence.**  
   A municipal operator would distinguish collected material, material sent to a process, output meeting safety specifications and output sold or used. Public sources do not verify every final tonne. The limitation is present and must remain in the executive summary and evidence section.

5. **Moisture creates a measurement ambiguity.**  
   Draining water can reduce recorded weight without preventing edible food waste. The draft identifies this, which should be retained as a core analytical limit.

6. **The pilot-to-scale story is incomplete.**  
   Published comparative studies show staged adoption and stronger household-level incentives, but no public citywide gate document describes pilot duration, formal acceptance criteria and the sequence of executive decisions. This gap should be stated more explicitly.

7. **Human capability is broader than citizen compliance.**  
   The sources describe public education and civil-society mediation, but provide limited detail on training for maintenance crews, data analysts, billing staff and plant operators. The article should not infer a documented workforce program.

8. **Cost and equity are under-evidenced.**  
   The fee level is reported, but no transparent total-cost, distributional or low-income burden analysis was found. The article correctly avoids savings and fairness claims; transferability guidance should pose equity as a design question, not declare it solved.

9. **Formal foresight is not evidenced.**  
   The historical change was largely a response to landfill and leachate pressure. The article correctly treats pillar 1 as a gap and the 2030 ambition as a target.

10. **Video provenance.**  
    A sponsored BBC StoryWorks/Seoul item appears in city material, but no suitable, verified and embeddable YouTube source was found. Publishing without video is safer than embedding loosely related or promotional material.

## Revisions made

- Reframed the pillar paragraph to state that seven of eight pillars are supported and pillar 1 remains an evidence gap.
- Added an explicit statement that the public record does not disclose a complete citywide pilot protocol or formal scale gates.
- Preserved the separation between device function and system outcomes.
- Preserved the difference between Seoul and national statistics.
- Preserved the difference between achieved results and the 2030 target.
- Preserved the moisture, final-use, finance, maintenance and output-quality limits.
- Kept cost savings, emissions reductions and equity outcomes out of the article because evidence was insufficient.

## Remaining unverifiable gaps

- Total capital and operating cost of the complete Seoul system.
- Distribution of household fee burden by income.
- Full internal governance and decision-rights map.
- Citywide pilot duration and formal scale criteria.
- Training architecture for municipal and facility staff.
- Independent audit of final beneficial use for the national recycling percentage.
- Case-specific net greenhouse-gas effect.
- Exact causal contribution of RFID to the 2013–2023 citywide decline.

## Verdict

**PASS WITH REVISIONS COMPLETED.** The case may advance to the English draft and parity review. Any later cover or metadata must avoid presenting RFID as the sole cause, and must not turn the 96.8% administrative recycling figure into proof that 96.8% became high-value output.
