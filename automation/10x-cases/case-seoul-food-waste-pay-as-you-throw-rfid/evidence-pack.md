# Evidence Pack — Seoul's food-waste pay-as-you-throw system

**Evidence decision:** PASS with explicit limitations  
**Case scope:** Seoul as the narrative center, inside the Republic of Korea's national regulatory and recovery system  
**Checked:** 24 October 2026

## Executive evidence judgment

The public record is strong enough to support a 10X case about the *system redesign*, not a technology-success story about RFID alone. The evidence establishes a long sequence of policy, pricing, separation, data and processing changes. It also supports cautious claims that weight-based charging changes household incentives and that Seoul's food-waste tonnage has fallen since citywide rollout.

The evidence does **not** support attributing every national recycling outcome to RFID, treating the reported recycling rate as proof of high-value final use, or claiming that recycling has solved food overproduction. Those limits must remain visible in both editions.

## Distinguishing four kinds of evidence

### 1. Announced intent / target

- Seoul announced a target to reduce food waste by 20% by 2030 against a 2019 baseline.
- The city also announced expansion of pay-as-you-throw RFID equipment; the precise coverage target changed across public reporting and should be stated only with a dated source.
- **Status:** target, not achieved result.
- Primary source: Seoul Metropolitan Government, 11 February 2022: https://english.seoul.go.kr/seoul-targets-20-lower-food-waste-by-2030/

### 2. Design capacity

- RFID units identify the household, weigh deposited food waste and charge by weight.
- The Korea Environment Corporation describes the method as an automated measurement and billing system; this verifies function, not impact.
- **Status:** designed capability.
- Primary source: Korea Environment Corporation: https://www.keco.or.kr/en/lay1/S295T387C404/contents.do

### 3. Implemented operation

- Seoul uses multiple charging methods: designated bags, chips/stickers and household-level RFID weight charging.
- Independent field reporting counted 27,289 RFID units, serving 81.6% of apartment residents; coverage across all housing types was 37.9%. Nationally, 150,738 units served 8.54 million apartment households in 186 of 229 municipalities.
- At Gangdong's resource-circulation center, collected material is shredded, contaminants are removed, water is pressed out, liquid goes to anaerobic digestion and solids are dried and processed as feed.
- **Status:** observed implementation; installation and coverage do not by themselves prove reduction.
- Sources: https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2025/dec/18/smart-bins-measuring-food-waste-south-korea and https://www.keco.or.kr/doc/K-eco_2025_sustainability_report_en_short.pdf

### 4. Achieved results

- Seoul food-waste tonnage fell 23.9% from 3,181 tonnes/day in 2013 to 2,419 tonnes/day in 2023, according to ministry data reported independently.
- Seoul's official SDG case states that RFID bins reduced 47,000 tonnes over six years and that observed reductions varied by method: 13% for standard bags, 14% for chips and 10%–30% for RFID weight charging. The source does not provide enough methodological detail on that page to treat the 47,000-tonne figure as independently causal.
- A 2017 natural-experiment study compared 12 Mapo-gu apartment complexes using household-level RFID weight charging with 61 control complexes and concluded that the design reduced waste more effectively than community-level charging.
- A 2026 household survey study covering 2019–2022 found weight-based PAYT more effective than volume-based systems and linked the effect to stronger incentives and food-preservation practices. It is supporting evidence on mechanism, not a causal evaluation of the whole Seoul system.
- National official figures reported through independent field reporting put 2023 food waste at 4.81 million tonnes and the recycling rate at 96.8%. This is a national measure and must never be presented as a Seoul-only or RFID-only outcome.

## Implementation chain

**Problem pressure:** landfill scarcity, odour, contamination and leachate.

**Value rule:** the generator, rather than the whole community through a flat charge, pays in closer proportion to discarded quantity.

**Operating design:** separation at source → measured/bagged disposal → municipal collection → contaminant removal and dewatering → feed/compost/biogas routes.

**Build sequence:**

1. 1995 national volume-based fee for general waste.
2. 2005 landfill ban for food waste and mandatory separation.
3. Early 2010s introduction and wider use of household-level RFID weight charging.
4. 2013 land-based handling pressure after the leachate ocean-dumping ban.
5. Continued expansion, maintenance and refinement at municipal level.

**Pilot / test evidence:** published sources describe staged local adoption and comparative apartment-complex evidence, but no single citywide pilot protocol, duration and formal scale-gate document was found. This is a material limitation, not a reason to invent a pilot.

**Learning and modification:** bags helped price waste but created inconvenience and contamination; weight charging increased visibility and allowed small, frequent deposits. Processing also evolved from disposal toward recovery routes. Public evidence is clearer on what changed than on formal internal decision gates.

## Causality rule for the article

Safe:

- “Food waste fell after the system was introduced and expanded.”
- “Household-level weight charging was associated with stronger reduction in studied complexes.”
- “The redesign combined rules, prices, visibility and processing capacity.”
- “Official figures classify 96.8% as recycled.”

Unsafe:

- “RFID alone cut Seoul's waste by 23.9%.”
- “South Korea eliminated food waste.”
- “Ninety-six point eight percent became useful product.”
- “The program reduced emissions by X” without a case-specific life-cycle assessment.
- “The system paid for itself” without transparent cost evidence.

## Confidence summary

- Policy chronology: **high**
- RFID operating function: **high**
- Seoul tonnage trend: **high**
- RFID-specific causal effect: **medium-high in studied apartment settings; medium citywide**
- National recycling statistic: **high as a reported administrative figure; medium as evidence of final beneficial use**
- Processing outputs: **medium-high**
- Costs and net environmental benefit: **low-to-medium; insufficient for strong claims**
- Transferability: **medium**, conditional on enforcement, housing form, billing, processing markets and municipal capacity

## Evidence gate

**PASS.** The case has a clear strategic problem, a documented redesign of value and operations, implemented capability, measurable results, academic support and meaningful limitations. Publication is allowed only if the article keeps Seoul and national scopes separate, treats the 2030 reduction as a target, attributes the 47,000-tonne figure, and makes clear that prevention is not the same as recycling.
