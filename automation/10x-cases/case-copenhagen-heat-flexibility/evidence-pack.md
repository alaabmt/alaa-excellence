# Evidence pack — Copenhagen heat flexibility
Date: 2026-11-25. Candidate only, not publication approval.
- Municipality final project summary: https://cphsolutionslab.kk.dk/en/news/flexible-district-heating-great-opportunities-for-the-future?language=en — 150 buildings, approximately one million square metres, 22 large owners plus private homes, 6–8 MW activatable heat flexibility. High confidence in reported scope; not independently audited delivered savings. MW is power, not energy.
- Danfoss/HOFOR contribution, 2024 district energy white paper, p.24: https://dbdh.dk/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/SoG_WhitePaper_DistrictEnergy2024_210x297_V09.pdf — 120 municipal buildings during 2021–22 and 2022–23 heating seasons; reported average morning building-level peak reduction 13.2%, heating consumption reduction 5–7% attributed to predictive solar compensation. Medium confidence; participant report, no randomized counterfactual established. Do not extrapolate to citywide or later cohort.
- HOFOR authors, 21 Oct 2025: https://dbdh.org/from-consumer-to-flexumer/amp/ — voluntary agreements, aggregator, 140 buildings and 20 owners at earlier reporting point; not inconsistent with later scope. Legal and commercial barriers remain.
- Climate KIC pilot account: https://www.climate-kic.org/news/copenhagen-heat-flexibility-drive-for-carbon-positivity/ — governance and predictive weather control; programme-associated source, not independent impact evaluation.
- Exclude monetary savings and emissions claims unless separately validated; no proof of avoided construction or universal comfort.
## 10X analysis
Future: align heat use with available supply without degrading service. Work backwards to building controls, consent, aggregation, dispatch and measurement. Evidence supports strategic direction, value reframing, cooperation, capabilities, trial, predictive intelligence and reported results. Business model change is exploratory, not a proven tariff model.
