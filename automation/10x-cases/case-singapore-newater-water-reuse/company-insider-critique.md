# Company Insider Critique — Singapore NEWater

**Simulation only:** This review models the questions a senior PUB programme or operations leader might raise. It is not testimony from PUB or any named person.

## 1. Do not reduce NEWater to a membrane story
**Challenge:** The draft largely avoids this error, but the purification sequence may still dominate attention. NEWater depended on the upstream used-water system, source-discharge controls, laboratories, a dedicated distribution network, industrial customers, reservoir operations and waterworks.

**Action:** Keep the process explanation short and retain the operating-model section as the analytical center.

## 2. “Independent” overstates the expert panel’s institutional distance
**Challenge:** The panel included local and international multidisciplinary experts, but public sources reviewed here do not establish institutional independence in the audit sense.

**Action:** Use “multidisciplinary international expert panel” or “external expert review,” not “independent panel,” unless a source establishes independence.

## 3. Separate plant count, supply share and design capacity
**Challenge:** Historical sources list different plant counts. PUB’s current page says four plants; earlier reports listed five before Bedok closed. “Up to 40%” is a capacity statement, while OECD’s 30% is a 2022 supplied-share statement.

**Action:** Retain explicit dates and do not put 30% and 40% in the same metric card without explaining the different definitions.

## 4. Do not attribute national water security to NEWater alone
**Challenge:** PUB manages an integrated system: catchment, imports, NEWater, desalination, demand management, pricing and drainage/used-water infrastructure. A programme insider would reject language suggesting one tap created resilience.

**Action:** State that NEWater added a material weather-resilient source within the Four National Taps.

## 5. Public acceptance is not a solved variable
**Challenge:** The public launch, naming, visitor centre and communication are documented. Their separate causal effect is not quantified, and continued outreach shows legitimacy requires maintenance.

**Action:** Present acceptance work as a sustained institutional capability. Avoid claiming that one event or brand caused acceptance.

## 6. “Designing industrial demand” needs qualification
**Challenge:** Wafer fabrication created a strong use case, and OECD reports pricing encouraged industry to switch. The public evidence does not fully reconstruct how customer contracts, network sequencing and plant investment decisions were ordered.

**Action:** Say PUB connected scale to a high-value industrial demand path; do not claim a fully documented demand-design chronology.

## 7. Testing supports assurance, not zero risk
**Challenge:** Testing and expert review supported indirect potable use. A technical leader would object to any wording that implies permanent or absolute safety independent of continued controls.

**Action:** Emphasize continuous monitoring, multiple barriers and source control. Preserve that assurance depends on ongoing operation.

## 8. Energy comparison is dated and bounded
**Challenge:** OECD’s “about one quarter of desalination” estimate is useful but from 2016 and depends on plant design, feed water, pressure, recovery and system boundaries.

**Action:** Keep it only as a dated OECD estimate in the evidence-limits section; do not use it as a headline metric.

## 9. The case understates upstream infrastructure
**Challenge:** Closing the loop depends on sewerage and water-reclamation capacity, including long-lived infrastructure beyond the NEWater factories themselves.

**Action:** Add upstream collection and source control explicitly to the “transfer conditions” and operating-model description.

## 10. Current economics and environmental residuals remain opaque
**Challenge:** Public sources reviewed do not provide a current full cost breakdown, plant-by-plant energy use, membrane replacement rate or concentrate-management profile.

**Action:** Preserve these as open evidence gaps; do not describe the programme as costless or universally superior.

## Revisions required before publication
- Replace “independent expert review” with “multidisciplinary international/external expert review.”
- Keep 30% (2022 supplied share) as the principal outcome and treat 40% only as historical “up to” capacity.
- Add a visible critique/limits section to both language versions.
- State that separating industrial and indirect-potable uses is an analytical interpretation of risk and adoption design, not a published causal finding.
- Keep the Four National Taps attribution limit in the Executive Summary and evidence section.
- Add sewerage/source controls to transferability.
- Do not use the 2016 energy ratio in social imagery or headline metadata.

## Critique result

**PASS WITH REVISIONS.** The core story is accurate and analytically valuable. Publication is conditional on the revisions above and bilingual parity.
