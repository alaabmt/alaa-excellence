# Evidence Pack — Brazil Family Health Strategy

- **Case:** Brazil's Family Health Strategy (Estratégia Saúde da Família, ESF)
- **Evidence review date:** 2026-11-12
- **Decision:** PASS for analysis, with explicit causal, measurement, and equity limits
- **Overall confidence:** High for operating-model redesign and national implementation; medium-high for contribution to selected mortality outcomes; mixed/insufficient for avoidable hospitalization and uniform quality

## Evidence classification

| Dimension | What the evidence supports | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Declared objective | Expand universal, equitable, continuous primary care, with priority for vulnerable and remote populations | Official objective, not itself an outcome |
| Designed capacity | Multidisciplinary teams assigned to a defined territory and registered population; home visits, prevention, diagnosis, treatment, rehabilitation, surveillance, and care coordination | High-confidence design rule |
| Actual implementation | National rollout since 1994; tens of thousands of teams and hundreds of thousands of community health workers operating in 2024 | High confidence; totals vary by date/definition |
| Observed outcomes | Multiple longitudinal and quasi-experimental studies associate coverage with lower infant, amenable, and cardiovascular/cerebrovascular mortality | Medium-high contribution evidence, not sole causation |
| Contested/unsupported outcome | A national panel study did not find lower avoidable hospitalizations; systematic review found no clear pattern | Strong counterevidence; do not claim general hospitalization reduction |
| Equity and quality | Coverage has been progressive toward poorer households, but geography, staffing, infrastructure, and integration remain uneven | Mixed |

## Claim-to-source ledger

### E1 — The transformation changed the operating model, not only service volume

**Claim.** The Family Health Strategy reorganizes primary care around multidisciplinary teams responsible for defined territories and populations. Teams provide individual, family, and collective care across prevention, diagnosis, treatment, rehabilitation, palliative care, and health surveillance.

- **Primary source:** Brazil Ministry of Health, “Estratégia Saúde da Família” and “Equipe de Saúde da Família”
  - https://www.gov.br/saude/pt-br/composicao/saps/esf
  - https://www.gov.br/saude/pt-br/composicao/saps/esf/equipe-saude-da-familia
- **Type/date:** Current official program pages, accessed 2026-11-12
- **Confidence:** High
- **Limit:** These pages define policy and intended operation; they do not prove consistent execution in every municipality.

### E2 — The minimum team and responsibility are explicit

**Claim.** Current Ministry guidance specifies a minimum family-health team of a physician, nurse, nursing assistant or technician, and community health workers, normally working 40 hours weekly. The registered-population parameter is 2,000 to 3,000 people depending on municipality size.

- **Primary source:** Brazil Ministry of Health, Family Health Team page
  - https://www.gov.br/saude/pt-br/composicao/saps/esf/equipe-saude-da-familia
- **Type/date:** Official operating guidance citing Portaria GM/MS No. 3,493 of 2024
- **Confidence:** High
- **Limit:** Formal staffing parameters do not establish vacancy rates, continuity, or service quality.

### E3 — Community health workers connect household knowledge to the team

**Claim.** Community health workers extend family-health teams into homes and communities, helping teams understand needs, identify problems, and support public-health action.

- **Independent institutional source:** World Health Organization, 2024 primary-health-care case compendium
  - https://www.who.int/teams/primary-health-care/evidence-and-innovation/primary-health-care-case-study-compendium/detail/integration-of-public-health-and-primary-care-at-the-core-of-the-phc-approach--country-illustrations-from-brazil--lithuania--and-solvenia-%28chapter-5%29
- **Operational study:** Qualitative study of community health workers in seven primary-care centers
  - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5934337/
- **Confidence:** High for the mechanism; medium for consistency across Brazil
- **Limit:** The qualitative study is not nationally representative and describes workload and administrative constraints as well as intended practice.

### E4 — Actual 2024 operating scale is large

**Claim.** The Ministry of Health's 2024 balance reported 53,356 teams in operation, 4,750 new teams, 271,000 community health workers, 67,600 endemic-disease control workers, and R$1.1 billion in municipal investment.

- **Primary source:** Brazil Ministry of Health, 2024 balance
  - https://www.gov.br/saude/pt-br/assuntos/balancos/2024/saude-na-familia/saude-da-familia
- **Type/date:** Official administrative balance for 2024
- **Confidence:** High as reported
- **Limit:** This is a government-reported inventory, not an independent audit. “New teams” and investment should not be treated as outcomes.

### E5 — A later review reports a different 2024 count and explains measurement changes

**Claim.** A 30-year review reports 54,600 Family Health Strategy teams, 300,000 community health workers, and estimated potential coverage of 78% in 2024. It also notes that coverage definitions changed after 2020 and again in 2024.

- **Source:** “Thirty years of the Family Health Strategy in the Brazilian Unified National Health System”
  - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13286402/
- **Type/date:** Peer-reviewed historical and policy review, 2026
- **Confidence:** Medium-high
- **Limit:** Some authors are close to the program and the review uses official data. Totals differ from the Ministry balance because of timing, inclusion, and indicator definitions; the article must not blend them.

### E6 — Equity reach is supported, but not uniform equity

**Claim.** In the 2019 National Health Survey, FHS coverage was reported at 74% in the lowest income quintile and 38% in the highest. People registered with FHS more often reported a regular source of care (79%) than those not registered (73%).

- **Source:** 30-year review summarizing the 2019 Brazilian National Health Survey
  - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13286402/
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed synthesis of national survey data
- **Confidence:** Medium-high
- **Limit:** Coverage is not the same as quality or resolved need. The same review documents geographical and infrastructure inequalities.

### E7 — Infant mortality contribution is supported, not sole causation

**Claim.** A longitudinal ecological analysis of Brazil's 27 states from 1990 to 2002 found that a 10-percentage-point increase in program coverage was associated with a 4.5% decrease in infant mortality after adjustment for sanitation, income, literacy, fertility, health workforce, and hospital beds.

- **Source:** Macinko, Guanais, and de Souza, *Journal of Epidemiology and Community Health* (2006)
  - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2465542/
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed longitudinal ecological analysis
- **Confidence:** Medium-high
- **Limit:** Ecological observational study; residual confounding remains. The authors describe the program as an important but not unique contributor.

### E8 — Cardiovascular and cerebrovascular mortality associations strengthen the case

**Claim.** A nationwide longitudinal analysis covering 2000–2009 found FHS coverage associated with lower mortality and hospitalization from ambulatory-care-sensitive heart and cerebrovascular diseases, with larger effects after longer implementation.

- **Source:** Rasella et al., *BMJ* (2014)
  - https://www.bmj.com/content/349/bmj.g4014
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed nationwide longitudinal analysis
- **Confidence:** Medium-high
- **Limit:** Observational design; the association is not proof that FHS alone caused the decline.

### E9 — Governance affects the result

**Claim.** A fixed-effects study of 1,622 municipalities from 2000 to 2012 estimated that moving from 0% to 100% FHS coverage was associated with a 6.8% reduction in amenable mortality, with stronger performance where municipal health governance was stronger.

- **Source:** “Large Reductions in Amenable Mortality Associated With Brazil's Primary Care Expansion and Strong Health Governance,” *Health Affairs* (2017)
  - https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28069858/
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed longitudinal fixed-effects study
- **Confidence:** Medium-high
- **Limit:** Model-based association; the full-coverage comparison is an estimated contrast, not a randomized intervention.

### E10 — Avoidable hospitalization is a material counterexample

**Claim.** A panel analysis of 5,506 municipalities from 2000 to 2014 found that coverage expanded from 14% to 64% and primary-care consultations increased, but did not find a reduction in avoidable hospitalizations attributable to FHS; full coverage was associated with a small increase in the modeled hospitalization rate.

- **Source:** Hone et al., *BMJ Global Health* / PubMed record
  - https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29225926/
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed national panel analysis
- **Confidence:** High for the study's finding
- **Limit:** Avoidable admissions are affected by hospital access, coding, service supply, and local context. This study rebuts a blanket efficiency claim, not the entire FHS model.

### E11 — Systematic review supports child-mortality association but not a universal effect

**Claim.** A systematic review found that 14 studies associated higher coverage with lower post-neonatal or child mortality, while 13 hospitalization studies showed no clear pattern and several other outcomes showed no effect.

- **Source:** Bastos et al., systematic review (2017)
  - https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28786997/
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed systematic review
- **Confidence:** High for the mixed state of evidence
- **Limit:** Most included studies were ecological and heterogeneous, preventing pooled estimates.

### E12 — National health gains are context, not FHS-attributable results

**Claim.** OECD reports that Brazil's life expectancy rose 5.7 years between 2000 and 2019, infant mortality fell 60%, and avoidable-hospitalization rates declined 45% between 2001 and 2016 while describing FHS as a key contributor to broader reforms.

- **Source:** OECD, *Primary Health Care in Brazil* (2021)
  - https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/primary-health-care-in-brazil_120e170e-en/full-report/executive-summary_071500f8.html
- **Type:** Independent international policy review
- **Confidence:** High for national trend; low for attributing the trend to FHS alone
- **Limit:** These are system-wide trends shaped by multiple policies and socioeconomic changes. They must not be presented as effects caused solely by FHS.

### E13 — Current gaps are material

**Claim.** The current system still faces uneven infrastructure, staff turnover, incomplete community assessment, weak local participation in many areas, and poor integration with specialist and hospital networks, especially in underserved regions.

- **Source:** 30-year review
  - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13286402/
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed review using the 2024 national basic health unit census
- **Confidence:** Medium-high
- **Limit:** Conditions vary by municipality; the article should not imply uniform failure.

## Goal, capacity, implementation, outcome — explicit separation

### Declared goal
The current official goal includes universal and qualified access, equity, continuity, prevention, and coordination, with a target of 80% coverage by 2026 in the 2024 balance. The target is not an achieved outcome.

### Designed capacity
Teams hold responsibility for registered populations in defined territories and combine clinical work with home and community presence. This is the designed operating capacity.

### Actual implementation
Implementation is evidenced by national policy since 1994, current team composition rules, 2024 operating inventories, the presence of tens of thousands of teams, and hundreds of thousands of community health workers.

### Observed result
The strongest results are repeated associations with selected mortality outcomes and greater access among lower-income populations. Evidence is not uniform: avoidable hospitalization results are inconsistent or negative, and quality and integration remain uneven.

## Confidence summary

- Operating-model redesign: **High**
- Current team rules and formal responsibilities: **High**
- 2024 implementation scale: **High as reported; definitions vary**
- Community-health-worker mechanism: **High**
- Contribution to lower infant mortality: **Medium-high**
- Contribution to cardiovascular/amenable mortality: **Medium-high**
- Reduction in avoidable hospitalizations: **Rejected as a general claim**
- Uniform quality and equity: **Rejected**
- FHS alone caused Brazil's national health gains: **Rejected**

## Acceptance decision

**PASS WITH LIMITS.** Evidence is sufficient for a 10X case because the operating-model transformation, scale, mechanism, outcome evidence, counterevidence, and governance limits are all traceable. Publication is permitted only if the drafts preserve the observational nature of most outcome evidence, keep coverage definitions separate, and present implementation gaps prominently.
