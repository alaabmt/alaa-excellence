# Company Insider Critique — Brazil Family Health Strategy

**Nature of review:** Evidence-based critical simulation from the perspective of a senior municipal or federal Family Health Strategy leader. This is not testimony from Brazil's Ministry of Health or any implementation team.

## Main challenges

### 1. The title could imply that clinics were replaced
The strategy did not move care wholly out of clinics. Basic health units remain essential. The transformation links facility care to territorial responsibility, home visits, and community presence.

**Required revision:** Preserve the title's contrast but state early that the model connects clinics and communities rather than replacing the facility.

### 2. National design is not uniform municipal practice
The draft describes the formal operating model accurately, but readers could infer that every municipality implements registration, home visiting, community assessment, staffing, and referral coordination in the same way.

**Required revision:** Strengthen the distinction between the policy design and actual local execution. Note that workload, vacancies, infrastructure, and administrative burden can weaken the intended role.

### 3. The 2024 inventory is not a quality measure
Team and workforce counts show scale. They do not prove continuity, productivity, or resolved health need.

**Required revision:** Keep the explicit distinction and avoid using the inventory in the social card as an impact result unless it is labeled as operating scale.

### 4. The causal story must remain plural
Infant and cardiovascular mortality changed alongside sanitation, education, income, immunization, hospital capacity, and other social programs. Even adjusted ecological studies can retain unmeasured confounding.

**Required revision:** Keep “associated with” and “contributed within a wider transformation.” Do not use “reduced” as a direct causal headline.

### 5. Avoidable-hospitalization evidence is not a footnote
For an internal team, the negative national study matters because the strategy is often expected to prevent avoidable admission. It shows that primary-care reach without timely specialist and hospital integration may not improve that indicator.

**Required revision:** Retain the counterevidence as a full analytical section and connect it to the design conditions.

### 6. The governance mechanism needs sharper decision rights
The draft shows federal funding and municipal organization, but it should make clear that decentralization creates adaptation and variation. The model relies on municipal management capacity, not merely compliance with a national blueprint.

**Required revision:** State that local governance is an active production factor and a source of variance.

### 7. The pilot-to-scale chain is not documented
The sources describe long-term rollout and policy evolution, but not a single national pilot protocol with baseline, duration, documented learning, and scale criteria.

**Required revision:** Exclude the innovation/experimentation pillar and preserve the gap.

### 8. Community-health-worker capability has a workforce cost
Workers can become overloaded with registration and administrative tasks, reducing time in homes and communities. A transfer lesson that ignores workload design would be incomplete.

**Required revision:** Add workload protection to the transferable conditions.

### 9. Coverage measures changed
The article correctly avoids a single uninterrupted coverage series. Any future chart or metric card must not combine pre-2020, 2021, and 2024 coverage definitions.

**Required revision:** Keep coverage percentages out of the visual headline; if shown in body, label the definition and period.

### 10. The official video is explanatory, not independent
The video can help users see the operating model but cannot validate outcomes.

**Required revision:** Label the video official and educational/promotional, and verify embed availability on both public pages before completion.

## Revisions supported by evidence

The following are required before the English draft and final gate:

1. Add a sentence that basic health units remain the operating base and the change is the connection between facility, territory, and household.
2. Strengthen the municipal-variation and worker-workload caveats.
3. Keep all outcome language associative or contributory.
4. Preserve the negative hospitalization result prominently.
5. Add workload protection to the transfer section.
6. Keep Pillar 6 excluded.
7. Use operating-scale numbers only with their official-reporting label.
8. Label the video as official explanatory material, not evaluation evidence.

## Remaining public-evidence gaps

- Nationally consistent data on vacancy, turnover, visit frequency, and continuity by municipality.
- A single comparable long-run coverage indicator after methodology changes.
- A documented national pilot-learning-scale protocol.
- Uniform measures of referral completion and specialist waiting time.
- Independent verification of the 2024 administrative inventory.
- A national causal estimate that isolates FHS from every concurrent social and health policy.

## Critique decision

**PASS WITH REQUIRED REVISIONS.** The case remains publication-worthy because the draft can represent the operating model, outcomes, and counterevidence without promotional simplification. The review does not authorize publication until the revisions are applied and the remaining gates pass.
