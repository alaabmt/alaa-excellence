## Duplicate and publication check

The repository publication-date registry, Arabic and English article indexes, sitemap, and the durable 10X workflow state were checked on `main`. No 10X case is recorded with a publication date of 2026-11-12, and no existing case covers Brazil's Family Health Strategy (FHS / ESF).

## Scan summary

The scan covered UAE, regional, and global institutional transformations. UAE and regional candidates were not advanced where the public record was dominated by announcements, targets, awards, or capacity claims without sufficiently independent outcome evidence. The Brazil Family Health Strategy was selected over these candidates because it provides a traceable operating-model redesign, three decades of scaled implementation, official current operating data, multiple quasi-experimental and systematic evaluations, and material evidence that challenges rather than merely supports the case.

## Why this case qualifies

Brazil's Family Health Strategy reorganized primary care around multidisciplinary teams responsible for defined territories and populations. Community health workers link households to clinics through registration, home visits, prevention, surveillance, and continuity of care. The transformation is therefore not a new facility or technology; it changes the unit of responsibility from episodic attendance to ongoing responsibility for a community.

The evidence supports:

1. a clear strategic problem: unequal access and a historically hospital-centered model;
2. a documented operating-model shift: territorial multidisciplinary teams with community health workers;
3. implementation at national scale since 1994;
4. current operating scale: the Ministry of Health reported 53,356 teams in operation and 271,000 community health workers in its 2024 balance;
5. independent evidence associating expansion with lower infant and amenable mortality in several studies;
6. contrary evidence: one national panel analysis found no reduction in avoidable hospitalizations and a systematic review found no clear pattern for that outcome;
7. transferable lessons about assigned populations, community presence, team-based care, and the governance required to connect local knowledge to the wider health network.

## Evidence risks identified before acceptance

- National health improvements cannot be attributed to the FHS alone; sanitation, income, education, immunization, hospital supply, and other policies changed at the same time.
- Many evaluations are ecological or quasi-experimental rather than randomized.
- Coverage definitions changed after 2020, so historical and current percentages are not directly interchangeable.
- Service quality, staffing stability, infrastructure, and integration with specialist care vary substantially by region.
- The strongest causal language will be avoided. Results will be described as reported, associated, or contribution evidence according to the source.
- The 2024 Ministry balance and the 2024/2025 review use different team totals because of timing and definitions; figures will retain their source and period.
