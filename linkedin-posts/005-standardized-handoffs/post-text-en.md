When a shift ends, the doctor going off hands over what they know about each patient. Miscommunication at that moment is a leading source of medical errors.

In 32 U.S. hospitals, resident doctors learned one standard way to hand over, called I-PASS, with 18 months of coaching. Verbal handoffs that covered all five key elements rose from 20% to 66%. Handoff-related adverse events, as reported by the doctors themselves, fell by 47%.

Results are not always this clear. In a randomized trial on nine Australian wards, the main outcome did not change, although pressure injuries fell. And in a review of 42 studies, results were more often positive when training came with changes to how the work is done.

My question for you:
Which handoff between two teams in your organization depends today on one person's memory?
