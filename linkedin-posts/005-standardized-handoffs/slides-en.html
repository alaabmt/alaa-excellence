The handoff

What happens when a shift ends?

The doctor going off shift hands over what they know about each patient: how they are doing, which tests are pending, and what to do if things get worse.

Miscommunication at this moment is a leading source of medical errors. So resident doctors in 32 U.S. hospitals learned one standard way to hand over, with 18 months of coaching.

The method is called I-PASS. Its five steps: Illness severity, Patient summary, Action list, Situation awareness and contingency planning, and Synthesis by the receiver.

Starmer et al., J Hosp Med, 20232 / 8
Findings

Better handoffs, fewer reported adverse events

47%

drop in handoff-related adverse events, as reported by the residents themselves.

66%

of verbal handoffs covered all five key elements, up from 20%.

83%

of handoffs ended with the receiver summing up what they heard, up from 31%.

Systematic review · 2025

Which handoff tool has the best evidence?

A systematic review looked at ten years of evidence on 12 handoff tools used within hospital units.

  • I-PASS reduces errors and adverse events, with moderate certainty, based on ten studies.
  • The older SBAR tool may improve safety, but with low certainty.
  • The other tools have not been tested enough to judge.
McCarthy et al., BMJ Qual Saf, 20254 / 8
Why does a standard help?

The key information comes up every time, in the same order.

Then the receiver sums up what they heard, so a misunderstanding shows up before the other person leaves.

Patient safety then depends less on one person's memory at the end of a long shift.

This is the author's interpretation, not something the study measured.

A standard is not always enough

Handoffs can improve while the main outcome stays the same

41%

3/16

of the studies that measured adverse events found an improvement, in a review of 42 studies.

Results were more often positive when training came with changes to how the work is done.

What the studies suggest

What can leaders do?

  • 1Choose a tested handoff method, and ask the receiver to sum up what they heard.
  • 2Don't stop at training. Change the work: a standard template, a set time and place, and support in the first months.
  • 3Measure handoff quality by direct observation, and track outcomes over enough time.

Sources and limits

What this evidence can and cannot tell us

  • The 32-hospital study had no control group, and adverse events were reported by the doctors themselves.
  • Some of its authors hold equity in a company that trains hospitals in I-PASS; an independent team ran the analysis.
  • None of the studies included Arab hospitals. Applying the idea to other sectors is an inference.
