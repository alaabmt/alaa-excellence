Staff fix a problem on their own. The next day, it is back.

In U.S. hospitals known for strong nursing care, Anita Tucker and Amy Edmondson observed nurses for 239 hours. For 93% of problems, staff did just enough to finish the task and told no one. This was not a lack of effort. Most stayed 45 minutes after their shift, without extra pay.

More than 20 years later, a Belgian study published in 2026 gave 80 nurses tasks with built-in failures in a simulation. They noticed 96.2% of the failures, but acted on the cause or reported it in only 21.9% of cases.

That is the paradox: the better people handle a problem alone, the more it stays hidden from those who could fix it.

My question for you:
How many problems did your team fix on its own today that will be back tomorrow?
