The study

Not a case study. A randomized experiment.

In 2008, economists from Stanford and the World Bank worked with 28 plants in 17 large textile firms near Mumbai.

Group A got a one-month diagnosis and a list of recommendations.

Group B got the same diagnosis, plus four months of hands-on help to put the changes in place.

The focus: 38 basic practices that are standard in well-run factories, covering quality, inventory, maintenance, people and orders.

Bloom et al., Quarterly Journal of Economics, 20132 / 8

Lesson 1

A report rarely changes how work is done.

Increase in adoption of the 38 practices (percentage points)

Diagnosis + recommendations only
+12
Diagnosis + 4 months of hands-on implementation
+38

Both groups knew what to fix. The group that had help doing it changed three times as much.

Bloom, Mahajan, McKenzie & Roberts, 2018/20204 / 8
The payoff
+17%

productivity in the first year

  • Fewer quality defects, so less fabric was scrapped
  • Routine maintenance and breakdown tracking, so looms stopped less often
  • Lower inventory

The researchers estimated the gain at about US$325,000 in profit per plant per year.

Bloom et al., Quarterly Journal of Economics, 20135 / 8
Eight years later (2017)

About half of the adopted practices had been dropped.

Why? The two reasons managers cited most:

  • 1A new plant manager. In the plants that got hands-on help, this explained more than half of all dropped practices.
  • 2Owners and directors had less time for the plant.

Still, these plants stayed clearly ahead in practices and performance, and many practices had spread to sister plants in the same firm.

Bloom et al., AEJ: Applied Economics, 20206 / 8
What this means for leaders

Excellence that depends on one person is not yet a system.

  • 1Fund implementation, not only diagnosis.
  • 2Build the practice into standards, daily routines and handover plans, so it outlives its champion.
  • 3Protect senior attention after go-live, not only before it.
If your best manager left tomorrow, which of your improvements would survive?

Sources and limits

What this evidence can and cannot tell us

  • One industry, one region, family-owned firms, 28 plants.
  • The profit figure is the researchers' estimate.
  • Reasons for dropping practices were reported by managers and owners.

[1] Bloom, N., Eifert, B., Mahajan, A., McKenzie, D., & Roberts, J. (2013). Does Management Matter? Evidence from India. Quarterly Journal of Economics, 128(1), 1–51.

[2] Bloom, N., Mahajan, A., McKenzie, D., & Roberts, J. (2020). Do Management Interventions Last? Evidence from India. American Economic Journal: Applied Economics, 12(2), 198–219. (World Bank Policy Research Working Paper 8339, 2018.)
