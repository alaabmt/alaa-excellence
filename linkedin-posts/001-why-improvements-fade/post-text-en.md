Productivity rose 17% in one year.
Eight years later, about half of the new practices were gone.

This is not a hand-picked success story. It is a randomized experiment in 28 Indian textile plants, run by economists from Stanford and the World Bank and published in two leading peer-reviewed economics journals.

Three lessons for operational excellence:

1. A report rarely changes how work is done. Plants that received only a diagnosis and recommendations adopted about a third as many practices as plants that also got hands-on implementation support.

2. The biggest threat came later. When a plant manager left, practices went with them. In the supported plants, manager turnover explained more than half of everything that was dropped.

3. Even so, the supported plants were still clearly ahead eight years on, and many practices had spread to sister plants in the same firm.

The takeaway: excellence that depends on one person is not yet a system.

So here is my question:
If your best manager left tomorrow, which of your improvements would survive?
