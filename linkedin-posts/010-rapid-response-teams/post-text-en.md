Before a patient's heart stops on a hospital ward, there may be warning signs that no one acts on in time. So many hospitals created rapid response teams that staff can call when a patient gets worse.

A review of 29 studies linked these teams to about 13% fewer deaths among adult patients. But most of those studies compared a hospital with itself, before and after the team started. In two randomized trials, in Australia and Belgium, there was no statistically significant difference in the main outcomes.

In the Australian trial, some patients met the calling criteria and were later admitted to intensive care. The team was called for only 30% of them. In my reading, creating a team may not be enough. It is more likely to help when staff know when to call it, and call it without hesitation.

My question for you:
Three years from now, do you want your response team to stay a phone number people hesitate to call, or do you want every employee to know when to ask for help, and to ask without fear?
