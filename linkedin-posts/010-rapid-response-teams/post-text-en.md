Before a patient's heart stops on a hospital ward, there may be warning signs that no one acts on in time. That is why many hospitals created rapid response teams that staff can call when a patient gets worse.

A review of 29 studies linked these teams to about 13% fewer deaths among adult patients than in periods or hospitals without a team. But most of those studies compared a hospital with itself, before and after the team started. In two randomized trials, in Australia and Belgium, there was no statistically significant difference in the main outcomes between care with and without the new approach.

In the Australian hospitals with teams, some patients showed the warning signs for calling the team and were later admitted to intensive care. The team was called for only 30% of them. In my reading, creating a team may not be enough. It is more likely to help when staff know when to call it, and call it without hesitation.

My question for you:
Three years from now, will your response team still be a phone number that people hesitate to call? Or will every staff member know when to ask for help, and ask without fear?
