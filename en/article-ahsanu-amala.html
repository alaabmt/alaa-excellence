Best in Deed: A Philosophy of Excellence in Life

Excellence is not doing more; it is making what we do better, and making its impact last. From here, the idea of ihsan — doing work with excellence and care — can become an approach that reaches from the individual to the home, and from the organization to society and the state.

By Dr. Alaa Mohammad Ahmed · 5 September 2026
“He who created death and life to test you, which of you is best in deed.” (Qur’an 67:2)

This verse points to a very deep standard. It does not say “most in deed”; it says “best in deed”. The difference between quantity and quality is not only a difference of words; it is a difference in how we look at life as a whole.

A person may get a great deal done without adding real value. An organization may work non-stop without improving. A state may have huge resources and projects, yet the most important questions remain: How good is what has been achieved? What is its impact on people? And will that impact last?

Ihsan in work opens a wider view of excellence: quality in action, awareness of purpose, and responsibility for impact.

Excellence starts with the individual

Before excellence becomes an organizational system, it starts as a personal decision. It shows in how we think, in discipline and commitment, in keeping promises, in the quality of our decisions, and in the work we do even when no one is watching.

Personal excellence does not mean being better than everyone else, or chasing an impossible ideal. It means constantly asking yourself: Can I do what I am responsible for in a better way?

Seen this way, self-development goes deeper than learning new skills. It is the development of awareness, responsibility, and the ability to review, learn and correct course.

Home: the first place where we practice excellence

We talk a lot about excellence in organizations, but the first place where we create quality every day is the home. The quality of conversation, respect for others, raising children, handling disagreement and taking responsibility are all practical forms of ihsan.

An excellent home is not a home without challenges, but one that has a better way of dealing with them. When the question inside the family becomes How do we do this better? improvement stops being a management idea and becomes a way of life.

At work: from finishing tasks to creating value

The difference between ordinary and excellent performance often starts with a small question. An employee may ask: “Have I done what was required?” Someone who thinks with an excellence mindset asks: Have I achieved the purpose? Have I added value? Can the way I work be improved?

An excellent leader does not just manage people; they build the environment that enables people to succeed. An excellent organization cares less about the number of initiatives and more about the quality of the system that turns strategy into results.

That is why organizational excellence is not a collection of separate documents, awards or indicators. It is an integrated capability to understand purpose, design work, measure results, learn from them, and then improve again.

An excellent state turns resources into better lives

The same principle extends to society and the state. A state’s value is measured not only by what it has, but by its ability to turn what it has into better education, better health, more efficient institutions, fairer opportunities and a better quality of life for people.

Countries do not excel through the number of projects alone, but through the quality of their choices, the strength of their institutions, the wise use of resources, and their ability to learn and keep going.

The question is not “How much did we do?” but “What is better because we did it?”

From “Am I done?” to “Did I do it well?”

Perhaps one of the most important shifts in thinking is changing the questions we use to judge our work. Instead of “Am I done?” the question becomes “Did I do it well?”

Instead of “Did we hit the number?” we ask “What value did the number produce?” Instead of “Did we succeed?” we ask “What impact did this success leave, and can it last?”

These questions do not only make better individuals; they can build stronger homes, more mature organizations and societies better able to progress.

Tamayuz 10X

Excellence is not a final station where we arrive and stop. It is constant movement between doing, reviewing, learning and improving. Every result opens a new question, and every success gives us a new chance to raise the bar.

So wherever we are, we can carry one simple but very powerful question:

What can I do better today?
Excellence is not doing more; it is making what you do better, and making its impact last.
