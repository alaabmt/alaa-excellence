Beyond the Ranking: What Makes a Hospital One of the World’s Best?

What should hospital leaders learn from an international ranking? The answer begins with understanding what is actually measured—and what is not.

By Dr. Alaa Mohammad Ahmed

Back to the series

INSIGHT 01 · WORLD’S BEST HOSPITALS 2026

What the 2026 methodology measures

The 2026 edition assessed more than 50,000 hospitals and recognized 2,530 across 32 countries. The national lists combine four types of evidence. The baseline weights are 40% for quality metrics, 35% for peer recommendations (30% national and 5% international), 18.5% for experience and 6.5% for PROMs implementation. Country-level data availability can change these weights.

  • Clinical quality metrics
  • Independent peer recommendations
  • Patient experience
  • Implementation of patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs)

What the ranking cannot tell us

A rank is a comparative composite, not a certificate of compliance or a direct measure of every clinical outcome. National scores cannot be compared directly between countries. The international Top 250 uses a separate combination of measures. The PROMs component assesses implementation rather than guaranteeing superior patient outcomes.

A leadership application

Start with an honest internal baseline. Ask the quality team to map the published measures to accountable owners and validated evidence; identify which are directly applicable in your country; then review improvement trends rather than treating the ranking as an end in itself. For UAE hospitals, the methodology has a dedicated country section that deserves separate review. This is a proposed management approach, not an official Newsweek requirement.

Leadership question: Can your organization demonstrate sustained improvement in the outcomes that matter to its patients, independently of its ranking?

A ranking is a benchmark. Excellence is a system of continuous improvement.
