What Did Moses Ask For Before He Went to Pharaoh?

When great work begins with prayer, help is asked for in the measure of need, and a partner becomes a help in remembering Allah

Methodological note: This is a contemporary reflection on verses from Surah Ta-Ha (20:24–36 and 20:42), read in light of the classical commentaries. It does not offer a new interpretation of the verses, and it does not attribute modern management ideas to the Qur’an.

Moses, peace be upon him, was traveling back from Madyan with his family when, one night on the road, he saw a fire in the distance. He walked toward it, hoping to bring back a burning brand, or to find someone who could show them the way. Instead, he was called and spoken to. He watched his staff change in his hands and his hand come out white. Then came the command that would change his whole life:

﴿اذْهَبْ إِلَىٰ فِرْعَوْنَ إِنَّهُ طَغَىٰ﴾Meaning in English: “Go to Pharaoh. Indeed, he has transgressed [i.e., tyrannized].”Surah Ta-Ha, 20:24

Pharaoh was the king in whose palace Moses had grown up, and the ruler he had fled in fear after killing one of his people. Now he was being sent back to him, to call him to Allah. Moses made no excuse and asked nothing about the road or the timing. He turned to his Lord with what he needed:

﴿قَالَ رَبِّ اشْرَحْ لِي صَدْرِي﴾ ﴿وَيَسِّرْ لِي أَمْرِي﴾ ﴿وَاحْلُلْ عُقْدَةً مِّن لِّسَانِي﴾ ﴿يَفْقَهُوا قَوْلِي﴾Meaning in English: [Moses] said, “My Lord, expand [i.e., relax] for me my breast [with assurance]” “And ease for me my task” “And untie the knot from my tongue” “That they may understand my speech.”Surah Ta-Ha, 20:25–28

Having asked for himself, he then asked for someone to stand with him:

﴿وَاجْعَل لِّي وَزِيرًا مِّنْ أَهْلِي﴾ ﴿هَارُونَ أَخِي﴾ ﴿اشْدُدْ بِهِ أَزْرِي﴾ ﴿وَأَشْرِكْهُ فِي أَمْرِي﴾ ﴿كَيْ نُسَبِّحَكَ كَثِيرًا﴾ ﴿وَنَذْكُرَكَ كَثِيرًا﴾ ﴿إِنَّكَ كُنتَ بِنَا بَصِيرًا﴾Meaning in English: “And appoint for me a minister [i.e., assistant] from my family” “Aaron, my brother.” “Increase through him my strength” “And let him share my task” “That we may exalt You much” “And remember You much.” “Indeed, You are of us ever Seeing.”Surah Ta-Ha, 20:29–35

The answer came in a single verse, and it called him by name: You have been granted your request, O Moses. Ta-Ha, 20:36

He met the heaviest task with prayer, not excuses

The first thing to notice is that Moses answered the command with a request, not a complaint. Al-Saadi describes him as knowing that “he had taken on a great burden,” and says he obeyed his Lord’s command “and received it with openness and acceptance.” He explains the expanding of the breast as a request for room “to bear harm in word and deed,” because “when the breast is narrow, its owner is unfit to guide people and call them” [1].

Moses did not ask to be excused from going. He asked for what would help him carry the task. That is the meaning of faith on which the whole scene rests: a servant of Allah goes where he has been sent and uses every means within reach, knowing all the while that an open heart and an easy path are in the hands of Allah, glorified and exalted is He. Prayer here does not replace the work. It is the first step of it.

He asked for one knot to be untied so that he would be understood

Notice that the prayer does not say “untie my tongue.” In the Arabic, it asks for “a knot” to be untied “from” his tongue: And untie the knot from my tongue. The commentators paid attention to how much he asked for. Ibn Kathir notes that Moses “did not ask for it to be removed entirely, only enough for the impediment to go and for them to understand what he meant, and that is the measure of need” [2]. Al-Qurtubi reports that scholars differed over whether the knot disappeared completely or something of it remained, and that one argument for the second view is that “he did not say: untie all of my tongue” [3].

The purpose then comes in a verse of its own: That they may understand my speech. We might reflect that the prayer sets a measure for speech, and the measure is whether the listener understands it, rather than whether the listener is impressed. He asked according to his need, and his need was defined by whether the meaning reached people.

Then he asked for a brother to strengthen him and share the task

Having asked for himself, Moses asked that his brother Aaron be with him. Elsewhere in the Qur’an, Moses names what set his brother apart: And my brother Aaron is more fluent than me in tongue Al-Qasas, 28:34. He knew what he lacked, and he knew who could make up for it.

The commentators differed over the words And let him share my task. Al-Tabari explains them as “make him a prophet as You have made me a prophet, and send him with me to Pharaoh” [4], while Ibn Kathir says they mean “in my consultation” [5]. On either reading, Aaron was to be a partner in the task itself, rather than a follower who handles its edges. And Aaron’s prophethood was Allah’s own choice, given in answer to Moses’ prayer, so it cannot be compared to appointing someone to a job.

The prayer begins in the singular and ends in the plural

Notice that every request in the first part of the prayer is in the first person singular: My Lord, expand [i.e., relax] for me my breast, And ease for me my task, And appoint for me a minister. Once Aaron is named and the partnership is complete, the prayer shifts to the plural: That we may exalt You much, And remember You much, and then Indeed, You are of us ever Seeing.

The aim of the partnership was not more hands or a louder voice. Al-Saadi explains that Moses knew “that all acts of worship, and the religion as a whole, turn on the remembrance of Allah,” and so asked that his brother be with him so that the two of them would “help and support each other in righteousness and piety” [6]. We might reflect that the help Moses asked for was help to stay close to his Lord in the hardest of moments, before it was help with speaking. He closed his prayer by placing their need before the One from whom nothing about them is hidden.

The answer came in full, with a charge

The whole prayer was granted: You have been granted your request, O Moses. Allah then reminded Moses of the favors He had shown him since infancy. After that, the command came again, this time to two people rather than one:

﴿اذْهَبْ أَنتَ وَأَخُوكَ بِآيَاتِي وَلَا تَنِيَا فِي ذِكْرِي﴾Meaning in English: “Go, you and your brother, with My signs and do not slacken in My remembrance.”Surah Ta-Ha, 20:42

Al-Saadi links this charge to the first prayer. He explains do not slacken in My remembrance as “do not slacken or grow lazy in remembering Me constantly; keep at it and hold fast to it, as you both promised” [7]. What Moses had asked for as the purpose of the partnership became the charge the two brothers carried to Pharaoh.

What do we ask for when we are given more than we can carry?

Here we move to our own work, as a reflection and not an interpretation, and we keep the difference in view. Moses was a prophet carrying a message from Allah, his prayer was answered by revelation, and Aaron was chosen by Allah as a prophet. None of that can be compared to an assignment at work. Still, the scene raises a question for anyone who has been handed more responsibility than they are used to.

Take a hypothetical example. A newly appointed manager is asked to turn around a department buried in complaints and to present her plan to the board within a month. She could start by proving she can do everything on her own. Instead, before she begins, she writes down what she actually needs: patience with the resistance she expects, a few clear priorities to make the work manageable, a way of explaining the plan that the staff will understand before the board does, and a colleague who knows the department from the inside and is good at what she is not. Then she asks for that colleague to share in the decisions rather than simply carry them out. She is not showing weakness. She is naming her needs before a crisis names them for her.

What questions do the eight 10X pillars open?

We are not saying that the verses offer a modern management model. But they raise questions that meet three of the eight pillars of 10X excellence.

Leadership starts with knowing what you lack

Under the pillar of leadership, transformation culture and future governance, the scene reminds us that a leader who names what they need before starting gives others permission to do the same. Over time, that builds a culture where people ask for help early instead of hiding gaps until they surface halfway down the road.

Impact multiplies when a partner supplies what is missing

Under the pillar of multiplying capabilities and empowerment, Moses did not ask for a crowd of helpers. He asked for one particular person who was good at what he was not, and then asked that this person share the task. Real empowerment gives a partner a say in the whole of the work, not just a piece of its edges.

The value of words is measured by whether they are understood

Under the pillar of redefining value and strategic direction, That they may understand my speech may lead us to ask about our messages, our reports and the instructions that come with our services: did the people they were meant for understand them? A message can seem eloquent to the person who wrote it and still fail to reach the person it was written for.

Questions to take into the next assignment

  1. Do we begin our big tasks with prayer, or remember it only when things get hard?
  2. What single knot, if untied, would let people understand what we mean?
  3. Who is good at what we are not, and have we made them a partner in the task or handed them its edges?
  4. Does our shared work bring us closer to the remembrance of Allah, or distract us from it?

The 10X excellence question

What could your team carry a few years from now if everyone on it knew what they needed and asked for help early?

Imagine your team a few years from now. Everyone on it knows what they lack before they start, asks without embarrassment for the person who can fill the gap, and judges its messages by whether they are understood. What will the team be able to carry then that it cannot carry today? And which partnership will you start building now?

A final reflection

The scene opened with a heavy command, Go to Pharaoh. Indeed, he has transgressed, and Moses answered with a prayer rather than an excuse: My Lord, expand [i.e., relax] for me my breast, And ease for me my task. Then he asked, And untie the knot from my tongue, for a clear purpose, That they may understand my speech. He asked that his brother be with him, Increase through him my strength, and made the purpose of it all That we may exalt You much And remember You much.

Then came the answer, You have been granted your request, O Moses, and the command came to two rather than one: Go, you and your brother, with My signs and do not slacken in My remembrance. What we might carry from this scene is to begin our heaviest work by asking Allah, to ask for what we need in the measure we need it, and to let the people we work with help us remember Him, as we say: Indeed, You are of us ever Seeing.
