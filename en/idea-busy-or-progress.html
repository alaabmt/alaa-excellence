Idea of the Day

Are You Busy, or Are You Making Progress?

Not every busy day is a productive day, and not every activity is a sign of progress. The more important question is not “How much did you do?” but “What is better because you worked today?”

We can fill a day with meetings, messages, calls and tasks, and end it feeling that we worked very hard. But a lot of movement does not necessarily mean we moved in the right direction.

Busyness measures movement. Progress measures the difference that movement made.

You may finish twenty small tasks and put off the one decision that could have changed the outcome of a project. A team may attend dozens of meetings while the core problem stays unsolved. An organization may launch many initiatives while its performance indicators and the experience of the people it serves do not improve.

This is where the difference between activity and value shows. Activity answers the question What did we do? Value asks What changed because we did it?

Before you start your day, ask: what is the one result that, if I achieve it today, would genuinely make this a better day?

This simple question resets your priorities. It pushes you to choose work that creates impact, not work that only uses up time. The same applies to leaders, teams and organizations: what matters is not the number of meetings, reports or initiatives, but how far you have moved toward the goal.

Excellence does not always mean doing more. Sometimes it starts when we stop doing certain things, protect our time for what matters most, and link every activity to a result we want to achieve.

Idea of the Day

Don’t measure your day by the number of tasks you finished. Measure it by the progress you made, and by what is better because of your work.
