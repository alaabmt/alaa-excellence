Idea of the Day

Before You Improve, Make Sure You Are Measuring What Matters

Organizations do not improve just because their performance indicators turn green. Real improvement starts when we make sure that what we measure reflects the value we want to create, not just what is easy to count.

One of the quietest mistakes in management is improving a number that does not accurately reflect the result we want. A process may get faster while rework increases. The number of people served may rise while the quality of their experience falls. A team may hit its target without any improvement in the problem the indicator was created for.

A good indicator does not just tell you what happened; it helps you see whether you are really getting closer to the goal.

So the question that should come before any improvement project is not How do we raise this indicator? but Does this indicator represent the success we want? The difference between the two questions is large. The first may improve the number; the second improves the system.

Mature measurement balances several angles: the final result, the quality of the process that produced it, the experience of the people served, and any side effects of the change. When we look at only one dimension, we can create a local success at the expense of the whole system.

Pick one of your important indicators today and ask: if this number improves, can I say with confidence that the service or the organization is better? If the answer is unclear, the indicator itself needs to be reviewed.

In organizational excellence, measurement is neither a dashboard for display nor simply a tool for accountability; it is part of how we think. The right indicator makes the problem clearer, shows the impact of decisions, and stops us from celebrating a cosmetic improvement.

Before you invest time and resources in improving performance, make sure you have defined success correctly. What we measure directs attention, what we pay attention to shapes behavior, and repeated behavior shapes the organization’s results.

Idea of the Day

Don’t start by improving the number. Start by making sure the number measures what is worth improving.
