10X Case · Biopharma Manufacturing · Institutional Intelligence

Building the Factory for What Comes Next

The most important question is not which technology to deploy. It is what the organization must be capable of in the future—and what has to be built now to make that future possible.

Executive Snapshot

Bristol Myers Squibb’s Devens campus in Massachusetts spans about 89 acres, employs roughly 1,700 people, and supports process development, clinical manufacturing and commercial manufacturing for biologics and cell therapies.[1]

BMS has built physical and digital infrastructure designed for adaptability and growth, combining advanced automation, connected systems, analytics and artificial intelligence in day-to-day operations. BMS and the World Economic Forum describe more than 30 digital and AI use cases across the manufacturing lifecycle, including predictive process monitoring, advanced analytics, real-time decision support and better orchestration of manufacturing and quality.[1][2]

The Future Devens Was Built to Face

Public sources do not show that BMS conducted a formal foresight exercise at Devens under that label. What they do show is a facility designed for adaptability and growth, with infrastructure intended to evolve alongside scientific advances and changing patient needs.[1]

That matters because biologics and cell therapies can depend on living cells or biological systems, making process variability and tight control especially important. The future challenge is not simply higher output; it is the ability to manufacture more complex therapies, introduce new products faster, adapt to scientific change and make better-informed decisions while production is under way.

A useful example is chimeric antigen receptor T-cell therapy (CAR-T). In simplified terms, a patient’s T cells are collected, modified so they can better recognize a target on certain cancer cells, prepared and expanded, then returned to the patient. Manufacturing therapies of this kind illustrates why process control and the management of biological variability matter.

Work Backward from the Future

Future

More complex and changing therapies.

Gap

Limited visibility and slower response when systems and data remain fragmented.

Flexible infrastructure, automation, connected data, analytics and AI.

Operating Model

Earlier detection and decision support during operations.

Shorter timelines, higher volume and lower emissions.

This is the core of the case. AI is neither the starting point nor the end state. It is one capability inside a broader operating system.

How the Capability Was Built

Adaptable infrastructure

BMS describes Devens as having physical and digital infrastructure designed for adaptability and growth, including advanced automation, digitally connected systems and flexible manufacturing arrangements that can evolve with changing requirements.[1]

Connected data

Data volume alone does not create intelligence. If equipment data, quality information, production records and historical process data remain separated, teams must assemble the picture after the fact. Connected systems move information closer to the point of work and create the foundation for analysis during operations.

Multidisciplinary teams

BMS also emphasizes collaboration among engineers, scientists and digital specialists. That is not a minor organizational detail. The data specialist understands the model, the engineer understands the process and equipment, the scientist understands the product, and operations teams understand the context in which a decision must be made. The capability is created when those perspectives work together.[1]

How Data Becomes a Decision

Predictive process monitoring uses current and historical data to identify signals or patterns that may precede a process deviation. It does not mean knowing the future with certainty.

Pressure, temperature and flow can each remain within acceptable limits while the relationship among them starts to shift in an unusual way. Advanced analysis can ask not only whether an individual value has crossed a threshold, but whether a group of values is moving in a pattern that deserves attention.

The operating sequence therefore shifts from problem appears → information is gathered → analysis → decision toward operating data → change detected → significance interpreted → early alert → expert review → decision → intervention.

The published Devens material refers to real-time decision support, not wholesale replacement of human judgment. The more precise value proposition is that specialists can see relevant information earlier, then use their expertise and quality procedures to decide whether action is required.[1]

Evidence of Impact

Product-introduction timelines: down by more than 42%

New product introduction (NPI) is the process of moving a product from development and preparation into stable manufacturing. It can include process and equipment preparation, qualification work, quality requirements, data readiness and coordination across specialist teams. BMS reports a reduction of more than 42% since 2022.[3]

Production volume: up by more than 40%

The public evidence does not allow a precise allocation of this increase among AI, automation, process redesign or other changes. It would therefore be incorrect to say that AI alone produced the 40%+ increase. The defensible conclusion is that the broader operating transformation was associated with substantially higher output.[2][3]

Emissions: down by more than 40%

This adds a sustainability dimension to the case. Public summaries, however, do not provide enough detail to attribute the reduction to individual interventions or fully unpack every measurement boundary. The result is documented; the fine-grained causal story remains limited.[2][3]

Evidence boundary: BMS is the primary source for most operating details. The World Economic Forum provides independent professional validation of the case and outcomes, but the detailed metrics can still depend on site-submitted data. Confidence in the reported results is therefore relatively high, with caution about assigning each result to a specific technology.

The 10X Lens

Foresight and Ambition

The case does not require claiming a formal foresight methodology. The stronger evidence is that design choices were made for a future expected to be more complex and changeable.

Capability Multiplication

Flexibility, data, analytics, automation and human expertise combine to expand what teams can see, understand and act on.

Operating-Model Redesign

Data is no longer only a record of what happened; it becomes an input into what should happen next while work is under way.

Institutional Intelligence and Results

Data becomes understanding, understanding supports a decision, the decision leads to action, and the outcome becomes new learning.

What Transfers Beyond Biopharma?

The transferable principle is not a pharmaceutical technology. It is the logic of capability building. A hospital might start with a future in which deterioration is detected before a crisis. A government agency might aim to anticipate demand before queues form. A utility might want to identify failure signals before service is interrupted.

In each case, the organization defines the future first, then works backward to identify the data, systems, skills, governance, decisions and ways of working that must be built now.

The 10X Question

Imagine your organization ten years from now operating at the level you genuinely aspire to reach. What does that future look like in practice? What can the organization do then that it cannot do today? Now work backward: what data, systems, skills, governance and ways of working must you start building now to make that future achievable?
