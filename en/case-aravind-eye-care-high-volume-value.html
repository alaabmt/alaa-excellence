When Cost Reduction Isn't Austerity: How Aravind Redesigned Whitewater Surgery?

Aravind Eye Care System is an Indian system that specializes in eye care. It includes hospitals, testing, treatment, training and manufacturing of eye medical supplies. It has built an operational model that can treat large numbers while maintaining strong clinical quality and providing a wide range of services free of charge or at a subsidized cost.

Executive Snapshot

In the year ending March 2025, Aravind recorded more than 6 million outpatient visits and 734,322 surgical, laser or injection procedures. Of these, 257,052 were supported and 106,770 were free of charge through screening campaigns.[1]

What is cataract surgery?

Cataracts, or cataracts, are cataracts that make vision blurry or dull, and may worsen to interfere with reading, driving, work and daily life. The condition is treated surgically by removing the cataracted lens and implanting a transparent artificial lens inside the eye.

The basic idea: What is important in this case is not only the technique of surgery, but how Aravind has redesigned the system that connects the patient from examination to treatment, and uses the time of the surgeon, staff and supplies more efficiently.

Challenge: When treatment is known but difficult to access

Cataract surgery is a well-known and effective procedure, but the problem does not end when treatment is in place. The price, distance, lack of ophthalmologists or poor capacity may prevent the patient from accessing surgery.

In the traditional model, part of a physician’s time can be wasted on tasks that a trained team can safely accomplish. When demand increases, the scarce experience becomes the choke point.

The 10X MoveInstead of asking the surgeon to work faster, Aravind redistributed the entire work, so that the doctor would spend his time on tasks that actually needed a doctor, and other tasks would be performed by trained teams and standardized protocols.

How did Aravind redesign the work?

Aravind deconstructed the service into specific tasks and then deployed each task at the most appropriate professional level. Much of the work in testing, measurements, preparation and support is done by trained support staff, while the ophthalmologist focuses on diagnosis, medical decision and surgery.

The design did not stop at the operating room. The system uses screening campaigns and sight centers in rural and semi-rural areas to bring care closer to people. Aravind notes that sight centers provide primary eye care, linking more complex cases to the hospital remotely or by referral.[2]

The system also created Aurolab, a unit for manufacturing ophthalmic accessories, which began to develop intraocular lenses at a more accessible price. This vertical integration made the cost of some inputs part of the design issue itself.[3]

Impact Guide: Size did not come at the expense of quality

The strongest recent evidence comes from an analysis of 3.6 million surgeries performed at Aravind hospitals between 2012 and 2023. The study showed continuous improvement in visual outcomes after surgery during the period.[4]

In an older study of high-volume surgery, 94% of completed patients achieved corrected visual acuity of 6/18 or better, with an average surgical time of 3.75 minutes for the condition.[5]

Economically, a study published in Health Affairs estimated the total cost of the operation at $120, or $195 per QALY Quality-Adjusted Life Year, a measure that combines years of life with a healthy quality of life.

Directory Limits: These studies prove that Aravind combines high volume, quality and low cost, but it does not isolate the impact of each operational component individually. Therefore, not all results are attributed to a single method of work distribution or manufacturing.

Redefining Value and Strategic Direction

Value is defined not by the number of operations or revenues alone, but by the ability to restore sight with quality, reach, and financial sustainability.

Capacity Building and Empowerment

Instead of considering the shortage of ophthalmologists only as a recruitment problem, the system redistributed work and built specialized support staff.

Reinventing the organization and its business model

Hospitals, vision centers, community access, training and manufacturing elements in one system address access, cost and capacity constraints simultaneously.

Maximizing Value and Sustainable Results

The ultimate test is not how much the system saves, but its ability to maintain both reach, quality, and scale.

What can be used in practice?

For the leader: Protect the time of rare experience

Identify the work that really needs the highest level of expertise, and then examine whether other tasks consume this time without needing to.

For the Foundation: Redraw the Service Trip

Classify each step into work that requires high competence, work that is standardized, work that is delegable, and work that can be transferred closer to the beneficiary.

Portability

Aravind is not literally copied to every health service or institution. Cataract surgery is highly suitable for consolidation and replication, while other services require greater customization.

But the design principles are transferable: protecting scarce experience time, unifying what can be consolidated, building the workforce around clear roles, reducing waiting, and bringing service closer to the beneficiary.

In the health sector in particular, the delegation of tasks must comply with the licensing laws, scope of practice and safety standards of each country.
