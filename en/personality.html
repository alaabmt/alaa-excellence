How You Learn

Learning Style Profile

What is this assessment?
A development tool that helps you understand how you tend to learn or build a new skill: through practice, reflection and review, understanding and analysis, or practical application.

In short: It builds your self-awareness about how you learn: which preferences you use more automatically, which ones show up less, and how to use this knowledge before a course or during a training needs analysis.
Benefits for learners and trainers
For learnersThe report shows the strengths in how you learn and the approaches you use less. You can use this when choosing a course, when discussing your needs with a training provider, or during a training needs assessment: you will be able to describe the activities and methods that usually help you engage and learn, while also noticing the areas you may need to practice more.
For trainers and learning designersParticipants’ results before a program can help design a more varied and balanced learning experience that combines practice, reflection, understanding and analysis, and practical application. The results also show what the group leans toward and what may need a stronger place in activities, discussions, exercises and reviews.

It is best used before training programs and courses, or during a training needs analysis, so that both learner and trainer start with a clearer picture of current preferences and of what may need balancing or developing.

What will you receive?
The learner receives a personal report that explains their relatively stronger and weaker preferences, how these relate to each other, notable sub-dimensions, potential strengths, the risks of relying too much on one preference, and practical steps to build flexibility across the learning cycle. When the assessment is used in a training session, the trainer can also receive a group view and summary of results to understand the group and use this in planning the program and discussing learning needs.
The aim is not to put learners into a “fixed type”, or to assume that people only learn better when a course matches their current preference. The result is a tool for self-awareness, dialogue and learning planning, used alongside the nature of the content, the program’s objectives and the learner’s actual needs.
Theoretical basis and how the tool was developed
The tool draws on Experiential Learning Theory, associated with the work of David Kolb, and on the Honey & Mumford Learning Styles Model, associated with Peter Honey and Alan Mumford. The content and questions were redeveloped into an original behavioral tool that focuses on current learning preferences rather than sorting people into fixed types.
Practice → Reflect → Understand → Apply → Practice again
The tool was developed in structured stages: reviewing the theoretical framework, defining the dimensions and their components, analyzing content, drafting and reviewing items, checking that the items cover each dimension, language review in Arabic and English, and designing one consistent system for scoring and interpretation.
Current stage: The tool is in its pilot stage. Content development and structured review are complete. Statistical psychometric validation will be completed using pilot data, to examine item properties, internal consistency and the structure of the dimensions, and to improve interpretation.

How You Work

Work Approach Assessment

What is this assessment?
A development tool that helps you understand how a person tends to handle different work situations: how they make sense of the situation, how they organize the work, when they move to action, how they coordinate with others, and which conditions help them work more effectively. It also looks at how responses can change with context, instead of assuming that a person has one fixed style in every situation.

In short: The assessment builds self-awareness about working style, strengths, needs from the work environment, and how to adapt to different situations. It can be used by individuals, managers, teams, coaches and development programs.
Benefits for the individual

The assessment builds self-awareness of your working style. It helps you identify the behaviors you use more naturally, and those you show less or may need to use more in some situations.

It also helps you understand what you need from your work environment, such as a clear purpose and role, access to information, room to make decisions, involvement, clear priorities and timing, and an understanding of change. You can use this in development and coaching sessions, when moving to a new role or team, or when discussing your professional needs with your manager.

No style is better than another. The result helps you understand what you usually do, and what you may need to stretch when the demands of a situation change.

Benefits for managers and teams
For managersManagers can use the results to understand the natural differences in how team members approach work. This helps improve communication, clarify expectations, discuss how responsibilities are shared, and understand why people differ in how they start, decide or coordinate.
For teamsWhen the assessment is used with a group, it can build a group picture of working styles and work-environment needs. This helps the team see its shared strengths and the areas that get less attention, and turn differences from a source of misunderstanding into a topic for dialogue and better ways of working.

The results can support practical questions such as: Does the team need more clarity before starting? Does it tend to analyze more than it acts? Does it need stronger coordination, or more room to make decisions?

Benefits for trainers, coaches and organizational development

The assessment can be used before leadership, team-building, professional development and coaching programs as part of a development needs analysis. Instead of delivering the same program in the same way to everyone, the trainer can understand how the group tends to approach work, which environmental needs are most common, and where gaps in flexibility or coordination may exist.

This helps design exercises, case studies and discussions that are closer to participants’ real work, and choose the development topics that need more focus.

When is it most useful?
  • Before leadership development and team-building programs.
  • When forming a new team or starting a new project.
  • When moving to a different role or responsibility.
  • During periods of organizational change.
  • In individual development and coaching sessions.
  • When there are repeated difficulties with coordination, role clarity or decision-making.
What will individuals, managers and coaches receive?
Individuals: a personal report explaining how they tend to make sense of situations, organize work and move to action, how they work with others, which work-environment needs matter most to them, how their responses change with context, their potential strengths, the areas that show up less, and when they may need to broaden their flexibility.

Managers or coaches using it with a group: a group view and summaries that show the spread of working styles and environment needs in the team, the most and least common tendencies, and what can be used in program design, team building, discussions about ways of working, and choosing group development topics.
Theoretical basis and how the tool was developed
The Work Approach Assessment was developed from three connected theoretical frameworks: Action Regulation Theory, to understand how a person moves from understanding and organizing a task to carrying it out; Person–Environment Fit Theory, to understand the relationship between a person’s characteristics and needs and the characteristics of the work environment; and Person–Situation Interactionism, to understand that behavior comes from the interaction between the person and the demands of the situation, not from the person’s characteristics alone.

The tool was developed in structured stages: reviewing the theoretical basis, building the map of dimensions, developing and drafting items and scenarios, reviewing content and coverage, conceptual and language adaptation in Arabic and English, developing the scoring and interpretation logic, and checking that the report is clear for participants.
Current stage: The tool is in a research pilot stage and has not yet been adopted as a validated psychometric instrument. Pilot data will be used to test item properties, consistency and structure, the suitability of interpretations, and to improve the version before any final adoption.
Important note on interpreting results

This assessment is a tool for development and self-awareness. It is not an ability test or a personality diagnosis, and it must not be used on its own to make hiring, promotion or exclusion decisions. There is no ideal result and no single right style; the main value of the tool is in raising awareness, improving dialogue and building flexibility across different work situations.

Agentic AI Organizational Readiness

Evidence-based readiness review

What is this review?
An evidence-based organizational review that helps determine whether the foundations required for Agentic AI are in place before broader execution authority is granted.

In brief: 18 questions across six domains: value and use case, governance and accountability, data and systems, delegation and human oversight, testing and evaluation, and monitoring and continual improvement.
What will you receive?
The result provides an evidence and gap map with action priorities, plus a suggested operational decision: complete critical foundations, proceed with a limited controlled pilot, or consider controlled scaling where evidence supports it.
Reference basis and limitations
The Tamayuz 10X review is informed by principles from NIST AI RMF and ISO/IEC 42001:2023. It is not a NIST or ISO questionnaire, a compliance certification, or a validated psychometric scale.
