The Defensible Framework: A Conceptual Model for Physician-Led Clinical Documentation Integrity

Defensible Framework: A Physician-Led Conceptual Model to Enhance the Integrity and Completeness of Clinical Documentation
International Journal of Academic Research in Business and Social Sciences Vol. 16 · Issue 8 · 2026 DOI: 10.6007/IJARBSS/v16-i8/28960

Authors: Dr. Alaa Mohammad Ahmed, Dr. Abdulgader Almessabi, Dr. Hamad AlZaabi, Dr. Omar Khaddam, Dr. Shirein Mohamed Sheikh Shakhabout Medical City (SSMC), Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates.

Summary in English

The Research Framework DEFENSIBLE As a ten-component conceptual model for organizing physician-led diagnostic documentation throughout a patient’s inpatient journey. It stems from a fundamental gap that many clinical documentation integrity improvement programs Clinical Documentation Integrity (CDI) It works interactively after a lack or ambiguity in documentation, while the framework proposes a proactive approach that links documentation to clinical reasoning stages from assessment, diagnosis to conclusion. The study adopted a non-systematic narrative review of existing literature and frameworks, and then analyzed the recurring shortcomings and formulated five key conceptual gaps, including the lack of a proactive, doctor-centered framework, weak correlation with the diagnostic cycle, and the lack of adequate mechanisms to align the diagnosis of exit with the clinical thinking that preceded it.

Exploring the impact and challenges of tele-ICU: A qualitative study on nursing perspectives

Summary in English

The study explores the perceptions of intensive care and tele-intensive nurses. Tele-Intensified Care Unit (Tele-ICU) Remote Intensive CareThe study adopted a qualitative approach and semi-structured interviews with nurses who moved from traditional intensive care roles to Tele-ICU roles. Understanding the Importance and Future Directions of Cultural Sensitivity in Healthcare: Developing a Culturally sensitive Patient Experience Framework with Emirati Patients as a Case Study

International Journal of Academic Research in Business and Social SciencesVol. 14 · Issue 3 · pp. 225–232DOI: 10.6007/IJARBSS/v14-i3/21045

Authors: Alaa Mohammad Ahmed, Md Saad Nor Hasliza, Samer Ellaham, Shahrukh Hashmi, Abba C Zubair, Marwan Mohammad Alrahmaneh, Nabeel Al-Yateem.

Summary in English

The paper discusses the importance Cultural sensitivity in the patient experienceEmirati patients are used as a case study to understand how values, beliefs and social contexts affect the perception of quality of care, trust and satisfaction. Patient Experience Framework as a structured perception of what an organization should design to deliver a positive experience, and between Patient Experience Survey As a tool to measure what the patient has actually experienced, an important challenge in the UAE is the wide diversity of the health workforce and cultural and linguistic backgrounds between providers and patients. Missing the point: Quality-driven healthcare institutions may mistakenly disregard their role in nursing and medical education

When health institutions focus on quality and overlook their role in nursing education and medicine
Nursing Forum · Vol. 57 · Issue 1 Pages 211–214 DOI: 10.1111/nuf.12647

Authors: Alaa Mohammad Ahmad, Amina Al-Marzouqi, Jacqueline Maria Dias, Ahmad Rajeh Saifan, Nurmalini Md Kassim, Marini Nurbanum Mohammad, Nabeel Al-Yatem.

Summary in English

The paper discusses an important paradox in health quality management: an organization may succeed in building strong systems for measurement, accreditation and performance improvement, but at the same time weaken one of the roles that support long-term quality sustainability, namely the education and qualification of doctors and nurses. It suggests that clinical education is not an activity separate from quality of service, but part of the institutional capacity to prepare future cadres, attract and retain professionals, and maintain a learning and operating health system.

Synthesizing core nursing skills to support behavioural-based interviews for nurses in the UAE: A nominal group study

Identify key nursing skills to support structured behavioral interviews for nurses in the UAE
Journal of Nursing Management · Vol. 29 · Issue 5 Pages 953–961 DOI: 10.1111/jonm.13232

Authors: Nabeel Al-Yateem, Alaa Mohammad Ahmad, May McCreaddie, Loay Bahaa's Edeen Al Hussini.

Summary in English

The research aims to build a clear foundation of clinical and performance skills that can later be used to design more structured behavioral interview questions when recruiting nurses in the UAE. The study used three courses from Nominal Group Technique (NGT) Nominal Group Technology The process resulted in the identification of ten performance skills and ten clinical skills for each specialty area. Skills such as communication and critical thinking emerged in the performance capabilities, while skills such as administering drugs, following the heart, and dealing with disorders of the systems emerged in the important clinical capabilities. The value of the study lies in moving employment from general questions to interviews that are more clearly linked to the skills actually needed in the work environment.

Nurse-identified patient care and health services research priorities in the United Arab Emirates: a Delphi study

Patient care and health services research priorities as identified by UAE nurses
BMC Health Services Research · Vol. 19 · Article 77 January 29, 2019 DOI: 10.1186/s12913-019-3888-5

Authors: Nabeel Al-Yateem, Muna Al-Tamimi, Maria Brenner, Hanan Al Tawil, Alaa Ahmad, Sharon Brownie, Shameran Slewa-Younan.

Summary in English

The study sought to prioritize research in patient care and health services from the perspective of frontline nurses in the UAE. Delphi Method Delphi Method In two phases to reach a broad consensus, 1,032 UAE nurses participated in Phase I and 1,339 in Phase II. The results showed that patient safety and staff awareness of international patient safety goals, including staffing levels, shift length issues and their potential safety implications, were a top priority. Infection control and infectious disease management practices also featured prominently in the health context. The results provide a practical basis for guiding nursing research programs toward issues that practitioners themselves see as more relevant to quality.

Quality and safety issue: language barriers in healthcare, a qualitative study of non-Arab healthcare practitioners caring for Arabic patients in the UAE

Language Barriers in Healthcare: A Case for Quality and Safety in the Care of Arabic-Speaking Patients in the UAE
BMJ OpenVol. 13 · Issue 12 · e076326DOI: 10.1136/bmjopen-2023-076326

Authors: Nabeel Al-Yateem, Heba Hijazi, Ahmad Rajeh Saifan, Alaa Ahmad, Rami Masa'Deh, Intima Alrimawi, Syed Azizur Rahman, Muhammad Arsyad Subu, Fatma Refaat Ahmed.

Summary in English

Qualitative research explored the experiences of 14 non-Arabic speaking health practitioners working in different health facilities in the UAE, using semi-structured interviews. The results showed that language barriers are not limited to the difficulty of conveying information, but may extend to the quality of service, patient safety and the experience of the practitioner himself. Three main themes emerged in the participants’ accounts: feeling isolated while dealing with the situation, trying to approach the patient and overcome the language gap, and feelings of guilt, fear and dissatisfaction when effective communication is not enough.

Research priorities for specialized nursing practice in the United Arab Emirates

Research Priorities for Specialized Nursing Practice in the UAE
International Nursing ReviewVol. 65 · Issue 3 · pp. 381–391DOI: 10.1111/inr.12402

Authors: Nabeel Al-Yateem, Muna Al-Tamimi, Maria Brenner, Hanan Altawil, Alaa Ahmad, Sharon Brownie.

Summary in English

The study used a two-stage Delphi method to identify clinically relevant research priorities in the areas of specialist nursing in the UAE. 783 people participated in the first round and 1116 in the second round after expanding access to additional clinical sites. The study identified 58 high-priority research priorities across disciplines including pediatric nursing, emergency, intensive care, maternity, operating room and long-term care. The results provide a practical map that can help researchers, academic institutions, funding bodies and policy makers guide research issues.

Mental health literacy among pediatric hospital staff in the United Arab Emirates

Mental Health Education in Children’s Hospitals UAE
BMC PsychiatryVol. 17 · Article 390DOI: 10.1186/s12888-017-1556-z

Authors: Nabeel Al-Yateem, Rachel Rossiter, Walter Robb, Alaa Ahmad, Mahmoud Saleh Elhalik, Sumaya Albloshi, Shameran Slewa-Younan.

Summary in English

The study assessed the level of mental health knowledge among health workers caring for chronically ill children in the UAE with a cross-sectional survey that included clinical scenarios of PTSD and depression accompanied by suicidal thoughts. The results showed limited ability to correctly identify disorders and evidence-based interventions; the highest accuracy for identifying psychosis was 54.3%. K10 scores also showed that about 40% of participants had moderate to high levels of mental health along with tertiary education.

Evaluating the Reliability and Validity of the Arabic Version of the Parents Perceptions of Uncertainty Scale (A-PPUS)

Assessment of the reliability and validity of the Arabic version of the Parental Perception Scale for Child Illness-related Uncertainty (A-PPUS)
Journal of Pediatric Nursing · Vol. 36Pages 179–185DOI: 10.1016/j.pedn.2017.06.017

Authors: Nabeel Al-Yateem, Maria Brenner, Alaa Ahmad, Syed Azizur Rahman.

Summary in English

The study deals with the adaptation and testing of the Arabic version of Parents Perceptions of Uncertainty Scale (PPUS)The study focused on verifying the psychometric properties of the Arabic version, including reliability and honesty, with the aim of providing a tool suitable for use with Arabic-speaking families in clinical and research contexts. The results showed a high level of internal consistency; the Kronbach alpha coefficient was 0.93, which supports the reliability of the tool. The importance of the research lies in providing an Arabic scale that can help the parents and researchers understand the lack of quality of the tool.

The quality of information received by parents of children with chronic ill health attending hospitals as indicated by measures of illness uncertainty

The quality of information provided to parents of chronically ill children as reflected in the levels of uncertainty associated with the disease
Scandinavian Journal of Caring SciencesVol. 31 · Issue 4 · pp. 839–849DOI: 10.1111/scs.12405

Authors: Nabeel Al-Yateem, Charles Docherty, Hanan Altawil, Muna Al-Tamimi, Alaa Ahmad.

Summary in English

The study examined the level of uncertainty experienced by parents of chronically ill children in the UAE as an indicator of the quality of health information and communication. The study used a cross-sectional descriptive design and the Parent Perception of Uncertainty Scale tool after being culturally adapted. The results showed that most participants experienced moderate or high levels of uncertainty, and that the level was higher in those who did not speak the local language, and in some groups related to the child’s age and stress level.
