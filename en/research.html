The Defensible Framework: A Conceptual Model for Physician-Led Clinical Documentation Integrity

The DEFENSIBLE framework: a physician-led conceptual model for more complete and reliable clinical documentation
International Journal of Academic Research in Business and Social Sciences Vol. 16 · Issue 8 · 2026 DOI: 10.6007/IJARBSS/v16-i8/28960

Authors: Dr. Alaa Mohammad Ahmed, Dr. Abdulgader Almessabi, Dr. Hamad AlZaabi, Dr. Omar Khaddam, Dr. Shairein Mohamed — Sheikh Shakhbout Medical City (SSMC), Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates.

Summary

The paper presents the DEFENSIBLE framework, a ten-component conceptual model for organizing physician-led diagnostic documentation across the patient’s hospital journey. It starts from a basic gap: many programs for clinical documentation integrity (CDI) work reactively, after documentation is already incomplete or unclear. The framework instead proposes a proactive approach that links documentation to the stages of clinical reasoning, from assessment and diagnosis through to discharge. The study used a non-systematic narrative review of existing literature and frameworks, analyzed recurring weaknesses, and identified five main conceptual gaps. These include the lack of a proactive, physician-centered framework, a weak link to the diagnostic cycle, and too few mechanisms for aligning the discharge diagnosis with the clinical reasoning that came before it. The paper offers the framework as a structured basis for education, better documentation quality and support for clinical coding in IR-DRG and MS-DRG settings, and stresses that its effectiveness must still be validated through expert review and applied studies before any claims are made about its practical impact.

Exploring the impact and challenges of tele-ICU: A qualitative study on nursing perspectives

The impact and challenges of tele-intensive care: a qualitative study of nurses’ perspectives
Applied Nursing Research · Vol. 82Article 151914DOI: 10.1016/j.apnr.2025.151914

Authors: Ahmad Rajeh Saifan, Mohammad Sameer Odeh, Bayan Oleimat, Mohannad Eid AbuRuz, Alaa Mohammad Ahmed, Nadin M. Abdel Razeq, Waddah D'emeh, Mohammad Yahya Ayoub, Nabeel Al-Yateem.

Summary

The study explores how intensive care nurses and tele-intensive care unit (tele-ICU) nurses see the benefits, challenges and requirements of bringing this model into critical care. Using a qualitative design with semi-structured interviews, it followed nurses who had moved from traditional ICU roles to tele-ICU roles. Participants described benefits linked to continuous monitoring, support for early intervention and better use of resources, along with possible organizational gains such as shorter stays, fewer readmissions and better planning after discharge. They also described challenges: technical infrastructure, cost, resistance to changes in workflow, gaps in training, and cultural and organizational factors. The study concludes that tele-ICU succeeds only when technology, training, supportive policies and a redesign of clinical work come together in a way that protects quality of care and patient safety.

Understanding the Importance and Future Directions of Cultural Sensitivity in Healthcare: Developing a Culturally sensitive Patient Experience Framework with Emirati Patients as a Case Study

Why cultural sensitivity matters in healthcare: toward a culturally sensitive patient experience framework, with Emirati patients as a case study
International Journal of Academic Research in Business and Social SciencesVol. 14 · Issue 3 · pp. 225–232DOI: 10.6007/IJARBSS/v14-i3/21045

Authors: Alaa Mohammad Ahmed, Md Saad Nor Hasliza, Samer Ellaham, Shahrukh Hashmi, Abba C Zubair, Marwan Mohammad Alrahmaneh, Nabeel Al-Yateem.

Summary

The paper discusses the importance of cultural sensitivity in the patient experience, using Emirati patients as a case study to understand how values, beliefs and social context shape how people judge quality of care, trust and satisfaction. It distinguishes a patient experience framework, a structured view of what an organization should design to deliver a positive experience, from a patient experience survey, a tool that measures what patients actually experienced. It highlights an important challenge in the UAE: a highly diverse health workforce, whose cultural and language backgrounds often differ from those of patients. It also points to cultural beliefs, traditional medicine, privacy and modesty, spirituality and the role of the family as factors that can shape the experience of Arab and Muslim patients. The paper calls for patient experience frameworks that fit the local context, and for more qualitative research and training in culturally sensitive care, and presents this view as a basis for further research and for testing frameworks in different cultural settings.

Missing the point: Quality-driven healthcare institutions may mistakenly disregard their role in nursing and medical education

When health organizations focus on quality and overlook their role in educating nurses and doctors
Nursing Forum · Vol. 57 · Issue 1 Pages 211–214 DOI: 10.1111/nuf.12647

Authors: Alaa Mohammad Ahmad, Amina Al-Marzouqi, Jacqueline Maria Dias, Ahmad Rajeh Saifan, Normalini Md Kassim, Marini Nurbanum Mohamad, Nabeel Al-Yateem.

Summary

The paper discusses an important paradox in healthcare quality management. An organization may succeed in building strong systems for measurement, accreditation and performance improvement, and yet weaken one of the roles that sustains quality over the long term: educating and training doctors and nurses. It argues that clinical education is not separate from service quality but part of an organization’s capability to prepare the future workforce, attract and keep professionals, and maintain a health system that can learn and renew itself. The paper offers a conceptual reflection on the problems that can arise when quality assurance and operational demands crowd out the teaching role, and proposes a more integrated approach that treats education, service and quality as overlapping responsibilities rather than competing goals.

Synthesizing core nursing skills to support behavioural-based interviews for nurses in the UAE: A nominal group study

Identifying core nursing skills to support structured behavioral interviews for nurses in the UAE
Journal of Nursing Management · Vol. 29 · Issue 5 Pages 953–961 DOI: 10.1111/jonm.13232

Authors: Nabeel Al-Yateem, Alaa Mohammad Ahmad, May McCreaddie, Loay Bahaa' Edeen Al Hussini.

Summary

The study aimed to build a clear base of clinical and performance skills that could later be used to design more structured behavioral interview questions when recruiting nurses in the UAE. It used three rounds of the nominal group technique (NGT), a structured method for reaching group consensus, with thirty frontline nurses in pediatrics, outpatient clinics and cardiac/transitional care. The process identified ten performance skills and ten clinical skills for each specialty. Communication and critical thinking stood out among the performance skills, while medication administration, cardiac monitoring and managing arrhythmias stood out among the clinical skills. The study’s value lies in moving recruitment from general questions to interviews more clearly tied to the skills that clinical work actually requires.

Original research · BMC Health Services Research · 2019

Nurse-identified patient care and health services research priorities in the United Arab Emirates: a Delphi study

Research priorities in patient care and health services, as identified by nurses in the UAE
BMC Health Services Research · Vol. 19 · Article 77 29 January 2019 DOI: 10.1186/s12913-019-3888-5

Authors: Nabeel Al-Yateem, Muna Al-Tamimi, Maria Brenner, Hanan Al Tawil, Alaa Ahmad, Sharon Brownie, Shameran Slewa-Younan.

Summary

The study set out to identify research priorities in patient care and health services from the perspective of frontline nurses in the UAE. It used a two-round Delphi method, a structured process for building expert consensus: 1,032 nurses took part in the first round and 1,339 in the second. Patient safety and staff awareness of the international patient safety goals came out as top priorities, including staffing levels, shift length and their possible effects on safety. Infection control and the management of infectious diseases were also important areas. The results give a practical basis for directing nursing research toward the issues that practitioners themselves see as most closely linked to quality of care and health outcomes in the UAE, and can inform similar health settings.

Quality and safety issue: language barriers in healthcare, a qualitative study of non-Arab healthcare practitioners caring for Arabic patients in the UAE

Language barriers in healthcare: a quality and safety issue in caring for Arabic-speaking patients in the UAE
BMJ OpenVol. 13 · Issue 12 · e076326DOI: 10.1136/bmjopen-2023-076326

Authors: Nabeel Al-Yateem, Heba Hijazi, Ahmad Rajeh Saifan, Alaa Ahmad, Rami Masa'Deh, Intima Alrimawi, Syed Azizur Rahman, Muhammad Arsyad Subu, Fatma Refaat Ahmed.

Summary

This qualitative study explored the experiences of 14 health practitioners who do not speak Arabic and work in different health facilities in the UAE, using semi-structured interviews. It found that language barriers do more than make it hard to pass on information: they can affect service quality, patient safety and the practitioner’s own experience. Three main themes emerged: feeling isolated when handling the situation, trying to reach the patient across the language gap, and feelings of guilt, fear and dissatisfaction when communication falls short. The study supports more structured professional interpreting services and training for health teams in working well with interpreters, and treats language as part of safety and quality rather than a side issue of communication.

Published research · 2018

Research priorities for specialized nursing practice in the United Arab Emirates

Research priorities for specialized nursing practice in the UAE
International Nursing ReviewVol. 65 · Issue 3 · pp. 381–391DOI: 10.1111/inr.12402

Authors: Nabeel Al-Yateem, Muna Al-Tamimi, Maria Brenner, Hanan Altawil, Alaa Ahmad, Sharon Brownie.

Summary

The study used a two-round Delphi method to identify clinically important research priorities in specialized nursing in the UAE. In the first round 783 people took part, and 1,116 in the second, after access was extended to more clinical sites. The study identified 58 high-priority research topics across specialties including pediatric, emergency, intensive care, maternity, operating room and long-term care nursing. The results offer a practical map that can help researchers, academic institutions, funders and policymakers direct research toward clinical questions that bear directly on practice and on evidence-based care.

Mental health literacy among pediatric hospital staff in the United Arab Emirates

Mental health literacy among staff in children’s hospitals in the UAE
BMC PsychiatryVol. 17 · Article 390DOI: 10.1186/s12888-017-1556-z

Authors: Nabeel Al-Yateem, Rachel Rossiter, Walter Robb, Alaa Ahmad, Mahmoud Saleh Elhalik, Sumaya Albloshi, Shameran Slewa-Younan.

Summary

The study assessed mental health literacy among health staff who care for children with chronic illness in the UAE, using a cross-sectional survey with clinical scenarios of post-traumatic stress disorder, psychosis, and depression with suicidal thoughts. Participants showed limited ability to recognize the disorders correctly and to identify evidence-based interventions; the highest recognition rate, for psychosis, was 54.3%. Scores on the K10 scale, a standard measure of psychological distress, showed that about 40% of participants had moderate to high levels of distress. The findings point to the need to strengthen mental health in university education and continuing professional development, and to support the mental health of staff themselves.

Published research · Journal of Pediatric Nursing · 2017

Evaluating the Reliability and Validity of the Arabic Version of the Parents Perceptions of Uncertainty Scale (A-PPUS)

Testing the reliability and validity of the Arabic version of the scale measuring parents’ uncertainty about their child’s illness (A-PPUS)
Journal of Pediatric Nursing · Vol. 36Pages 179–185DOI: 10.1016/j.pedn.2017.06.017

Authors: Nabeel Al-Yateem, Maria Brenner, Alaa Ahmad, Syed Azizur Rahman.

Summary

The study adapted and tested the Arabic version of the Parents Perceptions of Uncertainty Scale (PPUS), a scale that measures how much ambiguity and uncertainty parents face while their child is ill and being treated. It focused on checking the psychometric properties of the Arabic version, including reliability and validity, so that the tool can be used with Arabic-speaking families in clinical and research settings. The results showed high internal consistency, with a Cronbach’s alpha of 0.93, which supports the tool’s reliability. The study matters because it provides an Arabic scale that can help health teams and researchers understand parents’ uncertainty more systematically, and connect it to the quality of communication, psychological support and family involvement in the child’s care.

Published research · 2017

The quality of information received by parents of children with chronic ill health attending hospitals as indicated by measures of illness uncertainty

The quality of information given to parents of children with chronic illness, as reflected in their uncertainty about the illness
Scandinavian Journal of Caring SciencesVol. 31 · Issue 4 · pp. 839–849DOI: 10.1111/scs.12405

Authors: Nabeel Al-Yateem, Charles Docherty, Hanan Altawil, Muna Al-Tamimi, Alaa Ahmad.

Summary

The study examined the uncertainty experienced by parents of children with chronic illness in the UAE as an indicator of the quality of health information and communication. It used a cross-sectional, descriptive and exploratory design with the Parent Perception of Uncertainty Scale after cultural adaptation. Most participants reported moderate or high uncertainty. Levels were higher among parents who did not speak the local language, and in some groups linked to the child’s age and the severity of the condition. Only about 5% of participants reported low uncertainty. The findings support clear, consistent communication with families as part of quality of care, decision support and reducing psychological stress.
