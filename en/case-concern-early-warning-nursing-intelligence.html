When Nursing Concern Becomes a Signal the Whole Team Can See: The CONCERN Case

The idea was not to replace clinical judgment with an algorithm. It was to turn nursing surveillance signals into shared situational awareness that reaches the care team earlier.

Executive Snapshot

CONCERN — COmmunicating Narrative Concerns Entered by RNs is an early warning system that uses patterns in registered nurses’ surveillance documentation as signals of clinical concern. It was tested in a one-year pragmatic cluster-randomized trial across 74 clinical units in two health systems, covering 60,893 hospital encounters. [1]

35.6%lower instantaneous risk of in-hospital death; HR=0.64, 95% CI 0.53–0.78. [1]
7.5%lower instantaneous risk of sepsis; HR=0.93, 95% CI 0.86–0.99. [1]

+24.9%higher instantaneous risk of unanticipated ICU transfer, requiring cautious operational interpretation. [1]

The Challenge: Information Exists, but It Does Not Always Arrive in Time

Patient deterioration can begin before conventional indicators make the crisis obvious. Traditional early warning systems often rely heavily on vital signs and physiological data. CONCERN used a different layer: nursing surveillance and documentation patterns that can reflect rising concern about a patient. [1]

EWS — Early Warning System is a tool intended to flag increasing deterioration risk before a crisis escalates. CONCERN uses ML — Machine Learning, models that learn patterns from data, and displays a regularly updated risk category inside the electronic health record. [1]

The 10X Move

The shift was not from clinician to algorithm. It was from distributed, partly tacit surveillance signals to a shared signal embedded in workflow. Before implementation, nurses participated in user-centered design and simulation, shaping alerting and interface choices. During implementation, unit champions supported peers as local superusers. [3][4]

Evidence of Impact

The strongest outcome was a 35.6% reduction in the instantaneous risk of in-hospital death. Sepsis is life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated response to infection; it matters here because earlier recognition of deterioration can create time for assessment and intervention. Its instantaneous risk was 7.5% lower. [1]

On 13 April 2026, Nature Medicine corrected a modeling error affecting length of stay. The adjusted IRR — Incidence Rate Ratio changed from 0.91 to 0.96 (95% CI 0.93–0.99; P=0.015), while the unadjusted means changed to 6.7 days in the intervention group and 7.4 days in usual care. This case therefore does not use “11.2% shorter stay” as the corrected effect-size claim. [2]

Unanticipated transfers to the ICU — Intensive Care Unit, the hospital setting for patients needing more intensive monitoring and treatment, increased (HR=1.25). Earlier escalation is a plausible interpretation, but the trial does not establish that every additional transfer was appropriate or that this was the sole causal mechanism. [1]

10X Lens

Capability & Empowerment

Nursing surveillance patterns became a signal that the wider care team could share.

Reinventing How the Organization Works

The signal was integrated into the EHR — Electronic Health Record, the digital system already used in clinical work, supported by workflow design, simulation and local champions. [3][4]

Institutional & Technical Intelligence

The algorithm did not create clinical knowledge from nothing; it made an existing pattern in work more visible, shareable and trackable.

From the Future to Impact

Desired future ← a hospital that sees meaningful deterioration signals before they become an obvious crisis.

Required capabilities ← trustworthy data, predictive models, EHR integration, workflow-centered design, governance, monitoring and human interpretation.

What must be built now ← the delivery path to the decision point, simulation, local champions, performance and equity measures, and learning from actual use.

Different way of working ← moving from waiting for a late signal to investigating an earlier shared signal and acting through clinical judgment.

Results and impact ← documented differences in mortality, sepsis, length of stay and ICU transfer, with each measure kept within its evidentiary limits. [1][2]

Practical Use for Leaders and Organizations

The stronger starting point is not “buy an AI alert.” It is to identify important knowledge already generated in work that does not reach the right people soon enough. Then ask whether it can be made visible without creating new noise, who interprets it, who owns intervention, and how the organization will measure real outcomes rather than alert volume.

Transferability and Evidence Limits

The transferable principle is broader than the model: identify early signals already present in work, make them shared and interpretable, place them inside the decision workflow, then test real outcomes. Transferring CONCERN itself requires revalidation because documentation practices, EHR architecture, nursing roles and escalation cultures differ.

Public evidence does not reveal every step between a displayed score and each team decision, nor does it fully separate the effect of the model from design, training and team behavior. The sources used here also do not provide a complete public economic return estimate. These are evidence gaps, not invitations to infer.

The Future-Focused 10X Question

What future do we want our organization to operate in—one where important risk and opportunity signals are visible before they become obvious crises? What would that future look like in practice, and what would our teams be able to see, understand and decide that they cannot do in the same way today? If that is the desired future, what data, human expertise, systems, skills, governance and ways of working must we build now so an early signal becomes a better decision and a measurable outcome?
