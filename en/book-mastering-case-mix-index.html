When the record captures less than the care

Every patient’s care has a story: diagnoses, coexisting conditions, complications, treatment decisions and the resources needed along the way. When those details are missing from the medical record, the data can tell an incomplete story.

Mastering Case Mix Index begins with that gap. It connects clinical practice, documentation, coding and leadership through a practical approach to turning the patient’s story into clear, reliable information.

Real care. Accurate documentation. Measures that reflect reality.

The book’s guiding principle is an accurate medical record that represents the patient’s condition and care. Better documentation may raise Case Mix Index, lower it or leave it unchanged. The right result is the one that faithfully reflects the clinical reality.

Why I wrote this book

This book grew out of my experience in clinical leadership, project management and healthcare consulting, particularly while leading an organisation-wide Case Mix Index improvement project.

One question kept coming up: How can we improve Case Mix Index (CMI) performance while keeping documentation accurate, ethical and focused on the patient?

The book brings together my practical answers. What does the physician need to document? How can the documentation team help? And what must leaders put in place to make improvement part of everyday work?

Two pillars working together

The approach brings physician documentation performance together with the performance of the Clinical Documentation Integrity (CDI) programme, which supports clear, complete and accurate records.

The physician: a clear, complete clinical story

The DEFENSIBLE Framework helps physicians document the patient’s actual condition clearly and completely, connecting clinical reasoning with what is recorded.

The organisation: a system that supports good documentation

The integrated change framework aligns the team around a shared workflow, addresses operational barriers, and supports testing solutions and sustaining improvement.

A shared clinical workflow connects the two pillars, supported by leadership, measurement, change management and professional standards. Documentation becomes an organisational practice in which everyone has a role.
