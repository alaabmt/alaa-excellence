Case 10X Abu Dhabi Proactive Government

From envisioning the government of the future to building its capacity today: What does Tamm’s experience in Abu Dhabi reveal?

The deeper value of TM is not that it bundled services into a single application. What makes it worth the Tamayuz 10X lens is a more fundamental trend: envisioning a different form of future relationship between government and customer, then building the data, integration, artificial intelligence and automation capabilities that bring this future closer to the present.

Executive Snapshot

Abu Dhabi’s Tamm platform serves 3.6 million users, and gathers more than 1,100 government and private services within a single system, according to Abu Dhabi government data published in 2025.[1] But size is not the core of the case. More important is the path of development: from assembling services, to an artificial intelligence assistant that can handle requests, and then to AutoGov to manage some repetitive tasks more proactively.[2][3]

The challenge: when the service becomes digital and the burden remains the same

The government can digitize hundreds of transactions without redefining the customer experience. The individual remains required to know the right entity, choose the service, understand its requirements, remember the renewal date, and link transactions that belong in his life to one trip but are organizationally distributed among different entities. The UAE literature on the adoption of digital government services consistently emphasizes the importance of perceived utility, ease of use, trust, quality of experience and public value.[5][6][7]

The problem, then, is not just the absence of technology; rather, the traditional service model assumes that it is the customer who carries the burden of understanding government.

The Future and Ambition: A Government Less Relying on Service Demand

Tamm’s development reveals a different future: a government that does not always wait for a client to start a transaction from scratch, but rather understands the context, gathers services around their needs, allows some requests to be made in conversation, and then moves towards managing repetitive tasks according to their preferences. In the broader strategic context, Abu Dhabi has announced an ambition to move towards an AI-based government by 2027; a future ambition that should be clearly distinguished from the results currently achieved.[3]

03 True Test

Are the abilities built?

Capabilities built for the future

This shows the importance of testing. Future-to-Capability Fit In the Tamayuz 10X model, the status of TM is read not only from its interface, but from the capabilities that the operation of this model needs and depends on.

System Integration

Compiling more than 1,100 services and dozens of partners means building the capacity to coordinate a journey beyond one's borders, not just aggregating links.

Artificial Intelligence Executive

The assistant is not limited to answering questions; published data describes it as a channel capable of managing requests and services, moving artificial intelligence from knowledge to implementation.[2][4]

Automation and Proactive

AutoGov represents a transition from "Make the service easier on demand" to "Let the system manage certain recurring needs according to user preferences".

Governance and Trust

As the system becomes more capable of working on the user’s behalf, consent, preference management, privacy, security, and rollback capability become operational components rather than side-by-side additions.

The 10X MoveThe decisive shift was not to place more services within one application; it was to change the design unit from a “transaction within one” to a “customer journey,” and then build capacity to make the government better understood, implemented, and preempted.

Impact Guide: What Do We Really Know?

The strongest evidence currently available proves Scope, use and operationMore than 1,100 services, 3.6 million users, more than 700,000 conversations with the smart assistant, and more than 1.4 million requests managed by the assistant independently in the data published related to the project.[1][2] Abu Dhabi Customs also announced that customer satisfaction with its digital services through Tamm reached 97.1% in 2025, a useful sectoral indicator of the quality of the experience, but it does not prove that Tamm alone caused the result.[8]

Directory Limits: Scope indicators should not be turned into a claim that every service has become proactive, nor the ratio of every improvement in a government entity to the platform alone. The trend towards a fully AI-based government by 2027 is a strategic ambition and not a complete result to date. The strength of the evidence is high in proving expansion and operation, and medium in proving the overall causal effect on efficiency and public value.

What does the literature add to reading the case?

Academic studies in Abu Dhabi and the UAE prevent the reduction of success in the number of services or the level of technology. A study of 845 participants in Abu Dhabi highlighted the perceived utility, ease of use, trust, enablers and awareness within the determinants of e-government adoption.[5] Journal of Internet Commerce Confidence and the trend towards the use of technology within the factors of user satisfaction in the UAE.[6] In a more recent study on smart government applications in the UAE, ease of use, quality of information and interaction emerged within the factors of appreciation and confidence.[7]

This literature makes the 10X test more rigorous: it’s not enough for government to be more technologically intelligent; the new capability must be turned into tangible value while maintaining trust and ease of use.

Tamayuz 10X Lens

Looking ahead and crystallizing ambition

There is a clear picture of a government path towards more intelligence and proactive and reducing the burden on the customer, with a declared ambition for the coming years.

Redefining Value and Strategic Direction

Value shifts from “digital access” to “reducing government effort in human life.”

Capacity Building and Empowerment

Achieving the target future imposes new capabilities in integration, data, artificial intelligence, automation and governance.

Reinventing the organization and its business model

The trans-boundary journey becomes a design unit, requiring an operating model that is not constrained by traditional organizational boundaries.

Innovation and experimentation to accelerate transformation

The path from a unified platform to an executive assistant and then AutoGov reflects an accumulation and gradual expansion of capabilities.

Maximizing institutional and technical intelligence

Artificial intelligence moves from an information channel to an understanding, implementation and automation layer.

What can the leader and organization use in practice?

Start from the future, not from the list of problems.

First determine what form of experiment or business model should become normal in five or ten years, and then ask what capabilities should be built now.

Turn Vision into Capability Map

Connect each future ambition with specific capabilities: data, skills, governance, technology, partnerships, processes and operating model.

Don't make artificial intelligence a layer above complexity.

If processes and data remain fragmented, the intelligent assistant becomes a modern interface to an old system. Redesign precedes cosmetology.

Reduce the burden, not just the number of services.

The strongest indicators for the future are time, effort and steps that have disappeared, the proportion of trips that have become proactive, confidence, satisfaction and results.

Portability

The idea is highly transferable, but the interface is not what should be copied. Arab institutions and governments that want a similar model need first a reliable identity, data integration, clear ownership of cross-unit flights, governance of consent and privacy, and the ability to redesign policies and processes. Without these foundations a unified application can be produced, but a unified government is difficult to experiment with.
