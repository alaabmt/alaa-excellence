From Digital Government to Anticipatory Government: How Abu Dhabi Is Building 2027 Capabilities Today

Abu Dhabi did not begin with the question, “Which artificial-intelligence applications can we add today?” It defined a target state in which AI is an integral part of government operations by 2027, then worked backward to build the infrastructure, skills, governance, leadership and ways of working that future requires.

Listen to this case

Executive Snapshot

In January 2025, the Abu Dhabi Government announced its Digital Strategy 2025–2027, backed by AED 13 billion in investment and aimed at moving toward an “AI-native” government: one in which artificial intelligence is built into the design of work and services rather than added after a process has already been designed. The plan includes 100% adoption of sovereign cloud for government operations, full digitisation and automation of processes, a unified enterprise resource planning platform, and the development of more than 200 AI solutions.[1]

By September 2025, more than 100 AI use cases were operating across more than 40 government entities. More than 95% of a workforce exceeding 30,000 government employees had completed comprehensive AI training, and a Chief Data and AI Officer role had been established in every government entity.[2] These are important interim results, but they do not mean the 2027 goal has already been achieved.

The Challenge: Digitisation Is Not Enough If the Operating Model Stays the Same

A government can move forms and transactions onto screens while still operating according to the traditional service model: the customer knows the responsible entity, identifies the service, initiates the request, tracks the transaction, and returns when the next need arises. In this model, digitisation improves the channel without redefining the relationship between government and the customer.

Abu Dhabi’s ambition raises the question to a different level: what if government can understand context, connect data and services, and handle some needs more proactively? The challenge then is no longer simply to build a better application, but to build a different government from the inside.

Working from the Future Back to the Present

The foresight value in this case is not the use of the word “future.” The starting point is a defined picture of what government should become by 2027, followed by translating that picture into capabilities that must exist before the destination can be reached. This reflects the Tamayuz 10X logic: the future is not something to wait for; it is a design hypothesis for the present.

01 · Target future state

Government with AI built into its core

Intelligence is not a separate project; it is part of decisions, services and operations.

02 · Work backward

What capabilities does that future require?

Sovereign cloud, data, governance, leadership, skills, integration and automation.

03 · Test in the present

Are those capabilities actually beginning to appear?

Operational use cases, broad workforce training, new leadership roles, and more anticipatory platforms and services.

The decisive shiftInstead of starting with technology available today and then searching for a use for it, the institution starts with the way it wants to operate in the future and builds, from the present, the capabilities that can make that future possible.

The Capabilities Being Built

Digital infrastructure and data

Sovereign cloud, a unified government data centre and data integration are not merely back-office infrastructure; they are operating conditions for a government that wants to use AI across entities.

Government-wide skills

Training more than 95% of the government workforce moves AI from a specialist capability confined to technical teams toward a broader operational competence.[2]

Leadership and governance

Establishing a data and AI leadership role in every government entity connects transformation to clear institutional accountability instead of leaving it as a collection of separate technology initiatives.[2]

Service redesign

TAMM’s evolution toward conversational services and AutoGov reflects a gradual move from making requests easier to managing selected recurring needs more proactively.[3]

Evidence of Impact: What Has Been Achieved, and What Remains an Ambition?

Department of Government Enablement data published for 2025 reports 3.8 million TAMM users, more than 1,150 services, and 1.9 million service cases handled through conversational AI, with user satisfaction at 92.5%.[3] In a more specific sector example, Abu Dhabi Customs reported 9.4% growth in digital customs transactions in 2025 compared with 2024, while customer satisfaction with its TAMM services rose to 97.1% from 94.7%.[4]

Evidence limits: most published performance indicators come from Abu Dhabi government entities themselves. An independent analysis of TAMM noted that some figures do not have published independent audits; that the “visits avoided” indicator is an estimate based on a counterfactual model rather than a direct count; and that definitions differ across some usage measures.[5] These figures are therefore not used here to claim that AI alone caused every improvement in service performance.

Results must also be separated from targets. The strategy’s expected contribution of approximately AED 24 billion to GDP and the creation of 5,000 jobs are strategic forecasts, not realised outcomes. Likewise, Abu Dhabi’s stated aim to become an AI-native government by 2027 does not mean it has already reached that state.

The Case Through the 10X Octad

1 · Foresight and Ambition

A clear time-bound picture for 2027 turns the future into a standard that directs what needs to be built today.

2 · Redefining Value and Strategic Direction

Value moves from providing a digital service toward a more integrated and anticipatory government that relies less on customers initiating every transaction.

3 · Leadership and Future Governance

Data and AI roles at the level of every entity make governance part of the transformation architecture.

4 · Capability Multiplication and Empowerment

Broad training and adoption programmes make transformation a human-capability issue, not simply a technology purchase.

5 · Reinventing the Institution and Its Operating Model

Anticipatory and cross-entity services redistribute responsibility between the customer and the government system.

7 · Maximising Institutional and Technological Intelligence

Data, AI and integration become shared operating capabilities.

8 · Maximising Sustainable Value and Results

The standard is not the number of AI technologies deployed, but their evidenced contribution to speed, quality, satisfaction and government capability.

What Can Another Institution Transfer from This Case?

Start with the future operating model

Define how the institution should operate three to five years from now before selecting a list of technologies.

Turn the future state into a capability map

Ask what data, skills, governance, leadership, infrastructure and ways of working must exist to make that future possible.

Build interim measures

Do not wait for the final target. Measure progress in capability building, scope of use, service quality and operational outcomes.

Separate ambition from evidence

A future target guides decisions, but it becomes an achievement only when supported by realised results that can be verified.

Transferability

The logic applies beyond government and artificial intelligence. A hospital that imagines more anticipatory care needs connected data, different workflows and new skills today. A factory that imagines autonomous operations needs sensors, data, reliability and governance before it buys an intelligent platform. An institution that defines its future first can distinguish between a capability that must be built and a technology showcase that can wait.

10X QuestionIf, within three years, artificial intelligence becomes a normal part of every decision and service in your institution, what capability must you begin building today so that you do not arrive in that future with tools, people and ways of working designed for the past?
