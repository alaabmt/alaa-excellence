10X Reflections from the Qur’an | Surat Ibrahim

Ibrahim: When the Future Begins Before Everything Is in Place

Do we set our ambition by what we have today, or build what tomorrow will need?

العربية

Methodological note: This contemporary reflection draws on the supplication of Ibrahim (peace be upon him) in Surat Ibrahim 14:35–41, in light of classical tafsir. It neither offers a new tafsir nor attributes modern management concepts to the Qur’an.

“Our Lord, I have settled some of my descendants in an uncultivated valley near Your sacred House.”

Ibrahim 14:37 — Saheeh International

An uncultivated valley. Had you been there, what would you have seen? Land with almost nothing to sustain life, and a future whose means were not yet visible. Yet Ibrahim does not stop at describing what the place lacks:

“Our Lord, that they may establish prayer. So make hearts among the people incline toward them and provide for them from the fruits that they might be grateful.”

Ibrahim 14:37 — Saheeh International

Before turning to the future and to building capability, pause on the order of the supplication. It does not begin with provision. It begins with “that they may establish prayer.” Provision comes next, and even that is not the end point: “that they might be grateful.”

If the Verse Spoke to Your Life Today

You may pray for your children's success, for wider provision, for progress at work, and for your ambitions to be fulfilled. Where do prayer and gratitude sit on that list? And what would have to happen for you to say, “Now I have succeeded”?

How many blessings were once a prayer, then became ordinary after they arrived? Perhaps the question is not only “What do I want from Allah?” but also “Who will I become if Allah gives me what I want?”

“Our Lord, indeed You know what we conceal and what we declare.”

Ibrahim 14:38 — Saheeh International

Here the question turns inward. Why do you want what you want? What is your ambition building in you? Does it make you more grateful and closer to Allah, or more attached to how others see you?

What Is the “Uncultivated Valley” in Your Life?

It might be a project that lacks what it needs, a child whose future worries you, a capability you do not have, or a stage of life whose path you cannot fully see. What do you fear because you cannot yet see the means? What supplication do you need? And what step can you begin taking now?

Reliance on Allah does not mean waiting for every condition to be met. Elsewhere in the Qur’an, Ibrahim and Ismail are shown working while they pray:

“And [mention] when Abraham was raising the foundations of the House and [with him] Ishmael, [saying], ‘Our Lord, accept [this] from us.’”

Al-Baqarah 2:127 — Saheeh International

Prayer and work. Reliance on Allah and taking the means. Start with what you can do, seek Allah's help with what you cannot, and do not let the means themselves become the source of your certainty.

Do Not Let the Present Cap the Future

An organization might say: we cannot do this now because we lack the data, the technology, the expertise, or the people. That may well be true. But the word now changes the whole question. Does a lack of capability today mean the future is out of reach, or does it point to a capability we need to build?

There is a difference between “What can we do with what we have?” and “What must we build so we can do what we aspire to?”

Here Comes the 10X Question

The verse does not present a management model, and the “uncultivated valley” is not a term for a capability gap. Reflection does, however, lead naturally to a contemporary question: Are we letting what we have today decide the future we dare to imagine?

In Tamayuz 10X, foresight and a clear ambition are followed by a question about the capabilities that ambition requires. If the future will be different, we may need knowledge, technology, talent, partnerships, or a way of working that we have never had before.

Do not let what you lack today define what you can become tomorrow.

Start from the Future, Then Return to Today

Choose one ambition and ask: if it came true, what would I, or my organization, need to be able to do that we cannot do today? Then ask: What is the first capability we can start building now?

That is how the future stops being a wish and becomes something under construction.

Then Return to the Valley

The question is no longer only how to build the future. It becomes: Which future do I want? Why do I want it? And who do I want to become on the way there?

As you build what you hope to build in this world, a deeper question remains: What does Allah want to build in you as you walk toward what you want to build?
