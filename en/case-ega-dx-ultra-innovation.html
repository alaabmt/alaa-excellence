Application Case Innovation and Institutional Capabilities

From a factory in the UAE to a technology operating in Bahrain: When will innovation become an exportable capability?

It is easy for an organization to describe an internal project as an innovation. It is harder for this innovation to prove itself in operation, then chosen by another organization and build on it part of its production capacity. This is the story of a smelter technology developed in the UAE, and then out of its original environment to operate on an industrial scale in Bahrain.

The central ideaOperational knowledge can become an exportable strategic asset

The problem isn't just the metal.

Aluminium smelting is an energy-intensive industry. The quality of smelting technology is therefore measured not only by its ability to produce metal, but also by the amount of metal produced by the cell, the energy it needs, the operating stability, and the space and capital required for the plant.

In simple terms: Imagine a “melting cell” as a large industrial production unit that uses electricity to separate aluminum from its raw material. The technical race is for this unit to produce more aluminum, less energy and cost, and with stable performance.

EGA has developed its own aluminium smelting technologies in the UAE over decades. According to its published technical record, this development, with previous work starting in 1980, has reduced the electricity required to produce every ton of aluminium by 37.5%, while its latest industrial technology, DX Ultra, has more than doubled the productivity of the company's first indigenous technology since 1990.[1]

The Decisive ShiftThe leap was not just for EGA to use its technology inside its factories; it was for the accumulated knowledge to be turned into a technology that another factory could choose, test, and operate outside the UAE.

From in-house expertise to transferable technology

In 2016, Aluminium Bahrain selected DX Ultra for its sixth production line expansion project after an international competition. This marked the first time that an Emirati industrial company licensed its core technology to the production process internationally. EGA sent a team of 40 engineers and technicians to Bahrain, and also hosted Alba employees for training at its UAE sites.[2][3]

This detail is important: technology transfer is not the sale of an engineering scheme. Industrial knowledge contains implicit operational experiencehow the process adjusts, how it responds to deviations, and how it maintains performance. Human development and knowledge transfer therefore becomes part of the technical product itself.

What is 465 kA? The symbol kA stands for "kiloampere", which is a measure of the intensity of the current used in smelting cells. The original design level was 460 kA, while the operation proved the line's ability to operate at 465 kA while maintaining strong performance indicators, i.e. at a load higher than the original design level.[2]

The Alba Bulletin itself confirms that selected cells from the sixth production line passed final performance tests with results that exceeded the guarantees in the technology transfer agreement, and that the line was operating at 465 kA with 95% current efficiency and a power consumption of 12.87 kWh per kilogram of aluminium.[4] A technical paper published at the ICSOBA conference also documents the successful performance test at 465 kA in October and November 2019, and the continuation of the power surge in subsequent operation.[5]

What does "current efficiency" and kWh/kg mean? Stream efficiency tells us, in simplified form, how much of the current used actually converts into useful production. kWh/kg measures the amount of electricity needed to produce one kilogram of aluminium. In an energy-intensive industry, low electricity consumption per unit of production while maintaining a stable operation has a direct economic and environmental impact.

EGA states that it has licensed its technologies for more than half a million tonnes of smelting capacity installed outside the UAE.[1] In 2024, Alba and EGA signed a new technical services agreement for Line 6 covering support, monitoring, consulting, training and operational auditing; further evidence that the technical relationship continued beyond the initial installation phase.[6]

Cash Note: EGA’s 37.5% reduction in electricity consumption is a published figure of its technology development over decades, not an independent result from the Alba experience alone. So don’t confuse it with the results of the DX Ultra test in Bahrain. The strength of the case comes from the combination of three types of evidence: a long technical development record, specific operating results in Alba, and published technical documentation on testing and operation.

What does the case reveal about Tamayuz 10X?

06

Innovation and experimentation to accelerate transformation

DXYU Ultra started with experimental cells in Jebel Ali before moving to a large industrial application. The idea is not to jump from laboratory to expansion, but to build a series of testing, learning and proof before expansion.

04

Capacity Building and Empowerment

Technology has not moved alone; knowledge of engineers, training and operational support have moved with it. Human capacity here is not a supporting activity, but a condition for successful innovation transfer.

05

Reinventing the organization and its business model

When an in-house expertise goes from a factory improvement tool to an externally licensed technology, the nature of value changes: the enterprise not only sells its product, but begins to turn its knowledge into an asset that can be transferred and marketed.

08

Doubling Value and Sustainable Impact

The impact here is readable through productivity, energy efficiency and the technical feasibility of working outside its original environment. This is stronger than counting patents or initiatives alone.

What can you use?

Commander.

Ask about each successful internal innovation: is success tied to specific people, or has it become documented, teachable, and replicable knowledge?

The Foundation

Look for practices that produce repeatable results, then turn them into “knowledge assets”: clear standard, performance data, mode of operation, training, and a testing mechanism in a different environment. Portability is a stronger test than the success of a single experiment.

What can be moved... and what shouldn't be copied?

It can be moved.

Capacity building approach: Accumulation of knowledge, disciplined experimentation, performance measurement, documentation of knowledge, training of others, and then testing the solution outside of its original environment before claiming scalability.

Depends on the context.

The technical figures of the smelting technology itself do not serve as a standard for institutions outside the aluminium industry. Capital investment and the engineering and regulatory environment are also specific to the sector. What is conveyed is the logic of building innovation, not its industrial specifications.

Deeper Lesson

Many organizations say they manage knowledge’, but measure it by the number of documents or courses. EGA’s case suggests a more difficult criterion: Can accumulated knowledge produce better performance, and then turn into a way for others to use and achieve results?

At this point knowledge is no longer an archive, nor is innovation a seasonal event. Both become institutional capacity.

A question to think about.What knowledge has your organization developed over years of work, but is still locked into the expertise of individuals or internal processes and could have been converted into a documented, replicable, transferable, and possibly new source of value?
