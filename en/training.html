Capability building in Tamayuz 10X

Building organizational capability for the future

Tamayuz 10X starts from a question about the future: what must an organization become able to achieve that is not possible today? The answer begins with understanding the resources and potential available, then turning them into real organizational capability, and then developing a renewing capability that can adapt, reshape itself and create lasting impact.

What must the organization be able to do in the future that it cannot yet do at the required level today?

Core concepts

What an organization has is not the same as what it can achieve

Capability building becomes more precise when we distinguish individual competency, available organizational capacity and actual organizational capability. The distinction stops capability building from being reduced to training or to adding resources.

Competency

Individual competency

Knowledge, skills and behaviours that enable an individual to perform a role or task. Individual competencies matter, but on their own they are not enough to create organizational capability.

Capacity

Available organizational capacity

The amount of resources, energy and potential an organization has or can mobilize: people, time, funding, data, systems, technology, infrastructure, knowledge and partnerships.

Capability

Actual organizational capability

What an organization can actually achieve, repeatedly and reliably, by organizing, integrating and coordinating its resources in a joined-up way of working that delivers a defined result.

In short: Available capacity defines what an organization has to work with; actual capability defines what it can achieve with it.
Capacity and capability

Capacity provides the potential; capability turns it into performance

High capacity does not guarantee high capability. An organization may have substantial resources and still miss the result it needs if those resources are not connected or do not work within a reliable system.

Example: a health organization with strong resources

High available capacity

Skilled doctors and nurses, a digital platform, advanced equipment, data, a budget and suitable facilities. All of these raise available capacity.

But actual capability may stay limited

If data, teams, processes and decision rights do not work within one pathway that detects a patient’s deterioration and responds early, the available capacity will not become actual organizational capability.

Dynamic capability

Renewing capability: when an organization can rebuild itself

Actual capability answers the question of what an organization can achieve today. Renewing capability is about its ability to sense change, seize opportunities, and reshape resources, processes and capabilities as the future changes.

Sense

Sensing change

Reading shifts, opportunities, risks and new needs before they turn into operational pressure.

Seize

Seizing opportunities

Making clear decisions and directing resources toward opportunities that can create more value.

Transform

Reshaping

Redesigning processes, resources, operating models and capabilities so the organization is fit for the future, not only the present.

The Tamayuz 10X logic

From anticipating the future to building capability and proving impact

A 10X shift does not mean multiplying resources tenfold. It can mean recombining existing capacity to build new capabilities that change what the organization can achieve. This logic draws on the spirit of Dubai 10X: thinking from the vantage point of the future, and moving from incremental improvement to transformation.

01

Anticipate

Identify the shifts that will redefine what customers, society and the sector expect.

02

Define

Define the critical capabilities the organization must have in the future.

03

Diagnose

Compare today’s actual capability with the capability the future will require.

04

Build

Reshape available capacity across people, operations, data, technology and governance.

05

Activate

Turn capability from a design on paper into a repeated practice in daily work.

06

Prove and renew

Measure performance and impact, then use what you learn to keep reshaping the capability.

The organizational capability gap

is the difference between what an organization can actually achieve today and what it must be able to achieve in the future. Diagnosing this gap comes before any decision to train, invest or redesign processes.

The 10X capability-building architecture

Capability is not built from a single element

Available capacity becomes actual capability when the parts of an organization work as one system. The gap can appear in any part of that system, not only in people’s skills.

Capability maturity

From available resources to 10X renewing capability

Each future capability can be assessed by its level of maturity, not only by the resources allocated to it.

1

Available resources

The elements of capacity exist, but they are used unevenly and depend on individuals.

2

Emerging practice

Repeatable efforts appear, but they are not yet consistent.

3

Organizational capability

Clear roles, processes and standards that produce reliable performance.

4

Intelligent capability

Uses data, technology and continuous learning to improve performance.

5

10X renewing capability

Senses shifts and reshapes its resources and operating model before change is forced on it.

Measurement

Knowing that learning happened is not enough

Capability building needs a chain of measurement that links learning to application, then application to organizational capability, performance and impact. Good measurement separates change in the individual from change in the organization.

KnowledgeWhat is now known?
SkillWhat can the individual now do?
BehaviorHas learning moved into practice?
CapabilityHas the practice become embedded and reliable?
PerformanceHave quality, speed and reliability improved?
ImpactHas lasting value emerged for the organization and its stakeholders?
Model library

Evidence-based tools for building people, learning and the transfer of learning

These models are not the Tamayuz 10X model itself. They serve specific parts of capability building, especially when the capability gap concerns learning, skills, behavior and the transfer of practice into the workplace.

K

The Kirkpatrick model

From reaction to learning, behavior and results.

＋

Four levels of evaluation

The value of learning does not end with participant satisfaction.

Results
Behavior
Learning
Reaction

Philosophy

The model separates the quality of the learning experience, what was learned, what carried over into behavior at work, and what results followed. The levels are not a guaranteed causal chain; results are shaped by many organizational factors.

Use and measurement

Reaction is measured by how relevant and useful the experience was; learning by tests, simulations or applied tasks; behavior by observation and usage indicators; and results by indicators of performance, quality, time and value.

StrengthShifts the discussion from training hours to the effect on practice and outcomes.
Key limitationNo change in results should be attributed to training alone without considering the other factors.
Within 10X, Kirkpatrick helps test whether learning has moved from the individual into behavior, but on its own it does not measure full organizational capability.
B

Revised Bloom’s Taxonomy

From remembering and understanding to analyzing, evaluating and creating.

＋

Depth of learning

The level of thinking chosen should match what the work requires.

Create
Evaluate
Analyze
Apply
Understand
Remember

Philosophy

Not all learning objectives sit at the same level. Knowing a policy is different from analyzing a problem or designing a new solution. The taxonomy helps set the cognitive depth required and match the assessment to it.

Use and measurement

Remembering can be measured with a short test, applying with a practical task, analyzing with a case study, evaluating with a justified decision, and creating with a new project or design.

StrengthMakes objectives more precise and stops simple tests being used for tasks that need higher-order thinking.
Key limitationFocuses on the cognitive domain; on its own it does not cover behavior, the organizational environment or the transfer of learning to work.
Within 10X, Bloom is useful when the capability gap concerns the depth of thinking required: from understanding the basics to analysis, redesign and innovation.
KOLB

Kolb’s experiential learning cycle

Experience, reflection, conceptualization, then experimentation.

＋

The cycle of learning from experience

Experience alone is not enough; learning forms when experience is understood and tested again.

Concrete experience
Reflective observation
Abstract conceptualization
Active experimentation

Philosophy

Learning develops through continuous movement between experience, reflection, making sense and experimenting. The cycle turns daily practice into a source of more mature capability.

Use

Simulations, projects, after-action reviews, quick experiments and learning from real cases become more valuable when followed by a moment of reflection, drawing out lessons and applying them again.

Within 10X, Kolb supports a culture of experimentation and fast learning, which is essential when an organization needs to build new capability in a changing environment.
A

Knowles’ andragogy

Principles that help design adult learning in a professional context.

＋

Assumptions about adult learning

Learning is worth more when it connects to experience, a real problem and an actual need.

The need to know why
Drawing on previous experience
Role-related readiness
Problem-Centered Learning
Motivation and autonomy

Philosophy

Adults learn more effectively when the need is clear, their experience is put to use, learning is tied to a real problem or professional role, and they play an active part in the process.

Limits

These are guiding principles and assumptions rather than fixed laws; learners’ needs and professional and cultural contexts vary.

Within 10X, Knowles helps bring learning closer to real capability challenges instead of leaving it as an activity separate from work.
B&F

The Baldwin and Ford framework

Why does learning sometimes work in the classroom and fail in the workplace?

＋

Transfer of learning

Whether learning transfers depends on the learner, the design and the work environment together.

Learner characteristics
Learning design
Work environment and support
Retention and generalization in performance

Philosophy

Learning does not move into work automatically. Chances to apply it, manager support, feedback, job design and learner characteristics all affect whether a new skill lasts and spreads.

Use

The framework helps diagnose why learning has not turned into behavior: does the problem lie in the learner’s readiness, the design of the experience, or a work environment that does not allow it to be applied?

Within 10X, this framework is the bridge between building individual competency and turning it into a practice that can contribute to actual organizational capability.
