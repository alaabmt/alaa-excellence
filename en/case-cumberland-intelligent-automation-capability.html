When Automation Is Not Always the Answer: How Cumberland Built an Institutional Capability to Redesign Work

The deeper question is not how many tasks a software robot can execute. It is whether the organization knows when to automate, when to redesign a service, and what must remain a human decision.

Executive Snapshot

Cumberland Council was created in April 2023 by bringing together four legacy local-government organizations and their different systems and processes. Its official plan targets more integrated systems, earlier insight into resident needs, and less administrative burden on staff. [2][3]

The Challenge

When systems do not integrate, staff become the manual bridge between applications. Cumberland's plan describes a move from four legacy system estates toward a more integrated organization with better data visibility and earlier decisions. [2]

The 10X Move

RPA — Robotic Process Automation is software that performs repetitive digital steps previously completed manually. Cumberland's shift was not simply to buy RPA: automation works alongside service design, business analysis and user research. Work starts with discovery; if automation is not the right answer, the issue goes to service design or digital delivery rather than automating a poor process. [1]

Evidence of Impact — and Its Limits

Invoice automation expanded from removing manual re-keying to document management, audit trails and retention requirements, reaching annual workload equivalent to roughly 12 FTE. For referrals from organizations such as health services, police and ambulance services, administrative work that had taken 20–30 minutes was reduced to a short human review after automation handled the repetitive portion. [1]

These results do not mean 12 jobs were eliminated, nor that every released hour became cash savings. The 2,000-hour figure is a council estimate republished by the LGA. The public sources used here do not publish an independent method for recalculating it or audited financial returns for each process.

10X Lens

Leadership & Future Governance

The official plan links projects to a Design Authority, a governance body intended to align solutions with enterprise architecture and priorities. [2]

Capability & Empowerment

The initial team was not built only from software developers; people from project, training and analytical backgrounds developed technical capability through structured learning and practice. [1]

Reinventing the Organization

The question becomes: should the step be removed, redesigned, integrated, or automated?

Institutional & Technical Intelligence

The council targets a more integrated data view while automation moves toward reusable components and integration patterns. [2][4]

From the Future to Impact

Desired future ← one council that sees resident needs earlier and directs staff time toward work requiring human judgment.

Required capabilities ← service design, process understanding, integrated data, system integration, automation, enterprise architecture, skills and governance.

What must be built now ← multidisciplinary teams, discovery before development, prioritization criteria, reusable components, training and measurement of value and quality.

Different way of working ← start from the problem and outcome rather than the tool, retaining human judgment where needed.

Results and impact ← administrative time released, higher processing capacity and more consistent work, while final financial impact still needs stronger measurement.

Practical Use

Before approving automation, map who sees information, who interprets it, who decides, and where data is re-keyed. Separate rules-based work from judgment-based work. Measure released time, exceptions, data quality, service time, errors and what actually happened to the capacity released.

Transferability

The principle transfers well to organizations with multiple legacy systems, repetitive manual work and high transaction volumes. The most transferable asset is not Blue Prism; it is the selection system: discover the problem, involve frontline experts, start low-risk, keep humans in the right decisions, and measure value before scaling.

The Future-Focused 10X Question

What future do we want our organization to operate in five to ten years from now: what will an employee see at the moment of decision, which tasks will disappear because they never needed a person, and which decisions will remain human? If that is the desired future, what integrated data, skills, service design, systems, governance and value measures must we build today so automation does not become another layer on top of an old way of working?
