Agentic AI 10X

From AI that answers… to AI that helps get work done.

Agentic AI is an approach in which AI pursues a goal across connected steps, uses authorized tools and systems, reviews what happens during execution, and operates within explicit delegation and human-oversight boundaries.

Four different levels of assistance

Not every product using a language model is an agent. The practical distinction is how much of the workflow the system can manage, which tools it can use, and what authority it has.

PatternWhat it doesExampleWho drives the workflow?
ChatbotAnswers a direct question or request.“Summarize this report.”Human.
CopilotSuggests and analyzes while the person remains in charge of the steps.“Analyze the report and suggest three options.”Human with AI support.
AI AgentPursues a goal and selects steps and tools within defined permissions.“Collect the data, validate it, prepare the report and escalate exceptions.”System within delegated authority.
Multi-Agent SystemCoordinates specialized agents and combines their outputs.Research + analysis + verification + execution under one coordinator.Automated orchestration with human control points.

How does Agentic AI work?

At minimum, an agent needs a model that interprets and reasons, tools that can read from or act on systems, and instructions and operating boundaries that define the job and permissions. It then operates in a loop until the goal, an exit condition, or an escalation point is reached.

Goal→Context & data→Plan→Tools→Act→Check outcome→Correct or escalate

Model

Interprets context and selects the next action under the task instructions.

Tools

Enable retrieval, reading, updating, messaging or actions in approved systems.

Memory and context

Maintain the task state and relevant information so each step does not restart from zero.

Boundaries and oversight

Define what the system can see, decide and do, and when control must return to a person.

Do you need an agent at all?

Is the task stable, rule-based and predictable?

For example, moving data between systems under fixed rules.

Start with conventional automation.
Is the main need content generation, summarization or analysis?

With no requirement to manage a workflow or take actions outside the conversation.

Generative AI may be enough.
Is the work multi-step, exception-heavy and dependent on unstructured information or multiple tools?

Can you define permissions and measure the outcome?

An agent becomes a credible candidate.
Is the decision high-impact or hard to reverse?

For example, significant financial, clinical or legal actions.

Design human approval into the workflow from the start.

Agentic AI × Tamayuz 10X

Do not start with “Where can we put an agent?” Start with the future in which the organization needs to be able to operate.

Desired future → outcome that must change → decisions and work required → what remains human → where AI assists → what can be delegated → authority boundaries → impact measurement

Autonomy is not the goal. The goal is organizational capability: a better, faster or more consistent outcome, while retaining human authority wherever the risk requires it.

1. Describe the future

How should the service or function work in several years if today's constraint disappears?

2. Backcast to capabilities

Which data, tools, skills, decisions and integrations does that future require?

3. Redesign the work

Do not place AI on top of an old process; redistribute work across people, automation and agents.

4. Measure the outcome

Time, quality, cost, risk and new capability matter more than model usage counts.

Five boundaries of delegation

Every increase in an agent's ability to act requires matching clarity about authority and accountability. Technical guardrails are not enough; identity, permissions, logging, evaluation and human escalation also matter.

Data

What can the system see? Which data is restricted or sensitive?

Decision

What may it recommend or approve? What requires authorization?

Action

Is it read-only, or may it write, edit, send or delete?

Money

Can it initiate a purchase or commit funds? What is the financial limit?

Escalation

When must it stop, who takes over, and what context follows the handoff?

Maturity model: from asking to redesigning work

Ask

AI answers or creates content; the person manages every step.

Assist

AI analyzes and suggests inside the workflow; the person executes.

Execute

The agent performs defined steps through authorized tools.

Orchestrate

The system coordinates tasks, tools or multiple agents with control points.

Transform

The operating model itself is redesigned around the best allocation of capability between people and AI.

Organizational Agentic AI Evidence Review

Purpose and evidence basis

This 18-question review examines six domains before an organization expands an AI agent's operational authority. It identifies evidence gaps and action priorities rather than producing a numerical readiness score.

The review was developed by Tamayuz 10X with reference to the NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF) and ISO/IEC 42001:2023 — Artificial Intelligence Management System. Official NIST AI RMF · Official ISO/IEC 42001.

Methodological limitation: this is a Tamayuz 10X evidence review, not a questionnaire issued by NIST or ISO. It is not presented as a validated measurement scale or a certification assessment. Its output is an evidence-gap review, not a scientific readiness score.

How to answer: choose the strongest evidence the organization can demonstrate now.

Value and use case

1. The organization has clearly defined the operational outcome it expects Agentic AI to improve and how that improvement will be measured.
2. The organization has documented a specific Agentic AI use case, including the task, intended beneficiaries, expected outcomes, and consequences if it does not perform as required.
3. Has the organization confirmed that this use case genuinely requires Agentic AI after comparing it with simpler alternatives such as conventional automation or AI assistance without execution authority?

Governance and accountability

4. Has the organization formally named a person or function accountable for this Agentic AI use case, including performance, risk management, and decisions when problems occur?
5. Does the organization have approved policies governing Agentic AI use, including data protection, security, privacy, accountability, and risk requirements?
6. Has the organization defined what happens when an Agentic AI error or incident occurs, including recording, notification, accountability, and escalation?

Data and systems

7. Has the organization identified the data Agentic AI needs, its sources, quality and suitability, and any restrictions on its use?
8. Has the organization defined which data and systems Agentic AI may access and implemented controls that prevent unnecessary access?
9. Has the organization identified the systems and tools Agentic AI will use and what it may do in each, such as read, enter, modify, or execute actions?

Delegation and human oversight

10. Has the organization clearly defined what Agentic AI may execute autonomously, what it may only recommend, and what requires approval by an authorized person?
11. Has the organization identified high-impact or hard-to-reverse actions and placed explicit controls or approval requirements on them?
12. Has the organization defined when Agentic AI must stop and hand the task to a qualified person, such as when information is insufficient, an exception occurs, or risk exceeds an allowed level?

Testing and evaluation

13. Has the organization tested Agentic AI before operational use in representative normal, failure, and exceptional cases?
14. Has the organization tested whether Agentic AI operates safely and reliably, including security, privacy, output quality, and the safety of actions it may execute?
15. Has the organization defined in advance the results or acceptance conditions Agentic AI must meet before broader deployment or greater execution authority is allowed?

Monitoring and continual improvement

16. Does the organization monitor Agentic AI after deployment using indicators or data that show whether it achieves the required outcomes and whether risks remain within acceptable limits?
17. Can the organization detect errors, incidents, or performance deterioration after deployment and stop Agentic AI or reduce its authority when limits are exceeded?
18. Does the organization periodically review Agentic AI results, incidents, and lessons learned and use them to improve the system, controls, workflow, and scaling decisions?

Answers are processed in the browser only and are not submitted by this tool.

Where should an organization start?

1. Choose an outcome

Cycle time, quality, cost, staff burden, response time or a capability that is not possible today.

2. Choose a workflow

Complex enough to benefit from reasoning, but bounded enough to keep the first experiment manageable.

3. Define authority

What can the agent read, recommend, execute, and when must it stop?

4. Test before scaling

Establish a baseline, test success, failure and edge cases, then widen authority gradually.
