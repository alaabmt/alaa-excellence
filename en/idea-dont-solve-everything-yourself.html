Idea of the Day

Don’t solve every problem yourself: leadership that builds a stronger team

Leadership and team empowerment do not mean that the leader has all the answers, but that he builds the ability of others to think, make decisions and take responsibility with confidence.

It’s easy for a leader to get used to the intervention. A problem arises, a team member hesitates to make a decision, the work gets stuck and takes over. This behavior often seems effective; the decision is quicker and the error is less at the same moment.

But there’s a cost that doesn’t come up immediately: Every time a leader takes thinking from the team, the team has less chance of learning how to think. Over time, an organization is formed that waits to be answered from above, even in issues that could have been solved soon.

The leader who solves everything may become more important... but the team becomes less capable.

Effective leadership starts with team empowerment.

Effective leadership is not a retreat from responsibility, it's not letting people face problems without support. It's moving from question to question. What solution will I give? To a question How can I help them find a good solution on their own?

This shows meaning. Empower the teamIndividuals have the information, confidence, and space to participate in the solution and decision-making, rather than just waiting for the leader’s direction in every detail.

Delegation of command is not just a division of tasks.

True delegation doesn’t just mean transferring a task from the leader to a team member. It means transferring a piece of thought and responsibility as well. Sometimes the best leadership intervention is a question: What do we know? What are the options? What are the risks? What do you suggest? These questions build a way of thinking that the team can use later without waiting for the leader.

When someone comes to you with a problem today, try not to start answering. Ask them first to offer two options and their recommendation. You don’t test them; you expand their ability to take responsibility.

A strong team is less dependent on the leader.

An outstanding organization does not rely on a few people who know how to behave, but rather on a system that distributes understanding, decision and ability. Developing a team and enabling employees to make decisions is not a side management issue, but part of building a more flexible and sustainable organization.

An outstanding leader does not measure their success by the number of problems they solve themselves, but by their team’s ability to deal with problems better over time. As the team becomes more independent, leadership shifts from a decision center to an environment that creates other leaders.

Idea of the Day

Don’t make your leadership value in the number of problems you solve yourself. Make it in the number of people who are better able to solve problems because they have worked with you.
