# Tamayuz 10X Executive Summary Standard — English

## Purpose
This standard governs every English Executive Summary or Executive Snapshot published by Tamayuz 10X.

The English version must read as original editorial English, not as a sentence-by-sentence translation of Arabic. Preserve facts, evidence, nuance, limitations and argument, but rebuild structure and wording for an international executive reader.

## Mandatory information order
Unless the case itself requires a better evidence-led order, the default sequence is:

**problem → why it matters → what the organization changed → operating-model change → documented results → evidence limits → 10X significance**

## The first-paragraph rule
The opening paragraph must make the real-world problem visible before introducing 10X theory, technology or abstract management language.

Within the first 20–30 seconds, a reader who has not read the title or full article should be able to answer:
1. What is this case about?
2. What problem was the organization facing?
3. What did it do differently?
4. Why did that matter?

If those answers are not clear, the Executive Summary fails the quality gate.

## Editorial method
Use an inverted-pyramid logic: lead with the most decision-relevant information, then add mechanism, evidence and interpretation.

Do not imitate any publication's distinctive voice. Use high-quality international news and executive-writing principles: clarity, specificity, disciplined attribution, active verbs, restrained claims, and important facts early.

## Original-English requirement
The workflow is:

**understand source meaning → verify evidence → restructure for English → draft in natural English → edit for rhythm and precision**

Never use:

**Arabic sentence → nearest English sentence**

The English version may change paragraph order, sentence structure, transitions, headline syntax and emphasis. It may not change facts, figures, sources, uncertainty, causality or intended analytical conclusion.

## Executive Summary content test
A strong summary normally contains:
- the operational or strategic problem and its consequence;
- the organization and intervention;
- what changed in the operating model, not merely the technology used;
- two or three decision-relevant results, with context;
- one material limitation when necessary to prevent overclaiming;
- the 10X significance stated in one clear management proposition.

## What to avoid
- opening with a technology before explaining the problem;
- opening with 10X philosophy or abstract language;
- generic scene-setting;
- literal translations of Arabic rhetorical structures;
- metric cards interrupting the summary before the narrative is understood;
- repeating the article introduction;
- excessive numbers without explaining what changed;
- implying causality beyond the evidence;
- ending with a generic motivational question when a sharper executive conclusion is available.

## 10X test
The final paragraph should identify the broken or weakened operating assumption and explain what became possible because of the new capability.

The transferable question is generally not “How can we copy this technology?” but “Which operating assumption in our system may no longer be necessary because a capability or constraint has changed?”

## Final quality gate
Before publication, confirm:
- Problem appears at the beginning.
- Intervention is clear.
- Operating-model change is clear.
- Key results are contextualized.
- Evidence limits are preserved.
- 10X significance is explicit.
- The prose reads as original English.
- No sentence exists merely because the Arabic version used the same sentence structure.
- A reader can understand the case from the Executive Summary alone.
